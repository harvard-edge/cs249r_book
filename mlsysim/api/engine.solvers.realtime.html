    engine.solvers.realtime

    engine.solvers.realtime

    Real-time solvers: the timed path between a learned proposer and the drives.

    Domain implementations behind mlsysim.solvers (the public import path, derived from engine.solvers.__init__). The equations live in physics.robotics.control (phase lag of a delay, response-time bound, CAN FD and EtherCAT serialization, the lease ceiling) and physics.robotics.stopping; these models compose them for one stated situation (added 2026-10-20).

    CanFdBusModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.CanFdBusModel()

    Serialization occupancy of a cyclic CAN FD exchange: frames x t_frame against the cycle.

    Frame time and bits come from calc_canfd_bus_utilization (ISO 11898-1:2015 frame format, worst-case stuffing by default). Occupancy is a necessary check only; blocking and retransmission need a response-time analysis. axes_capacity is the most axes whose frames fit one cycle.

    payload_bytes int Application bytes per frame, from zero through 64; padded to a legal CAN FD data-field size. required
    frames_per_axis int Positive number of command/feedback frames per axis per cycle. required
    baud_arbitration Quantity Positive nominal-phase bit rate, convertible to bit/s. required
    baud_data Quantity Positive data-phase bit rate, convertible to bit/s. required

    Returns
    Name Type Description

    See Also

    mlsysim.physics.robotics.control.calc_canfd_bus_utilization : CAN FD frame accounting and serialization.

    Notes

    Equation: t_frame = bits_nominal/baud_arbitration + bits_data/baud_data; occupancy = num_axes*frames_per_axis*t_frame; axes_capacity = floor(cycle/(frames_per_axis*t_frame)).

    Assumptions: Standard identifiers, worst-case dynamic stuffing, and the frame-format accounting of calc_canfd_bus_utilization; all frames have the supplied payload.

    Excludes: Competing traffic, nonpreemptive blocking, retransmissions, and a schedulability guarantee.

    References

    Derivation: serialization time is bit count divided by bit rate, summed across the nominal and data phases. Frame-format assumptions are documented in the linked physics function.

>>> from mlsysim.solvers import CanFdBusModel
>>> result = CanFdBusModel().solve(1, 8, 2, Q_(1, "Mbit/s"),
...     Q_(5, "Mbit/s"), Q_(1, "ms"))
>>> bool(result.fits and result.axes_capacity >= 1)
True

    CommandAuthenticationModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.CommandAuthenticationModel()

    Authentication of a remote command as one more pre-brake delay, and a flood of forgeries.

    A supplied cycle count divided by the permission processor’s clock gives the time of one signature verification and of a MAC over a frame of mac_blocks cipher blocks. Verification adds to the delay before a remote stop can act, so each millisecond is travel at speed against the finished budget’s spare; a replay at the edge of the freshness window carries the machine v x tau_fresh. Verified serially, n forged signed packets queued ahead of a genuine veto finish it at (n + 1) t_verify; the fewest that push it past the deadline is n = floor(T_latest / t_verify), sustained at n / ((n + 1) t_verify) packets per second (added 2026-10-20).

    Notes

    Equation: t_sig = verify_cycles/clock; t_mac = mac_blocks*mac_block_cycles/clock; travel = speed*time; n = floor(deadline/t_sig) forged commands delay the following veto until (n+1)*t_sig.

    Assumptions: Cycle counts are supplied, verification is serial, and each forged signed packet consumes a full verification before rejection.

    Excludes: Cryptographic security strength, early rejection, parallel verification, network admission controls, and a deployed-system timing guarantee.

    References

    Derivation: convert cycles to time at a fixed clock and count serial service slots before the deadline; integrate constant speed over that delay.

    Examples

...     Q_(1, "m/s"), Q_(1, "m"), Q_(10, "ms"), Q_(5, "ms"), Q_(1, "ms"))
>>> bool(result.veto_verified_at > result.deadline)
True

    LeaseBudgetModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.LeaseBudgetModel()

    A chunk lease as a term of the stopping budget, bounded by the lease rule.

    After the last admission a stalled proposer’s chunk drives on for the lease; the tick that notices expiry and the bus cycle that carries the stop follow, then the brake onset. That interval is travel at speed, added to the braking travel, the localization and margin overhead, and the onset travel (StoppingBudgetModel). The lease rule bounds the lease from both sides:

    T_renew + T_jitter  <  T_lease  <=  tau_max - T_tick - T_bus - T_onset - age

    where tau_max is the longest pre-brake delay whose stop fits and age is the evidence’s age when the chunk is admitted. The stall timeline puts the stall after the second renewal completes.

    Notes

    Equation: stop distance = speed*(lease + tick + bus + brake_onset) + speed**2/(2*deceleration) + loc_margin + physical_margin. Subtract fixed delays from the distance-derived delay ceiling to obtain the lease ceiling.

    Assumptions: Straight-line constant speed before constant-deceleration braking; the constructed stall occurs after the second renewal completes.

    Excludes: Jerk ramps, changing obstacles, stochastic delays, and proof that a deployed watchdog or brake meets these budgets.

    References

    Derivation: integrate constant cruise and braking motion, reserve both distance margins, then isolate the lease term in the stopping-distance inequality.

True

    PhaseLagBudgetModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.PhaseLagBudgetModel()

    Phase a sampled loop spends on delay at its crossover frequency.

    The hold costs half a sample period, then the compute and transport delays; a pure delay tau removes 360 f_c tau degrees at crossover (calc_phase_lag_total). Against a stated phase allowance the model also gives the largest total delay the allowance absorbs and the highest crossover the delay supports. A budget, not a stability proof.

    sample_period,
    compute_delay=None,
    transport_delay=None,
    phase_allowance=None,

    Budget the phase consumed by sampling, computation, and transport.

    Parameters
    Name Type Description Default
    crossover Quantity Positive loop crossover frequency, convertible to Hz. required
    sample_period Quantity Positive controller sampling period, convertible to seconds. required
    compute_delay Quantity Nonnegative sample-to-output time; None contributes zero delay. None
    transport_delay Quantity Nonnegative output-to-actuator time; None contributes zero delay. None
    phase_allowance Quantity Positive phase budget in degrees or radians; None omits budget comparisons. None
    Returns
    Name Type Description
    PhaseLagBudgetResult Delays in ms, phases in degrees, and optional crossover ceiling in Hz. The fits flag checks only the stated phase budget.
    See Also

    mlsysim.physics.robotics.control.calc_phase_lag_total : Delay and phase calculation.

    Notes

    Equation: tau = sample_period/2 + compute_delay + transport_delay; phase = 360*crossover*tau degrees. The allowance gives tau_max = phase_allowance/(360*crossover).

    Assumptions: The zero-order hold is approximated by a half-sample delay well below the sampling frequency; compute and transport are pure delays.

    Excludes: Plant poles, controller dynamics, jitter distributions, and a closed-loop stability proof.

    References

    Derivation: a pure delay has frequency response exp(-j*omega*tau), hence phase loss omega*tau; composing delays adds their phase losses.

    Examples

>>> result = PhaseLagBudgetModel().solve(Q_(10, "Hz"), Q_(1, "ms"),
...     phase_allowance=Q_(30, "degree"))
>>> bool(result.fits and result.total_delay > Q_(0, "ms"))
True

    ResponseTimeModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.ResponseTimeModel()

    Response-time bound of one periodic task, R = C + B + I, against its deadline.

    calc_response_time_bound sums the declared WCET, the blocking bound, and the interference bound; the margin and the largest blocking that still meets the deadline follow. With an observed unloaded run, the gap between it and the declared WCET is the claim that timing analysis must justify.

    interference Quantity Nonnegative declared higher-priority interference bound in time units. required

True

    SharedDramReadModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.SharedDramReadModel()

    A permission tick’s snapshot read behind FIFO backlogs on a shared DRAM controller.

    One backlog (a weight tile, a camera transfer, and a telemetry record, at AXI transaction granularity) waits bytes / bandwidth plus its row conflicts (calc_axi_fifo_contention); the tick’s snapshot read follows. Against the sense allowance, the declared WCET, and the execution ceiling (deadline less blocking and interference), each less the compute after sensing, the model counts the most whole backlogs that still fit and the tick’s end time at each count. A FIFO controller is optimistic: a row-hit-first controller can keep passing the read.

    ThermalThrottleModel

    engine.solvers.realtime.ThermalThrottleModel()

    A tick on a governed clock: a thermal trip reduces the clock until the junction clears.

    The tick is sized to its declared WCET at the nominal clock; the throttled clock stretches the same cycles. The junction is a first-order lumped model, T(t) = T_inf - (T_inf - T_0) exp(-t / tau) with steady state T_amb + P theta; the clock returns only at the clear threshold, so the hysteresis lasts tau ln((T_trip - T_quiet) / (T_clear - T_quiet)) after the load stops (calc_first_order_time_to_temperature).

    Excludes: Memory-bound execution, temperature-dependent leakage, spatial thermal gradients, and governor transition overhead.
