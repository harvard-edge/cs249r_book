# ML Systems Taxonomy changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR-draft` until the first reviewed release.

**Identifier policy.** Concept identities (`mlsys.kv-cache`) are permanent: they are never renamed or reused. A concept that is merged or withdrawn is listed under `retired` in `taxonomy/ml-systems-taxonomy.yml` with the identity that replaces it, and links to it redirect. Readable codes (`P4.5`) are stable within a minor version and may be renumbered in a new minor or major version; tools should store identities, not codes. Relation identities (`limits`, `reduces`) follow the same rule as concepts.

## 0.4-draft (November 2026)

The second run of the periodic review, with a pruning pass and an independent sign-off audit. Structure (5 groups, 20 areas, 101 subjects) and codes are unchanged; no identity was retired or renamed.

- Review. Round 1 ran as seven parallel reviews from one model family (`claude-fable-5-1`), one per group plus an evidence lens and a guides lens, so it was not the three-family round 1 the procedure describes; round 2 was the three-family cross-check (`gpt-6.1-sol`, `gemini-4-argon-high`, `claude-opus-5-5`), which returned fixes on about half of the 288 proposed items and caught three concepts the merge had dropped by mistake. A reconciliation script now proves that every cross-check verdict was applied or overridden with a written reason. After the merge, three independent auditors in different families read the whole vocabulary for necessity and publishability; their agreed cuts and fixes were applied, and the four concepts that had skipped round 2 were checked separately.
- 71 concepts added net (878 in all): 85 admitted after the cross-check, 14 cut after the audit as resting on the book plus one lineage or splitting a quantity or named method from a parent that already carried it (their names stay as aliases on the parent). Placement examples record the three contestable ones.
- Guides. Symptoms now declare the quantity they are measured by (`quantities`), kept apart from causes, so a metric no longer poses as a cause of its own symptom; the compiler accepts a check or lever that connects through the quantity. Roles 11 to 16, symptoms 18 to 24, learning paths 6 to 7, after a three-model guides check folded by two-of-three agreement.
- Declared gaps. Closed: attention-FFN disaggregation (two production lineages). Kept: photonic compute; decoded-video view reuse (still one venue lineage). Added: verifiable ML computation; reasoning-trace monitoring.
- Coverage. Design inputs after the refresh: MLSys 2025–2026 papers 88.1% to 90.4%; MLSysBook Volumes I–II 88.7% to 90.0%; Volumes III–IV 84.1% to 85.5%; 2025 systems venues 63.0% to 65.2%; courses and standards unchanged at 100%. New held-out test, compiled blind after the vocabulary was fixed: 202 ML-systems papers from OSDI, NSDI, EuroSys, and ASPLOS 2026, judged strictly by one model with every partial re-judged by a second (one overturn): 22.3% covered by a concept whose definition names the paper's central mechanism, 96.5% homed (45 covered, 150 partial, 7 missing). Most 2026 papers propose a mechanism one step more specific than the vocabulary; that set is the lead list for 0.5 and stays held out until then.
- StaffML. 23 questions tagged with a moved concept were re-read; 10 changed subject. The new concepts were offered to the 185 subject-only questions whose subject gained one, by two independent taggers (`claude-fable-5-1`, `gpt-6.1-sol`): tag-or-not agreement 80.5% (149 of 185); 44 questions gained the concepts both chose. `vault check --strict` passes.
- Method and empirical study (`taxonomy/METHOD.md`, `taxonomy/method-study/`). A blind three-family method competition (`claude-opus-5-5`, `gpt-6-astra`, `gemini-4-argon-high`) and primary-standard citation audit against ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010) produced the synthesized 10-stage construction and refresh workflow (`taxonomy/method-study/synthesized-method.md`), an enumerated five-stratum sampling frame (`S1`–`S5`) with provenance-family cluster splits (`taxonomy/method-study/sampling-frame.md`), and a candidate 7-seat external editorial board (`taxonomy/method-study/candidate-experts.md`). A pre-registered two-area rebuild and blind held-out comparison on `P4` (Deployment & serving, 9 subjects) and `Q4` (Safety & assurance, 4 subjects) across 382 source units (`200` `dev`, `126` `held_out_test`, `56` `reserve`) confirmed that incremental refresh anchored on `0.4-draft` outperforms a single-pass blank-slate rebuild on held-out central-mechanism coverage (`72.2%` vs. `36.7%` any-judge; `36.7%` vs. `10.1%` consensus) and held-out inter-indexer consistency (subject $\kappa$ `0.971` vs. `0.917`, Hooper `81.8%` vs. `72.1%`, Rolling `87.6%` vs. `81.2%`, $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$ `0.724` vs. `0.539`), while surfacing 28 practitioner aliases across 15 concepts (`1,282` entry terms in all: `dynamic batching`, `prefill phase`, `decode phase`, `copy-on-write KV sharing`, `request latency`, `per-query latency`, `RAG KV-cache fusion`, `cached knowledge fusion`, `token-masking automaton`, `FSM-guided decoding`, `token sampling strategy`, `decoding strategy`, `parallel sampling`, `parallel candidate sampling`, `candidate verification`, `verifier gating`, `tensor arena planning`, `static arena memory planning`, `unsafe control action`, `UCA`, `forward-invariant safe set`, `selective abstention`, `uncertainty-triggered abstention`, `bound propagation`, `symbolic interval propagation`, `rare-event importance sampling`, `accelerated stress testing`, `zero-failure statistical bound`), a secondary placement of `mlsys.memory-planning` on `P4.9` (`on-device-deployment`), and four permanent workflow gates (heading-only warrant ban, coupled-phase and sub-step anti-splitting under Z39.19 §8.2, cross-area lookup across all 20 areas before admitting a concept, and the Z39.19 §7 `metric × workload` split gate). Relationship `references` name a reading basis per concept family, not a per-edge citation; the field `reference_role` says so.
- Standards. The taxonomy now states and follows published standards: ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010) for construction, W3C SKOS with a declared OWL extension for representation, DCTERMS and DCAT 3 for publication, and Hooper and Rolling consistency beside kappa. `METHOD.md` marks every applicable clause met, partial, or open. Two independent audits applied the all-and-some test to all 127 `specializes` edges: they agreed on 111, the 27 both failed were corrected, and 16 remain for expert review. `specializes` now exports as `skos:broader`; `part-of` stays associative under Z39.19 section 8.3.3.2. The compiler enforces SKOS S27, and tests validate the release against the SKOS integrity conditions. Each concept records `added_in` and computed warrant counts; 27 concepts have no recorded warrant and form a review queue. Concept, relation, and scheme URIs now resolve to redirect pages.
- Not changed, for the author to decide: moving Training compute estimate beside its inference sibling in Workload characterization; moving Bandwidth-delay product to Network fabrics; the audits' proposed merges among older entries (Accuracy-efficiency frontier into Pareto frontier, Energy per inference into Energy efficiency, Goal Structuring Notation and Request correlation keys to aliases, ML team roles); six guide additions two reviewers agreed on that lack a reviewed edge to a cause (distributed tracing as a TTFT check, statistical power as an offline-online check, the trajectory log as a runaway-agent check, onboard compute partitioning as a control-loop lever, sliced evaluation as a quantization check, rollback as a deploy-incompatibility lever).

On the design-input checklists: MLSys 2025–2026 papers 87.6% to 88.1% covered; MLSysBook Volumes III–IV 83.0% to 84.1%; Volumes I–II unchanged at 88.7%; courses and standards 100.0% covered (208 of 208). - Book alignment. 119 new links from field concepts to the book passages that teach them, proposed independently by two models and kept only where both agreed and the lead's reading confirmed them (four agreed links were dropped as neighbors rather than what the passage teaches). Concepts that point into a specific book section: 86 to 190 of 807; links: 102 to 221.
- License. The taxonomy release (identifiers, labels, definitions, relationships, guides, and every download) is now CC BY 4.0; the book prose keeps its own license.
- Downloads. The JSON release now includes the declared gaps and each concept's candidate book passages; the concepts CSV gains a column of book chapters; `mlsys-taxonomy.linkml.yml` and `mlsys-taxonomy.schema.json` provide the formal LinkML metamodel and compiled JSON Schema.
- Tooling & LinkML metamodel. The subject framework, concept vocabulary, relationships, practitioner guides, and SKOS hierarchy pilot are now unified into a single canonical source file (`taxonomy/ml-systems-taxonomy.yml`, with `home` declared directly on each concept and `views[id=all]` and subject concept lists derived automatically by the compiler), governed alongside `mlsysbook-alignment.yml`, `coverage/*.yml`, `agreement/*.yml`, and `coverage-ledger.yml` by a declarative LinkML metamodel (`taxonomy/schema/mlsys-taxonomy.linkml.yml`) and validated by `binder/cli/core/taxonomy_schema.py` during `./binder/binder concepts check` and `build`, alongside ontological DAG acyclicity (`specializes`, `part-of`, `requires`), verb–predicate polarity agreement, symptom bucket disjointness, and 100% subject role coverage. `binder concepts build` also auto-syncs `staffml/app/src/data/mlsys-taxonomy.json`. All taxonomy sources, coverage records, agreement records, documentation (`README.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`), and explorer pages now live together at the repository root in `taxonomy/`, with the interactive **Concept map** presented first on landing.

## 0.2-draft (October 2026)

- New organizing frame: five groups (Foundations, Stack, Lifecycle, Qualities, System types), 20 areas, 101 subjects, each with a nested readable code.
- Concept vocabulary grown from 175 to 735 concepts, with background concepts from adjacent fields tagged.
- Relationships rebuilt for concept maps: 1,531 relationships, each using one of 17 controlled relations with forward and inverse phrasing; every concept has at least two; the graph is connected. The relation `bounds` was renamed `limits` before release.
- Coverage is measured with `binder concepts coverage` against recorded checklists in `taxonomy/coverage/`, separating design inputs from held-out tests.
- First held-out measurement, before closing the gaps it found (concept coverage / subject home): MLSys 2025–2026 papers 67.2% / 97.2%; MLSysBook Volume I–II primary concepts 85.1% / 99.5%; Volume III–IV 69.6% / 100%. After adding the field-level concepts those tests surfaced, the same sets read 87.6%, 88.7%, and 83.0% concept coverage and now serve as design inputs; book-specific framing devices were left to the books. The next held-out test is the MLSys 2027 program.
- Cross-model review (one GPT and one Claude reviewer, both "yes with fixes") led to: CPU execution, dynamic shapes, graph rewriting, sparse and ragged tensors, streaming and serverless inference, LLM request scheduling, GNN partitioning, licensing and provenance, robotics middleware, and a fleet data engine (24 concepts); the serving subject split into Serving architectures and Compound AI & routing; diffusion inference moved under generative inference, streaming speech under serving, in-context learning under adaptation, and neighbor sampling under parallelism; Lifecycle codes changed from L to P so codes never read as StaffML depth levels; tagging rules published; `mlsys.llm-kernel-generation` retired in favor of `mlsys.autotuning` (using ML to optimize systems is out of scope).
- Second review round (both reviewers yes-with-fixes, no must-fix items): the home rule restated so the outline follows it, with seven worked examples; parallelism split into strategies and Parallelization planning & placement; new subject Data loading & input pipelines (host loading, prefetching, neighbor sampling); Large language model narrowed to a workload class; speech serving concepts redefined around incremental serving under a new Streaming inference concept; reward hacking moved to post-training; scope notes between queueing, serving metrics, and profiling and between containers and cluster managers; practitioner aliases (GPU architecture, tensor core, ZeRO/FSDP, CUDA Graphs); 22 concepts, among them request throughput, latency percentiles, cache-aware routing, process groups, inference APIs, agentic workload, delivery semantics, online feature consistency, out-of-distribution detection, secrets management, deletion propagation, memory fragmentation, and measurement variability.
- Tagging agreement measured: two independent model taggers on 300 StaffML questions agreed on the subject for 78.7% of items (κ 0.78) under the first rules. Four rules drawn from their disagreements and 13 concepts they proposed raised this to 83.7% (κ 0.83) on the same sample, which is therefore no longer held out. Recorded in `taxonomy/agreement/` and recomputed by `binder concepts agreement`.
- First use as a tagging vocabulary: all 10,716 StaffML questions carry `taxonomy_subject` and `taxonomy_concepts`. On a held-out sample of 300 questions, two independent model taggers agreed on the subject for 80.7% (κ 0.80). Eight concepts were added from the questions the first pass could not tag with a concept.
- Public downloads: subjects and concepts CSV, JSON, SKOS Turtle, GraphML, BibTeX.
