# ML Systems Taxonomy changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR-draft` until the first reviewed release.

**Identifier policy.** Concept identities (`mlsys.kv-cache`) are permanent: they are never renamed or reused. A concept that is merged or withdrawn is listed under `retired` in `taxonomy/ml-systems-field.yml` with the identity that replaces it, and links to it redirect. Readable codes (`P4.5`) are stable within a minor version and may be renumbered in a new minor or major version; tools should store identities, not codes. Relation identities (`limits`, `reduces`) follow the same rule as concepts.

## 0.3-draft (October 2026)

A completeness review, the first run of the periodic review described in `taxonomy/README.md`. Structure (5 groups, 20 areas, 101 subjects) and codes are unchanged; no identity was retired or renamed.

- Review. Three model reviewers (`gpt-6.1-sol`, `gemini-4-argon-high`, `claude-opus-5-5`) reviewed 0.2 independently through different lenses and made 114 proposals. The merged set was then checked item by item by all three, which returned a fix for most items; definitions, relations, conditions, aliases, and background tags were corrected before acceptance. Ten more concepts came from StaffML questions the tagging pass could not tag with a concept (each proposed by two or more questions), and three from the check of those ten; they went through the same three-way check.
- 70 concepts added (805 in all), with 166 relationships (1,697 in all). Foundations: Multi-head latent attention; Residual connection; Recovery time and point objectives; Bandwidth-delay product. Stack: Pipeline schedule; Graph pipeline parallelism; Variable-length parallelism planning; Fine-grained accelerator sharing; Sequence packing; Optical circuit switching; Co-packaged optics; Advanced accelerator packaging; FPGA-based ML acceleration; Device-initiated communication; Wafer-scale acceleration; Register pressure; Software pipelining; Execute-in-place weights; Collective algorithm selection; Always-on sensing cascade; Forward error correction; Erasure coding; Backfill scheduling; Network traffic classes and QoS; DMA cache maintenance. Lifecycle: Expert offloading; Intra-device overlap of bottlenecks; RAG pipeline scheduling; Off-policy evaluation; Document chunking; Model merging; Group relative policy optimization; Inter-token latency; Hybrid retrieval; Model collapse; Embedding model migration; Model-grader bias; Data curriculum and annealing; Sharded streaming dataset format; Fan-out tail amplification; Agent training environment; Misuse monitoring; Masked diffusion decoding; Multi-task learning; Multiple comparisons correction; Model-assisted labeling; Telemetry sampling and cardinality control. Qualities: Cross-stack profiling; Training data extraction; Resource-exhaustion attack; Generated-content watermarking; Representational bias evaluation; Error-correcting code memory; Integer-only inference; Confused deputy; Over-refusal; Fault tree analysis; Metastable failure; Proxy discrimination; Quantization granularity; Incremental checkpointing. System types: System identification; Motion forecasting; Action tokenization; Coordinate frames and transforms; Sensor degradation; Cross-embodiment training; Dual-rate hierarchical policy; Robot kinematics; Model-directed control flow.
- 15 definitions revised, each checked against the concept's existing relationships so they stay true: RL with verifiable rewards (a reward source, no longer aliased to GRPO), Time per output token (a per-request average; inter-token latency is now its own concept), Durable execution, Large language model, Diffusion or flow-matching policy (renamed from Diffusion policy), On-device inference runtime (now includes browsers), Accuracy-efficiency frontier, Context window (the supported span, not its contents; alias "sequence length" removed), Tensor parallelism, Label quality, Retraining, Tool discovery protocol, Tokenization, Model inversion, SIMT execution. Aliases added to nine more, among them A/B testing, canary release, indirect prompt injection, specification gaming, covariate and label shift, load shedding, prompt caching, and attention sinks.
- Moved concepts (identity unchanged; StaffML questions tagged with them keep their recorded subject until re-reviewed): PII detection and de-identification from P1.5 to Q3.3 under the home rule; Inference compute and memory estimate from F3.4 to F1.2, beside the other accounting methods.
- One name, one concept. Five names each led to two concepts (Pareto frontier, SIL, model export, data loader, app memory limit); each now belongs to its dedicated concept, and the compiler rejects a label or alias shared by two concepts.
- Declared gaps. Closed: compressed-KV attention variants, optical and co-packaged interconnect, browser inference runtimes, FPGAs and advanced packaging. Narrowed: emerging compute substrates to photonic compute. Added: attention-FFN disaggregation (one production lineage so far) and decoded-video view reuse (one venue paper so far).
- Coverage records. Judgments must name the covered mechanism; "GRPO coefficients" now maps to GRPO instead of the reward source, and AccelOpt is out of scope like TritorX (ML optimizing ML systems). On the design-input checklists: MLSys 2025–2026 papers 87.6% to 88.1% covered; MLSysBook Volumes III–IV 83.0% to 84.1%; Volumes I–II unchanged at 88.7%. Held-out test: 135 ML-systems papers from the 2025 programs of OSDI, SOSP, NSDI, EuroSys, and ASPLOS, compiled by one model without access to the taxonomy and judged by two others (`taxonomy/coverage/systems-venues-2025.yml`). At first measurement: 51.1% covered by a concept whose definition names the paper's central mechanism, and 100% homed in a subject, so no paper fell outside the subjects. The recurring gaps it surfaced, each backed by two or more independent papers, were then closed with seven concepts (expert offloading, variable-length parallelism planning, fine-grained accelerator sharing, intra-device overlap, graph pipeline parallelism, RAG pipeline scheduling, cross-stack profiling) after the same three-way check, raising the set to 60.0%; it is now a design input. The other open papers each propose a mechanism with one lineage so far; they stay partial until a second source appears. The next held-out test is the MLSys 2027 program.
- Guides. Eleven role profiles, seventeen production symptoms (causes, checks, levers), and five learning paths, reviewed item by item by three models and published on the page and in the JSON download. Each symptom check and lever must share a relationship with one of the symptom's causes; sixteen relationships the guides needed and the graph lacked were added (1,713 in all).
- Book alignment. 119 new links from field concepts to the book passages that teach them, proposed independently by two models and kept only where both agreed and the lead's reading confirmed them (four agreed links were dropped as neighbors rather than what the passage teaches). Concepts that point into a specific book section: 86 to 190 of 805; links: 102 to 221.
- License. The taxonomy release (identifiers, labels, definitions, relationships, guides, and every download) is now CC BY 4.0; the book prose keeps its own license.
- Downloads. The JSON release now includes the declared gaps and each concept's candidate book passages; the concepts CSV gains a column of book chapters.
- Tooling. A chapter pointer resolves only to a folder that holds its chapter source, so leftover build folders cannot break the build. All taxonomy sources, coverage records, agreement records, documentation (`README.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`), and explorer pages now live together at the repository root in `taxonomy/`.

## 0.2-draft (October 2026)

- New organizing frame: five groups (Foundations, Stack, Lifecycle, Qualities, System types), 20 areas, 101 subjects, each with a nested readable code.
- Concept vocabulary grown from 175 to 735 concepts, with background concepts from adjacent fields tagged.
- Relationships rebuilt for concept maps: 1,531 relationships, each using one of 17 controlled relations with forward and inverse phrasing; every concept has at least two; the graph is connected. The relation `bounds` was renamed `limits` before release.
- Coverage is measured with `binder concepts coverage` against recorded checklists in `taxonomy/coverage/`, separating design inputs from held-out tests.
- First held-out measurement, before closing the gaps it found (concept coverage / subject home): MLSys 2025–2026 papers 67.2% / 97.2%; MLSysBook Volume I–II primary concepts 85.1% / 99.5%; Volume III–IV 69.6% / 100%. After adding the field-level concepts those tests surfaced, the same sets read 87.6%, 88.7%, and 83.0% concept coverage and now serve as design inputs; book-specific framing devices were left to the books. The next held-out test is the MLSys 2027 program.
- Cross-model review (one GPT and one Claude reviewer, both "yes with fixes") led to: CPU execution, dynamic shapes, graph rewriting, sparse and ragged tensors, streaming and serverless inference, LLM request scheduling, GNN partitioning, licensing and provenance, robotics middleware, and a fleet data engine (24 concepts); the serving subject split into Serving architectures and Compound AI & routing; diffusion inference moved under generative inference, streaming speech under serving, in-context learning under adaptation, and neighbor sampling under parallelism; Lifecycle codes changed from L to P so codes never read as StaffML depth levels; tagging rules published; `mlsys.llm-kernel-generation` retired in favor of `mlsys.autotuning` (using ML to optimize systems is out of scope).
- Second review round (both reviewers yes-with-fixes, no must-fix items): the home rule restated so the outline follows it, with seven worked examples; parallelism split into strategies and Parallelization planning & placement; new subject Data loading & input pipelines (host loading, prefetching, neighbor sampling); Large language model narrowed to a workload class; speech serving concepts redefined around incremental serving under a new Streaming inference concept; reward hacking moved to post-training; scope notes between queueing, serving metrics, and profiling and between containers and cluster managers; practitioner aliases (GPU architecture, tensor core, ZeRO/FSDP, CUDA Graphs); 22 concepts, among them request throughput, latency percentiles, cache-aware routing, process groups, inference APIs, agentic workload, delivery semantics, online feature consistency, out-of-distribution detection, secrets management, deletion propagation, memory fragmentation, and measurement variability.
- Tagging agreement measured: two independent model taggers on 300 StaffML questions agreed on the subject for 78.7% of items (κ 0.78) under the first rules. Four rules drawn from their disagreements and 13 concepts they proposed raised this to 83.7% (κ 0.83) on the same sample, which is therefore no longer held out. Recorded in `taxonomy/agreement/` and recomputed by `binder concepts agreement`.
- First use as a tagging vocabulary: all 10,716 StaffML questions carry `taxonomy_subject` and `taxonomy_concepts`. On a held-out sample of 300 questions, two independent model taggers agreed on the subject for 80.7% (κ 0.80). Eight concepts were added from the questions the first pass could not tag with a concept.
- Public downloads: subjects and concepts CSV, JSON, SKOS Turtle, GraphML, BibTeX.
