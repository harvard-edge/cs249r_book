# How the ML Systems Taxonomy is built

This document states the published standards the taxonomy adopts, the project operational rules that implement them, how each recommendation is met, and where it is not yet met. Status terms are used consistently: **met** means the record or an automated check shows it, **met by design** means the structural choice in the schema or framework satisfies an option the standard leaves to the designer, **met by exclusion** means the governed entity or relation class is excluded by explicit project scope policy so the requirement holds vacuously, **partial** means part of the recommendation is implemented and the remaining gap is named, and **open** means it has not yet been done. Numbers describe version `0.4-draft` (including its post-`dda2fdb80a` Phase 7 entry-term and definition amendment) and are computed from the release files in `taxonomy/` (including `taxonomy/term-provenance.yml`), the public study and provenance artifacts in `taxonomy/method-study/` (including `taxonomy/method-study/study-receipts-summary.json`), and the maintainer's local campaign archive in `.claude/_reviews/taxonomy-method/20261102/`.

## The standards

| Layer | Standard adopted | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Construction | [ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010)](https://groups.niso.org/higherlogic/ws/public/download/6484/Guidelines%20for%20the%20Construction,%20Format,%20and%20Management%20of%20Monolingual%20Controlled%20Vocabularies.pdf), *Guidelines for the Construction, Format, and Management of Monolingual Controlled Vocabularies* | How terms are chosen, formed, related, tested, and maintained |
| Structural principle | [ISO 25964-1:2011](https://niso.org/schemas/iso25964) (*Thesauri for information retrieval*), via its public NISO/W3C SKOS alignment | Separation of permanent concept identity (`skos:Concept` URI) from string labels (`skos:prefLabel`, `skos:altLabel`) |
| Representation | [W3C SKOS Reference](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/) (Recommendation, 2009), with a declared OWL extension for typed relations | The published data model |
| Metadata and publication | [DCTERMS](https://www.dublincore.org/specifications/dublin-core/dcmi-terms/), [W3C DCAT 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/), [W3C Best Practice Recipes for Publishing RDF Vocabularies](https://www.w3.org/TR/swbp-vocab-pub/), and [Ten simple rules for making a vocabulary FAIR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8238180) | Identifiers, licensing, versions, and reuse |
| Validation | Library-science inter-indexer consistency measures — Hooper's measure and [Rolling's measure](https://informationr.net/ir/18-4/paper601.html) — reported alongside general multi-label and nominal agreement statistics (Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$ and Cohen's $\kappa$) and anti-gaming specificity bounds ($p_{\max} \le 15\%$) | Whether people and tools apply the vocabulary the same way |

**ISO 25964 scope.** ISO 25964-1 and ISO 25964-2 are the international thesaurus and interoperability standards, and [NISO describes their scope](https://niso.org/schemas/iso25964). Because their full text is sold rather than openly published, clause-level conformance is itemized against ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010) and W3C SKOS rather than ISO 25964 clause numbers. Structurally, `taxonomy/schema/mlsys-taxonomy.linkml.yml` adopts ISO 25964-1's separation of permanent concept identity (`Concept` mapped to `skos:Concept`) from lexical terms, representing preferred and entry terms as SKOS string properties (`label` $\rightarrow$ `skos:prefLabel`, `aliases` $\rightarrow$ `skos:altLabel`) rather than reifying SKOS-XL `skosxl:Label` instances (or ISO 25964 `ThesaurusTerm` node classes).

**Standard recommendations vs. project operational rules.** Z39.19 provides general controlled-vocabulary principles (literary warrant in `§5.3.5.1`, unique entities in `§6.3.3`, compound-term factoring in `§7`, equivalence and upward posting in `§8.2`, generic, instance, and whole-part relationships in `§8.3`, and construction/testing/maintenance in `§11`). The specific quantitative thresholds and domain rules used in this repository — the $\ge 2$ `provenance_family_id` warrant threshold with $\ge 1$ external lineage, excluding commercial products and multi-mechanism frameworks/benchmarks from concept nodes and suite aliases, the `metric × workload` split gate, the 4-part Differential Indexing Witness test, the `< 2` `dev`-lineage upward-posting granularity threshold, singular preferred labels, and the minimum of two relationships per concept — are **project operational policies** designed to implement those principles reproducibly.

**What Z39.19 says this is.** Under section 5.4 a *taxonomy* connects all preferred terms in a hierarchy, and a *thesaurus* displays several relationship types with standardized indicators. This vocabulary keeps the familiar name but is structurally a thesaurus: most links are associative, and subjects are browsing groups (`skos:Collection`) rather than broader terms.

**Ontology methodologies.** This project adopts controlled-vocabulary guidance and does not claim to implement an ontology-development methodology such as NeOn or METHONTOLOGY. Relations are declared in OWL only so that the SKOS extension is machine-readable.

## How a version is built

Z39.19 section 11.1.3 recommends a committee approach (`§11.1.3.1`), an empirical approach (`§11.1.3.2`), or a combination. This project uses empirical construction under a top-down outline, anchored on the published vocabulary and verified by three model families (`claude-opus-5-5`, `gpt-6-astra` / `gpt-6.1-sol`, `gemini-4-argon-high`). Full operational rules and mathematical definitions are recorded in `taxonomy/method-study/synthesized-method.md` and executed via `.claude/workflows/taxonomy-refresh.md`:

1. **Scope, fixed structure, and cluster-disjoint sampling frame** (implementing Z39.19 §5.2, §11.1.2; `Step 0` and `Step 1` of `taxonomy-refresh.md`). The five groups, 20 areas, and 101 subjects are fixed at the start of a refresh and change only by recorded decision. Completeness and candidate extraction draw from an enumerated five-stratum sampling frame (`taxonomy/method-study/sampling-frame.md`; note that sampling-frame strata `S1`–`S5` are distinct from Stack area codes `S1`–`S4`): textbooks and reference monographs (stratum `S1`, including external monographs plus internal `MLSysBook` chapters `pf_mlsysbook_*`), university graduate and upper-division courses (stratum `S2`, including external courses plus internal `Harvard CS249r` materials `pf_harvard_cs249r_*`), peer-reviewed systems and ML conference venues (stratum `S3`), practitioner artifacts, postmortems, runtime architecture docs, and untagged `StaffML` questions (stratum `S4`, including internal `pf_staffml_*`), and engineering, safety/governance, and benchmark standards (stratum `S5`). Every source unit is tagged globally across all 20 areas with a `provenance_family_id`, `origin` (`external` vs. `internal`), and `source_type` (`prose` vs. `heading`) and partitioned by whole cluster before construction (target cluster proportions `60%` `dev`, `25%` `held_out_test`, and `15%` `reserve` on fresh unsplit enumerations, combined with pre-existing `held_out` benchmark designations where present; `stage_reviewer_workspace.py` builds a physical read-only `reviewer-workspace` containing only `dev` files for Round-1 and Round-2 reviewers; see `sampling-frame.md` §3).
2. **Gate 1 — Cross-area lookup against the existing vocabulary** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §11.1.1, §11.1.5; `Step 2`–`Step 4b`). Before proposing any concept for a subject row, builders search all 20 areas and 101 subjects (`taxonomy/downloads/mlsys-taxonomy-concepts.csv`) by preferred label, aliases, and definition tokens (enforced semantically by the 3-family cross-checkers and mechanically for exact normalized-name and retired-ID collisions by `reconcile.py` and the compiler). When a concept homed in another area already owns the mechanism (such as `mlsys.memory-planning` in Stack area `S2` or `mlsys.selective-prediction` in Lifecycle area `P3`), the workflow adds a typed `alias` or a `secondary_concepts` link on the subject row rather than minting a duplicate concept.
3. **Gate 2 — Candidate extraction, two-lineage warrant, and heading-only ban** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §5.3.5.1–5.3.5.3, §11.1.3.2; `Step 2`–`Step 4b`). Candidates are extracted inductively from `dev` units across three model families. Admission requires literary warrant from at least two sources belonging to distinct `provenance_family_id` lineages across `S1`–`S5`, at least one of which is an **external (non-MLSysBook / non-CS249r / non-StaffML) lineage** (`origin: external`), and at least one of which contains explanatory prose defining the mechanism (`reconcile.py` unconditionally requires `<campaign>/sampling-manifest.json` whenever `concepts_add`, `upward-posting`, or `quasi-synonym` aliases appear, failing closed on missing manifests, duplicate `unit_id`s, missing `split`/`origin`/`source_type` fields, or cross-split `provenance_family_id` leaks, and verifies every `warrant_sources` and `checked_dev_units` entry against `dev` units in that manifest). **Heading-only warrant ban:** a syllabus or checklist topic heading (`source_type: heading`) may serve as the corroborating second `provenance_family_id` when paired with a prose source that defines the mechanism, but bare headings alone (zero prose sources) cannot mint a concept or justify extrapolated mechanisms. Distinct concepts with only one lineage in `dev` are recorded as declared `gaps`.
4. **Gate 3 — Product, vendor, benchmark, and SKU exclusion** (project scope policy inspired by Z39.19 §6.3.3 and §8.2; `Step 2`–`Step 4b`). Commercial products, multi-mechanism software frameworks or serving engines (`vLLM`, `TensorRT-LLM`, `SGLang`, `DeepSpeed`, `PyTorch`), hardware SKUs (`H100`, `B200`, `MI300X`), and benchmark suites (`MLPerf`) are never admitted as concept nodes. A named algorithm or single-mechanism implementation (`PagedAttention` on `mlsys.paged-kv`, `ZeRO` and `FSDP` on `mlsys.zero-sharding` [*ZeRO-style training-state partitioning*], `CUDA Graphs` on `mlsys.graph-capture-replay`) may be recorded as an `alias` (`alt_label`) **only when it is 1-to-1 synonymous with that single mechanism**; multi-mechanism frameworks and benchmark suites (`vLLM`, `TensorRT-LLM`, `SGLang`, `MLPerf`, `benchmark suite`) are excluded from both concepts and aliases (enforced by a non-overridable heuristic scanner in `reconcile.py` backed by 3-family semantic cross-checking; legacy model-family alias `XGBoost` on `mlsys.gradient-boosted-trees` is queued for editorial board review).
5. **Gate 4 — Term form, compound factoring, and `metric × workload` gate** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §6.1–6.7, §7.1–7.6; `Step 2`–`Step 4b`). Each concept receives one singular noun-phrase preferred label (`label`, a documented project departure from Z39.19 §6.5's plural count-noun default), entry terms (`aliases`), and a one-sentence plain genus-differentia definition (`definition`, accompanied on redefinitions by a mandatory `edge_impact_note` verifying incident relationships). Compound candidates (`A and B`, evaluated by the 3-family cross-checkers) are split unless institutionalized across $\ge 2$ provenance lineages. **Compound metric gate (heuristic token scanner in `reconcile.py` backed by cross-checkers):** generic `metric × workload` combinations (`inference throughput`, `inference energy cost`, `training power`) are rejected unless they define an allowlisted stage-specific structural measure (`TTFT`, `TPOT`, `inter-token latency`, `goodput`, `MFU`, `model bandwidth utilization`, `request throughput`, `latency percentiles`; legacy `mlsys.energy-per-inference` is queued for editorial merger into `mlsys.energy-efficiency`).
6. **Gate 5 — 4-part Concept-vs-Alias Differential Indexing Witness test and `Z39.19 §8.2` equivalence gate** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §6.1, §8.2.1–§8.2.4; `Step 2`–`Step 4b`). A warranted candidate $C$ becomes a distinct `Concept` rather than an `alias` of an existing concept $P$ only if all four hold: (a) it introduces a distinct system invariant, resource envelope, failure boundary, analytical causal model, or measurement semantics; (b) it carries an independent failure mode, trade-off, or decision consequence not identical to $P$; (c) it either is a valid `all-and-some` (`specializes`) child of $P$ with its own differentiating properties or participates in a causal/associative edge not inherited from $P$; and (d) it has a non-overridable structured **Differential Indexing Witness** (`witness_justification` on `concepts_add`: `compared_concept`, `criterion_a`/`b`/`c`, and distinct `scenario_a` and `scenario_b`). When a distinct concept passes Gate 5 (such as `adaptive stress testing`) but lacks two-lineage warrant in `dev` under Gate 2, it goes to `gaps:`, **never** automatically to `aliases`. **Alias equivalence and `§8.2.4` upward-posting tiebreak:** every added `alias` requires non-overridable `alias_metadata` (`synonym`, `quasi-synonym`, `acronym`, or `upward-posting` plus `source`, and for both `upward-posting` and `quasi-synonym`, manifest-verified `checked_dev_units` and `scope_note` checked mechanically by `reconcile.py`, plus integer `dev_lineages_requiring_separation` $\in \{0, 1\}$ on `upward-posting`; `remove_aliases` requires `remove_aliases_rationale`). Near-synonyms (`Z39.19 §8.2.3`, such as `selective abstention` on `mlsys.selective-prediction`) and generic class/member upward postings (`Z39.19 §8.2.4`, such as `dynamic request batching` on `mlsys.batching` [qualified per `Z39.19 §6.3.2` to avoid `S2` dynamic-computation-graph homographs] and `grammar-constrained decoding` on `mlsys.constrained-decoding`) are permitted **only when** verbatim-attested, free of cross-area homograph collisions (`Z39.19 §5.3.1`, which excluded unqualified `reject-option classification` due to `Q5` fairness post-processing), and (for `upward-posting`) every instance of the narrower term is an instance of the broader concept with fewer than `2` independent `dev` lineages requiring separate indexing (`>= 2` independent `dev` lineages requiring diagnostic separation wins the tiebreak and mandates a separate `Concept`, as with `mlsys.continuous-batching` vs. `mlsys.batching`). Constituent execution phases with distinct resource profiles (`prefill phase` vs. `decode phase`), distinct analytical methodologies (`FMEA`, `FTA`, and `STPA` under `Hazard analysis`), planning artifacts (`preregistered analysis plan`), and method-produced artifacts (`unsafe control action` under `STPA`) are **not** valid class/member upward postings and must not be added as aliases (enforced semantically by the 3-family cross-checkers under `briefs/crosscheck.md`).
7. **Home subject and typed relationships** (Z39.19 §8.1, §8.3, §8.4; SKOS S27; `Step 3`–`Step 6`). Each concept receives exactly one `home` subject under the deterministic 4-step Home-Rule decision tree. Every concept participates in at least two relationships from the 17 controlled verbs. Generic `specializes` (`skos:broader`) requires the 3-step `all-and-some` test (`Z39.19 §8.3.1`); `part-of` stays associative under `skos:related` (`Z39.19 §8.3.3.2`) because system components participate in multiple assemblies; `SKOS S27` makes `skos:related` disjoint with the transitive closure `skos:broaderTransitive`; and every relationship must read true in both forward and inverse phrasing under its explicit `conditions`.
8. **Cross-check, deterministic reconciliation, and independent audit** (Z39.19 §11.1.5, §11.2.2; `Step 4`, `Step 4b`, `Step 5`, `Step 5b`). All three model families cross-check the merged patch operation by operation (`accept`, `fix`, `reject`) inside an isolated `dev`-only `reviewer-workspace`. `.claude/workflows/taxonomy-refresh/reconcile.py` parses YAML with duplicate-key rejection (`UniqueKeyLoader`), validates operation schemas (`OP_SCHEMA`, shared with `apply.py`), enforces exact 3-family coverage across all canonical operations (including `concepts_retire` and canonicalized `row:<id>`), blocks $\ge 2$-family `reject` items and unreviewed operations without exception, verifies per-reviewer concrete field-level `fixes` (including recursive nested dictionary conflict checks and edge `source`/`target` endpoints) without mutating unrequested fields, runs non-overridable structural/baseline/manifest red-flag checks (`check_red_flags`), and writes `reconcile-receipt.json` (`schema_version: 3`), whose SHA-256 digests (`reconcile.py`, `ml-systems-taxonomy.yml`, `mlsys-taxonomy.linkml.yml`, `patch.yaml`, `final.yaml`, all three `*-r2.yaml`, `overrides.yaml`, and `sampling-manifest.json`) **plus an in-process re-execution of `verify_campaign`** are verified by `apply.py` before writing `ml-systems-taxonomy.yml` and persisting structured `warrant_sources`, `witness_justification`, and `alias_metadata` in `taxonomy/term-provenance.yml`. After compilation (`./binder/binder concepts build` and `check`), three auditors (`Step 5b`, `briefs/audit.md`) inspect the repository sources, `final.yaml`, `sampling-manifest.json`, `term-provenance.yml`, review files, and compiled CSVs for necessity, publishability, warrant grounding, and process compliance.
9. **Blind held-out coverage and inter-indexer agreement** (Z39.19 §11.2.2; `Step 1b` pre-change diagnostic and `Step 5b` post-freeze evaluation). On the sealed **external** `held_out_test` coverage slice (`origin: external` units only), two model evaluators score coverage using a 3-level ordinal expressibility scale (`1.0` single-concept exact match, `0.5` post-coordination of 2–3 concepts, `0.0` uncovered) alongside binary coverage $\kappa$ ($\ge 0.30$ floor) and a seeded-gap sensitivity audit on $k \ge 10$ ablated concepts, and index held-out items to compute subject $\kappa$, conditional and all-item Hooper and Rolling consistency, empty-assignment rate ($\le 20.0\%$), Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$, leaf-concept specificity (`0` incoming `specializes` children), vocabulary utilization, and anti-gaming specificity bounds ($p_{\max} \le 15\%$ max single-concept assignment share). Once any diagnostic or `held_out_test` slice has its gaps inspected, every source unit sharing its `provenance_family_id` clusters is promoted to `dev` (`held_out: false` on checklist files) and replenished from unexposed external `reserve` or fresh post-cutoff external clusters.
10. **Record and release** (Z39.19 §11.3; `Step 6` and `Step 7`). Changelog, permanent identity checks (`retired` with `replaced_by`), generated downloads (`CSV`, `JSON`, `LinkML`, `JSON Schema`, `SKOS Turtle`, `OWL`, `DCAT`, `GraphML`, `BibTeX`, `CITATION.cff`), and RDF/SKOS integrity validation (`pytest binder/tests/test_taxonomy*.py`).

**Human and machine roles.** Z39.19 section 11.1.3.4 assumes construction calls for intellectual decisions and describes machines as assistants for tasks such as identifying candidate terms. This project goes further: language models proposed terms, cross-checked them, and audited the result, and one human Editor holds final authority. That is a substantive departure from the standard's assumption, and it is the main reason the status stays *editorial draft* until the external editorial board (`taxonomy/method-study/candidate-experts.md`) completes its review.

## Empirical method study (November 2026)

In November 2026, the project ran an eight-stage (Phases 0–7) empirical study (public protocol and governance artifacts in `taxonomy/method-study/`, local execution receipts in `.claude/_reviews/taxonomy-method/20261102/`) to evaluate this construction method on two areas: `P4` (`serving` — Deployment & serving, 9 subjects) and `Q4` (`safety-assurance` — Safety & assurance, 4 subjects):

Three blind judges scored the anonymized methods on an 8-dimension rubric (`taxonomy/method-study/rubric.yaml`), and a citation auditor screened standard citations (`phase2-results.json`):

Hooper's measure is $c / (A + B - c)$ and Rolling's is $2c / (A + B)$, where $c = |S_1 \cap S_2|$ is the number of concepts both taggers assigned and $A = |S_1|$, $B = |S_2|$ are the numbers each assigned. Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$ weights multi-label set agreement using Passonneau's (2006) *Measuring Agreement on Set-valued Items* (MASI) distance $d_{\text{MASI}}(S_1, S_2) = 1 - J(S_1, S_2)\cdot M(S_1, S_2)$, where $J(S_1, S_2) = |S_1 \cap S_2| / |S_1 \cup S_2|$ is Jaccard similarity and $M(S_1, S_2)$ is the monotonicity factor ($1$ when $S_1 = S_2$, $2/3$ when $S_1 \subset S_2$ or $S_2 \subset S_1$, $1/3$ when $S_1 \cap S_2 \neq \emptyset$ and neither is a subset, and $0$ when disjoint). On the 300-question StaffML samples, conditional Hooper and Rolling average over items where both taggers assigned at least one concept (`280`, `286`, and `282` of `300`), while all-item Hooper and Rolling include single-empty items (`12`, `12`, and `11`, scored `0.0`) and both-empty subject-only items (`8`, `2`, and `7`, scored `1.0`); subject $\kappa$ uses all `300` items across all `101` subjects. On the held-out `P4`+`Q4` slice (evaluated against pre-amendment `0.4-draft` across the `13` subjects in `P4` and `Q4`, so its subject $\kappa = 0.971$ has a smaller 13-subject candidate space than the 101-subject StaffML runs), all `43` evaluated indexing items (`40` internal StaffML `S4` questions plus `3` external `Q4` `S2`/`S5` items) received at least one concept from both judges (`0` single-empty, `0` both-empty, making conditional and all-item means identical), and max single-concept share was $p_{\max} = 9.84\% \le 15.0\%$. These numbers measure agreement between models on these samples and do not stand in for a trial with human indexers.
