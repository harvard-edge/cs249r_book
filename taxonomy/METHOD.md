# How the ML Systems Taxonomy is built

This document states the published standards the taxonomy adopts, the project operational rules that implement them, how each recommendation is met, and where it is not yet met. Status terms are used consistently: **met** means the record or a check shows it, **partial** means some of it is done and the rest is named, and **open** means it has not been done. Numbers describe version `0.4-draft` and are computed from the release files and campaign receipts named beside them.

## The standards

| Layer | Standard adopted | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Construction | [ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010)](https://groups.niso.org/higherlogic/ws/public/download/6484/Guidelines%20for%20the%20Construction,%20Format,%20and%20Management%20of%20Monolingual%20Controlled%20Vocabularies.pdf), *Guidelines for the Construction, Format, and Management of Monolingual Controlled Vocabularies* | How terms are chosen, formed, related, tested, and maintained |
| Structural model | [ISO 25964-1:2011](https://niso.org/schemas/iso25964) (*Thesauri for information retrieval*), via its public NISO/W3C data-model alignment | Explicit separation of concept identity (`skos:Concept` URI) from lexical labels (`prefLabel`, `altLabel`) |
| Representation | [W3C SKOS Reference](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/) (Recommendation, 2009), with a declared OWL extension for typed relations | The published data model |
| Metadata and publication | [DCTERMS](https://www.dublincore.org/specifications/dublin-core/dcmi-terms/), [W3C DCAT 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/), [W3C Best Practice Recipes for Publishing RDF Vocabularies](https://www.w3.org/TR/swbp-vocab-pub/), and [Ten simple rules for making a vocabulary FAIR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8238180) | Identifiers, licensing, versions, and reuse |
| Validation | Library-science inter-indexer consistency measures — Hooper's measure and [Rolling's measure](https://informationr.net/ir/18-4/paper601.html) — reported alongside general multi-label and nominal agreement statistics (Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$ and Cohen's $\kappa$) and anti-gaming specificity bounds ($p_{\max} \le 15\%$) | Whether people and tools apply the vocabulary the same way |

**ISO 25964 scope.** ISO 25964-1 and ISO 25964-2 are the international thesaurus and interoperability standards, and [NISO describes their scope](https://niso.org/schemas/iso25964). Because their full text is sold rather than openly published, clause-level conformance is itemized against ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010) and W3C SKOS, while adopting ISO 25964-1's concept-centric structural distinction (`ThesaurusConcept` vs. `PreferredTerm`/`SimpleNonPreferredTerm`) in `taxonomy/schema/mlsys-taxonomy.linkml.yml`.

**Standard recommendations vs. project operational rules.** Z39.19 provides general controlled-vocabulary principles (literary warrant in `§5.3.5.1`, unique entities in `§6.3.3`, compound-term factoring in `§7`, equivalence and upward posting in `§8.2`, generic and whole-part relationships in `§8.3`, and construction/testing/maintenance in `§11`). The specific quantitative thresholds and domain rules used in this repository — the $\ge 2$ `provenance_family_id` warrant threshold, excluding commercial products and multi-mechanism frameworks/benchmarks from concept nodes, the `metric × workload` split gate, the 4-part Differential Indexing Witness test, singular preferred labels, and the minimum of two relationships per concept — are **project operational policies** designed to implement those principles reproducibly.

**What Z39.19 says this is.** Under section 5.4 a *taxonomy* connects all preferred terms in a hierarchy, and a *thesaurus* displays several relationship types with standardized indicators. This vocabulary keeps the familiar name but is structurally a thesaurus: most links are associative, and subjects are browsing groups (`skos:Collection`) rather than broader terms.

**Ontology methodologies.** This project adopts controlled-vocabulary guidance and does not claim to implement an ontology-development methodology such as NeOn or METHONTOLOGY. Relations are declared in OWL only so that the SKOS extension is machine-readable.

## How a version is built

Z39.19 section 11.1.3 recommends a committee approach (`§11.1.3.1`), an empirical approach (`§11.1.3.2`), or a combination. This project uses empirical construction under a top-down outline, anchored on the published vocabulary and verified by three independent model families (`claude-opus-5-5`, `gpt-6-astra` / `gpt-6.1-sol`, `gemini-4-argon-high`). Full operational rules and mathematical definitions are recorded in `taxonomy/method-study/synthesized-method.md` and executed via `.claude/workflows/taxonomy-refresh.md`:

1. **Scope, fixed structure, and cluster-disjoint sampling frame** (implementing Z39.19 §5.2, §11.1.2). The five groups, 20 areas, and 101 subjects are fixed at the start of a refresh and change only by recorded decision. Completeness and candidate extraction draw from an enumerated five-stratum sampling frame (`taxonomy/method-study/sampling-frame.md`): graduate syllabi (`S1`), systems and ML conference programs (`S2`), engineering and safety/governance standards (`S3`), practitioner role profiles and untagged StaffML interview questions (`S4`), and MLSysBook chapter contracts (`S5`). Every source unit is tagged with a `provenance_family_id` and partitioned by cluster before construction with target cluster proportions of `60%` `dev` (visible during construction), `25%` `held_out_test` (sealed until post-freeze evaluation), and `15%` `reserve` (to replenish held-out clusters once inspected).
2. **Gate 1 — Cross-area lookup against the existing vocabulary** (implementing Z39.19 §11.1.1, §11.1.5). Before proposing any concept for a subject row, builders search all 20 areas and 101 subjects (`taxonomy/downloads/mlsys-taxonomy-concepts.csv`) by preferred label, aliases, and definition tokens. When a concept homed in another area already owns the mechanism (such as `mlsys.memory-planning` in Stack area `S2` or `mlsys.selective-prediction` in Lifecycle area `P3`), the workflow adds an `alias` or a `secondary_concepts` link on the subject row rather than minting a duplicate concept.
3. **Gate 2 — Candidate extraction, provenance-family warrant, and heading-only ban** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §5.3.5.1–5.3.5.3, §11.1.3.2). Candidates are extracted inductively from `dev` units across three independent model families. Admission requires literary warrant from at least two sources belonging to distinct `provenance_family_id` clusters, at least one of which comes from external strata `S1`–`S4`, and at least one of which contains explanatory prose defining the mechanism. **Heading-only warrant ban:** a syllabus or checklist topic heading (`Topic: ...`) may serve as the corroborating second `provenance_family_id` when paired with a prose source that defines the mechanism, but bare headings alone (with zero prose sources) cannot mint a concept or justify extrapolated mechanisms. Distinct concepts with only one lineage in `dev` are recorded as declared `gaps`.
4. **Gate 3 — Product, vendor, benchmark, and SKU exclusion** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §6.3.3 and §8.2). Commercial products, multi-mechanism software frameworks or serving engines (`vLLM`, `TensorRT-LLM`, `SGLang`, `DeepSpeed`, `PyTorch`), hardware SKUs, and benchmark suites (`MLPerf`) are never admitted as concept nodes. A named algorithm or single-mechanism implementation (`PagedAttention` on `mlsys.paged-kv`) may be recorded as an `alias` (`alt_label`) **only when it is 1-to-1 synonymous with that single mechanism**; multi-mechanism frameworks and benchmark suites belong in reading references or examples, never as aliases of a single concept.
5. **Gate 4 — Term form, compound factoring, and `metric × workload` gate** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §6.1–6.7, §7.1–7.6). Each concept receives one singular noun-phrase preferred label (`label`, a documented project departure from Z39.19 §6.5's plural count-noun default), entry terms (`aliases`), and a one-sentence plain genus-differentia definition (`definition`). Compound candidates (`A and B`, `metric × workload`) are split unless institutionalized across $\ge 2$ provenance families. **Compound metric gate:** generic `metric × workload` combinations (`inference throughput`, `inference energy cost`, `energy per inference`) are rejected unless they define a stage-specific structural measure (`TTFT`, `TPOT`, `inter-token latency`, `goodput`, `MFU`, `model bandwidth utilization`).
6. **Gate 5 — 4-part Concept-vs-Alias Differential Indexing Witness test and coupled-phase anti-splitting** (project policy implementing Z39.19 §6.1, §8.2). A warranted candidate $C$ becomes a distinct `Concept` rather than an `alias` of an existing concept $P$ only if all four hold: (a) it introduces a distinct system invariant, resource envelope, failure boundary, analytical causal model, or measurement semantics; (b) it carries an independent failure mode, trade-off, or decision consequence not identical to $P$; (c) it either is a valid `all-and-some` (`specializes`) child of $P$ with its own differentiating properties or participates in a causal/associative edge not inherited from $P$; and (d) it has a recorded **Differential Indexing Witness** (`witness_justification`) naming two indexing scenarios where one requires $C$ rather than $P$ (or distinguishes $C$ from a sibling $C'$ under $P$). When a distinct concept passes Gate 5 but lacks two-family warrant under Gate 2, it is placed in `gaps:`, never forced into `aliases`. **Coupled-phase & sub-step rule vs. distinct methodologies:** tightly coupled execution phases (`prefill phase` and `decode phase` under `mlsys.prefill-decode`), acronyms (`ODD`, `FMEA`, `STPA`, `FTA`, `SOTIF`, `ALARP`), and non-independently-indexed sub-steps or artifacts of an admitted algorithm (`draft-verification` under `mlsys.speculative-decoding`, `paged copy-on-write KV sharing` under `mlsys.paged-kv`, `unsafe control action` / `UCA` under `mlsys.stpa`) are recorded as `aliases` on the parent concept, whereas distinct analytical methodologies (`Failure modes and effects analysis`, `Fault tree analysis`, and `System-theoretic process analysis` under `Hazard analysis`) are separate `specializes` concepts.
7. **Home subject and typed relationships** (Z39.19 §8.1, §8.3, §8.4; SKOS S27). Each concept receives exactly one `home` subject under the deterministic 4-step Home-Rule decision tree. Every concept participates in at least two relationships from the 17 controlled verbs. Generic `specializes` (`skos:broader`) requires the 3-step `all-and-some` test (`Z39.19 §8.3.1`); `part-of` stays associative under `skos:related` (`Z39.19 §8.3.3.2`) because system components participate in multiple assemblies; `SKOS S27` makes `skos:related` disjoint with the transitive closure `skos:broaderTransitive`; and every relationship must read true in both forward and inverse phrasing under its explicit `conditions`.
8. **Cross-check, deterministic reconciliation, and independent audit** (Z39.19 §11.1.5, §11.2.2). All three model families cross-check the merged patch item by item (`accept`, `fix`, `reject`). `.claude/workflows/taxonomy-refresh/reconcile.py` exits non-zero if any `fix` or `reject` verdict is unreflected in `final.yaml` without a non-empty written override in `overrides.yaml`, if an item rejected by $\ge 2$ models is retained, or if structural red-flag checks fail. After compilation (`./binder/binder concepts build` and `check`), three independent auditors verify necessity, publishability, quote grounding, and process compliance.
9. **Blind held-out coverage and inter-indexer agreement** (Z39.19 §11.2.2). On the sealed `held_out_test` split, two independent model evaluators score coverage using a 3-level ordinal expressibility scale (`1.0` single-concept exact match, `0.5` post-coordination of 2–3 concepts, `0.0` uncovered) and index held-out items to compute subject $\kappa$, Hooper's consistency, Rolling's consistency, and Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$, enforcing an anti-gaming specificity cap ($p_{\max} \le 15\%$ max single-concept assignment share). Once a `held_out_test` slice is used to diagnose gaps, its clusters are promoted to `dev` for future cycles and replenished from `reserve`.
10. **Record and release** (Z39.19 §11.3). Changelog, permanent identity checks (`retired` with `replaced_by`), generated downloads (`CSV`, `JSON`, `LinkML`, `JSON Schema`, `SKOS Turtle`, `OWL`, `DCAT`, `GraphML`, `BibTeX`, `CITATION.cff`), and RDF/SKOS integrity validation (`pytest binder/tests/test_taxonomy*.py`).

**Human and machine roles.** Z39.19 section 11.1.3.4 assumes construction calls for intellectual decisions and describes machines as assistants for tasks such as identifying candidate terms. This project goes further: language models proposed terms, cross-checked them, and audited the result, and one human Editor holds final authority. That is a substantive departure from the standard's assumption, and it is the main reason the status stays *editorial draft* until the external editorial board (`taxonomy/method-study/candidate-experts.md`) completes its review.

## Empirical method study (November 2026)

In November 2026, the project ran a seven-phase empirical study (`taxonomy/method-study/`, machine-readable receipts in `.claude/_reviews/taxonomy-method/20261102/`) to evaluate this construction method on two areas: `P4` (`serving` — Deployment & serving, 9 subjects) and `Q4` (`safety-assurance` — Safety & assurance, 4 subjects):

1. **Blind method competition and citation audit (Phases 0–2).** Three model families (`gpt-6-astra` as `Method-A`, `claude-opus-5-5` as `Method-B`, `gemini-4-argon-high` as `Method-C`) designed end-to-end taxonomy methods in isolated workspaces with zero repository access (`0` leaks across 14 leak patterns; `phase1-manifest.json`). Three blind judges scored the anonymized methods on an 8-dimension rubric (`taxonomy/method-study/rubric.yaml`), and a reference-knowledge citation auditor checked every standard citation (`phase2-results.json`):
   - Cross-family (non-self) mean scores out of `40`: `Method-B` = `37.25` (`4.656/5`), `Method-A` = `34.00` (`4.250/5`), `Method-C` = `25.75` (`3.219/5`).
   - Citation audit (`phase2-results.json`, `overall_audit_summary`): `Method-B` `112` checked (`108` upheld, `4` downgraded, `0` vacated); `Method-A` `36` checked (`31` upheld, `5` downgraded, `0` vacated); `Method-C` `79` checked (`45` upheld, `33` downgraded, `1` vacated for attributing `skos:broaderGeneric`/`skos:broaderPartitive` to core W3C SKOS Reference rather than the ISO 25964 extension).
2. **Pre-registered two-area rebuild and anti-reward-hacking audit (Phases 3–5).** A corpus of `382` source units across `S1`–`S5` for `P4` and `Q4` was pre-registered on `2026-11-02` at commit `1b26c03611` (`taxonomy/method-study/preregistration.yaml`, `random_seed = 20261103`). Because `provenance_family_id` clusters vary in size and are never split across partitions, the `60% / 25% / 15%` target cluster proportions yielded `200` `dev` (`52.4%`), `126` `held_out_test` (`33.0%`), and `56` `reserve` (`14.7%`) units. Three isolated builders extracted `P4` and `Q4` candidates from `dev` only (`claude`: `66` `P4` + `32` `Q4` = `98` concepts, `75` relationships; `codex`: `54` `P4` + `24` `Q4` = `78` concepts, `112` relationships; `gemini`: `51` `P4` + `26` `Q4` = `77` concepts, `85` relationships; `phase4-manifest.json`) and were deterministically reconciled (`reconcile_phase4.py`) into `111` admitted concepts (`68` `P4`, `43` `Q4`; `63` multi-builder, `48` single-builder multi-stratum), `178` relationships, and `27` rejected candidates (`phase4-reconciled-p4-q4.json`, `summary_counts.rejected_candidates = 27`). The Phase 5 audit (`phase5-audit-report.json`) confirmed a matching deterministic rerun SHA-256 (`9468a3dfff0a3c19b433e9c38fa034c994d5fbb64da28bda2f9dd11b8be646ed`), `0` product/SKU leaks, `0` remaining `metric × workload` split flags (`inference-throughput` and `inference-energy-cost` rejected during reconciliation), `0` shallow padding definitions, `137` of `137` (`55` sampled concepts) verified quote citations, and `26` of `35` (`claude`: `26` upheld, `8` downgraded, `1` vacated) and `27` of `35` (`codex`: `27` upheld, `6` downgraded, `2` vacated) concepts upheld on a 35-concept stratified semantic audit (`proposal-admission-gate` and `assurance-evidence-record` vacated for extrapolating from bare topic headings). To preserve pre-registration integrity, `Vocab-X` (`phase4-reconciled-p4-q4.json`, `111` concepts) was evaluated unchanged in Phase 6 before folding the Phase 5/6 `fix_method` rules into the refresh workflow.
3. **Blind held-out comparison (Phase 6).** The `126` `held_out_test` units (`heldout/phase6-heldout-slice.json`) comprised `82` `COV` checklist units (`79` in-scope evaluated: `75` `P4` + `4` `Q4`, plus `3` `P4` items judged out-of-scope) and `44` scenario/question units (`43` multi-rater indexing items where both judges assigned $\ge 1$ concept, plus `1` item where one judge assigned no concept). Two blind evaluator families (`claude-opus-5-5`, `gpt-6-astra`) scored `Vocab-Y` (`published_0_4_draft` at commit `dda2fdb80a` prior to the Phase 7 alias fold, `125` concepts in `P4`+`Q4`: `95` `P4`, `30` `Q4`) and `Vocab-X` (`rebuilt_synthesized_method`, `111` concepts: `68` `P4`, `43` `Q4`) (`phase6-comparison-report.json`):

   | Metric (`P4` + `Q4` held-out slice) | `Vocab-Y` (`0.4-draft` at `dda2fdb80a`) | `Vocab-X` (single-pass `dev` rebuild) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Any-judge central-mechanism coverage (`79` units: `75` `P4`, `4` `Q4`) | `72.2%` | `36.7%` |
   | Two-judge consensus coverage (`79` units overall) | `36.7%` | `10.1%` |
   | `P4` consensus coverage (`75` units) / `Q4` consensus coverage (`4` units) | `38.7%` / `0.0%` | `10.7%` / `0.0%` |
   | Two-judge exact single-concept coverage (`79` units) | `19.0%` | `2.5%` |
   | Mean ordinal expressibility score (`79` units) | `42.4%` | `17.4%` |
   | Coverage judge binary $\kappa$ (`79` units) | `0.366` | `0.325` |
   | Seeded-gap detection rate | `60.0%` | `55.2%` |
   | Held-out subject exact agreement (`43` items) / subject $\kappa$ | `97.7%` (`42/43`) / `0.971` | `93.0%` (`40/43`) / `0.917` |
   | Hooper inter-indexer consistency (`43` items) | `81.8%` | `72.1%` |

   | Max single-concept share $p_{\max}$ (cap $\le 15.0\%$) | `9.84%` | `6.94%` |

   **Scope and limitations of the Phase 6 comparison:** `Vocab-Y` was constructed across multiple earlier review passes over corpora overlapping the general venue and curriculum source classes, whereas `Vocab-X` was built in a single pass from the `200` `dev` excerpts alone; no separately measured incremental-refresh treatment arm was run on top of `Vocab-Y`, and the `Q4` held-out coverage subset (`4` units) is too small to power a standalone `Q4` coverage comparison. Based on `Vocab-Y`'s higher coverage of multi-pass systems-venue mechanisms (`mlsys.attention-ffn-disaggregation`, `mlsys.expert-offloading`, `mlsys.masked-diffusion-decoding`, `mlsys.intra-device-overlap`, `mlsys.compound-workflow-pipelining`, `mlsys.fidelity-adaptive-serving`), the Editor decided to deploy the synthesized method as an **incremental refresh and validation pipeline anchored on `0.4-draft`** rather than replacing `0.4-draft` with a blank-slate rebuild.
4. **Extraction repeatability test on `Q4.1` and 3-organization final sign-off audit (Phase 7).** Two isolated builders (`claude-opus-5-5` and `gpt-6-astra`) were given the written method documents (`README.md`, `METHOD.md`, `taxonomy-refresh.md`), the `Q4.1` (`hazards-operating-domain`) subject scope, the neighboring `Q4` concept list, and the `23` `Q4` `dev` source units (`phase7-repeatability-r1.json`, `phase7-repeatability-test.json`). In Round 1 (`phase7-repeatability-r1.json`), `claude` matched `5 of 7` (`71.4%` recall, `83.3%` precision) and `codex` matched `3 of 7` (`42.9%` recall, `100.0%` precision) of the `7` published `Q4.1` concepts, with `3` shared concepts (`60.0%` Hooper, `75.0%` Rolling), exposing two ambiguities in the written rules: whether a syllabus/checklist topic heading can corroborate a prose source under Gate 2, and how single-lineage `dev` concepts that pass Gate 5 are recorded in `declared_gaps`. In the post-clarification development rerun on `Q4.1` (`phase7-repeatability-test.json`), each builder proposed `3` active concepts and `4` `declared_gaps` (`0` unmatched extraneous concepts):
   - **Active-only admission agreement (`concepts`):** Each builder admitted `3 of 7` gold concepts (`42.9%` active recall, `100.0%` active precision, `42.9%` active Hooper vs. gold, `60.0%` active Rolling vs. gold), sharing `2` active concepts (`mlsys.hazard-analysis`, `mlsys.operating-domain`; `50.0%` inter-builder active Hooper, `66.7%` active Rolling) while swapping `mlsys.tolerable-risk` (`claude` active, `codex` gap) and `mlsys.functional-insufficiency` (`codex` active, `claude` gap).
   - **Pooled inventory recovery (`concepts + declared_gaps`):** Across active concepts plus single-lineage `declared_gaps`, both builders recovered all `7 of 7` (`100.0%` pooled recall and precision, `7 of 7` pooled inter-builder agreement) of the published `Q4.1` concepts (`mlsys.fault-tree-analysis`, `mlsys.fmea`, `mlsys.functional-insufficiency`, `mlsys.hazard-analysis`, `mlsys.operating-domain`, `mlsys.stpa`, `mlsys.tolerable-risk`). Because Round 2 was run on `Q4.1` after clarifying the instructions on Round 1's output, it is recorded as a development calibration rerun rather than an uncontaminated held-out test.
   - **Three-organization sign-off audit (`final-3org-audit-report.json`):** A cold 3-organization audit panel (`claude-opus-5-5`, `gpt-6-astra`, `gemini-4-argon-high`) audited the Phase 7 branch, catching mechanical loopholes in `reconcile.py` (now hardened to check rejected `concepts_update` items, modified rejected edges, two-model reject drops, and alias collisions) and pruning `10` over-broad or non-equivalent candidate aliases down to `18` equivalence-verified aliases across `11` concepts (`1,272` entry terms in all) plus `1` cross-area `secondary_concepts` link (`mlsys.memory-planning` on `P4.9`).

## Consistency

Agreement between two independent model taggers on the 300-question StaffML samples (`taxonomy/agreement/*.yml`, recomputed by `binder concepts agreement`) and on the pre-registered `P4`+`Q4` held-out slice (`.claude/_reviews/taxonomy-method/20261102/phase6-comparison-report.json`):

| Sample | Subject κ | Hooper (both non-empty) | Rolling (both non-empty) | $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 StaffML questions, first rules (`280/300` both non-empty) | 0.781 | 65.7% | 74.6% | — |
| Same 300, rules revised on them (`286/300` both non-empty) | 0.832 | 74.7% | 81.0% | — |
| Held-out 300 StaffML questions (`282/300` both non-empty) | 0.802 | 67.3% | 74.9% | — |
| Held-out `P4`+`Q4` slice (`43/44` both non-empty, `0.4-draft`) | 0.971 | 81.8% | 87.6% | 0.724 |

Hooper's measure is $c / (A + B - c)$ and Rolling's is $2c / (A + B)$, where $c$ is the number of concepts both taggers assigned and $A$ and $B$ are the numbers each assigned. Krippendorff's $\alpha_{\text{MASI}}$ weights set agreement by monotonic set-intersection distance. On the 300-question StaffML samples, Hooper and Rolling are conditional per-item means over items where both taggers assigned at least one concept (`280`, `286`, and `282` of `300` respectively; items where only one tagger assigned a concept — `12`, `12`, and `11` — are excluded from those conditional means, while subject $\kappa$ uses all `300`). On the held-out `P4`+`Q4` slice, `43` of `44` scenario items were assigned concepts by both judges (with `1` item assigned a concept by only one judge), and max single-concept share was $p_{\max} = 9.84\% \le 15.0\%$. These numbers measure agreement between models on these samples and do not stand in for a trial with human indexers.
