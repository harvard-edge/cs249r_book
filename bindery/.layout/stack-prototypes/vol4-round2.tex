{\large\bfseries Volume IV, round 2 --- the two reviewer positions, built}\par\vspace{2pt}
{\footnotesize Both reviewers agreed on three things: kill the five-step tint ramp for a binary
one, drop the per-rung icons, and keep the rate line but subordinate it. They disagreed on
\emph{contiguity}.}\par\vspace{9mm}
\newcommand{\slot}[3]{\begin{minipage}[t][7.4cm][t]{3.9cm}#1\par\vspace{4mm}%
  {\footnotesize\bfseries #2}\par{\fontsize{7}{8.6}\selectfont #3}\end{minipage}}
\noindent
\slot{\dSix}{Round 1 · D6 as drawn}{Five-step ramp, accent spread across every band. Both
reviewers rejected this ramp: it makes the column read as a gradient, so the eye looks for a
continuum instead of a selection.}\hfill
\slot{\dSixR}{A · contiguous, corrected}{One flat neutral tint, accent used once. Contiguity
is the point: it reads as a single stacked object, which is what makes the device legible
across four volumes.}\hfill
\slot{\dSeven}{B · gapped, mark outside}{Contiguity asserts support-from-below, which vol1
earns and vol4 does not. Gapped rows say ordered set and stay silent about dependency. Double
gap marks the propose/command seam.}\hfill
\slot{\dSevenB}{B$'$ · seam ruled}{The same, with the authority seam also drawn. Costs one
mark the reader must decode fifteen times.}
\par\vspace{2mm}\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}\par\vspace{2mm}
{\footnotesize\textbf{The disagreement, stated plainly.} Reviewer A wants contiguity because a
flush column reads as one object and keeps vol4 in family with vol1 and vol2. Reviewer B says
contiguity is a claim: a flush stack means each tier \emph{rests on} the one beneath it, which
is true of vol1's dependency order and false of vol4's rate bands, and notes that vol3 already
ships gapped pills, so gapping is in family too. Both are right about their own premise. The
question is whether vol4's tiers support each other or merely follow each other.}
