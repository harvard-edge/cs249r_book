Attestation Before Recovery Admission

books/vol2/13_security_privacy/13_security_privacy.qmd
vol2:fig-attestation-key-release

Open full-resolution image · Read in the rebuilt chapter

Chapter lead-in

The recovery sequence in figure 15 places key delivery between fresh appraisal and checkpoint restoration.

Published caption

Figure 15: Attestation Before Recovery Admission: A restarted node answers a fresh verifier challenge with authenticated measurements. Under the illustrated admission policy, the verifier authorizes the key service only after appraisal succeeds. Protected key delivery permits checkpoint restoration; the node rejoins the collective afterward. Stale or rejected evidence stops this sequence before authorization. The diagram shows ordering rather than elapsed time and assumes the platform protects key delivery and custody.

Following explanation

Attestation and key management add coordination work when admission or recovery requires renewed evidence and key delivery. The policy must specify when evidence expires and which changes require another check. When these operations delay participants or restart, their cost enters \(\SynchronizationTime(\DeviceCount)\) and the recovery path. A node that restarts after a failure must present fresh evidence and receive the checkpoint key again before it rejoins, so every failure costs that re-admission delay on top of the restart time and lost work that the checkpoint-interval calculation already charges.
