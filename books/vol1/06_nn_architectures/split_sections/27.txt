## Transformers: Parallel Sequence Processing {#sec-network-architectures-transformers-attentiononly-architecture-1b56}

Attention provides the computational primitive of dynamic, content-dependent routing between positions, yet it was originally layered on top of recurrent architectures, inheriting their sequential bottleneck. The **transformer architecture**\index{Transformer!definition} removes recurrence by combining attention with feed-forward layers, residual connections, normalization, and positional information. This enables parallel computation across sequence positions during full-sequence processing while retaining dynamic connectivity. This architectural shift introduces distinct physical costs: quadratic dense-score computation during prefill, accumulating key-value state during serving, and severe memory-bandwidth pressure during autoregressive generation.

::: {#dfn-nn-architectures-transformers .callout-definition title="Transformers"}

***Transformers***\index{Transformer!definition} are neural network architectures that combine self-attention, feed-forward layers, residual connections, normalization, and positional information without recurrence, enabling parallel processing across sequence positions during training and prefill.

1.  **Significance**: Parallelism is the systems payoff. An RNN processing an $\SequenceLength$-token sequence executes $\SequenceLength$ sequentially dependent steps. A transformer processes all positions of a sequence concurrently, mapping token interactions to large matrix multiplications (GEMMs) that maximize accelerator compute utilization. Dense self-attention still computes $\mathcal{O}(\SequenceLength^2)$ pairwise score interactions (@sec-network-architectures-memory-requirements-72c3).
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike attention used inside a recurrent backbone, which inherits the host architecture's $\mathcal{O}(\SequenceLength)$ sequential path, transformers use attention as the primary mixing operation between positions, alternating it with position-wise feed-forward networks.
3.  **Common pitfall**: A frequent misconception is that transformers have "infinite context." Two distinct memory footprints matter: the $\mathcal{O}(\SequenceLength^2)$ attention-score activation matrix\index{Attention matrix} when materialized during training and prefill, and the $\mathcal{O}(\SequenceLength)$ key-value (KV) cache that accumulates during autoregressive serving. Exact tiled attention avoids the full score matrix while retaining quadratic dense arithmetic. At long sequence lengths, the KV cache footprint can rival or exceed the static model weights, turning KV cache management and memory-bandwidth efficiency into dominant engineering constraints.

:::
