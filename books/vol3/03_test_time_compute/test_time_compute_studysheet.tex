\begin{bigidea}
More tokens, candidates or checks at decision time can raise the \hl{chance} of an
accepted answer; it never makes one correct. The runtime spends it as a \hl{budget with a stopping rule}, deciding how far
a pass is trusted and when to stop. Every round lengthens the horizon.
\end{bigidea}

\begin{leftcol}
\sheetsec{Key ideas}
\begin{ideas}
\idea{Three axes, one budget.}{Depth lengthens one candidate, breadth samples several, feedback revises after a
  check. Only a check adds a fact.}
\idea{Coverage is not selection.}{Votes, scores and visible tests rank. Return only on a pass from a sealed suite
  (one it cannot read or modify); else abstain.}
\idea{A pass inherits the base rate.}{Rare valid candidates plus small false acceptance fill the passes
  with broken ones; pool size does not change that share.}
\idea{Search exploits its check.}{The top noisy score carries positive error, and a larger pool more likely holds a
  loophole. Measure accuracy on selected candidates.}
\idea{Revise along recorded dependencies.}{A refuted premise invalidates only its downstream artifacts; a missing
  edge keeps stale ones alive.}
\idea{Budgets end searches, not uncertainty.}{Stop in order: hard ceiling, a pass, then calibrated gain per dollar below
  threshold. No pass: abstain or escalate.}
\end{ideas}

\sheetsec{Three axes under one output ceiling}
\begin{sheettable}{@{}l L L L@{}}

\fcard{Precision of a pass}%
  {\VerifierPrecision = \frac{\ValidCandidateRate(1-\VerifierFalseRejectionRate)}{\ValidCandidateRate(1-\VerifierFalseRejectionRate) + (1-\ValidCandidateRate)\VerifierFalseAcceptanceRate}}%
  {Valid share of passes, from valid rate $\ValidCandidateRate$, false rejection $\VerifierFalseRejectionRate$ and
   false acceptance $\VerifierFalseAcceptanceRate$. The denominator is the pass rate $\VerifierPassProbability$.}%
  {A pool selects with $1-(1-\VerifierPassProbability)^{\CandidatePoolSize}$; its first pass is valid
   with $\VerifierPrecision$.}{}
\fcard{Effective branching}%
  {\EffectiveBranchingFactor = \SearchBranchingFactor\,(1-\ChildPruningProbability)}%
  {Kept nodes propose $\SearchBranchingFactor$ children; a step check rejects each with
   $\ChildPruningProbability$. Nodes generated to depth $\SearchDepth$:
   $\SearchBranchingFactor(1+\EffectiveBranchingFactor+\dots+\EffectiveBranchingFactor^{\SearchDepth-1})$.}%
  {No pruning: $\EffectiveBranchingFactor = \SearchBranchingFactor$. Below one ($\ChildPruningProbability > 1-1/\SearchBranchingFactor$)
   the tree dies out; above one it grows, so ceilings must stop it.}{}

\begin{answers}
\ans{Finish}{Halved false acceptance: $\val{validpass} \div \val{halfpassprob}$ = \val{halfprecision}\%. Doubled valid rate:
  $\val{doublevalidpass} \div \val{doublepassprob}$ = \val{doubleprecision}\%. Two knobs of similar strength: buy the
  cheaper; a bigger pool moves neither (precision card).}
\ans{1}{Single: $\val{qaspend} \div \val{qapassfrac}$ = \$\val{qaeff}, but \val{qapass}\% misses the target. Breadth:
  $\val{qbspend} \div \val{qbpassfrac}$ = \$\val{qbeff}. Feedback: $\val{qcspend} \div \val{qcpassfrac}$ = \$\val{qceff},
  and its two rounds end by \val{qmaxsec}~s. Choose feedback, \val{qbovercs}$\times$ cheaper than breadth; \val{qcabstain}\% of
  tasks end unaccepted, still counted (cost line).}
\columnbreak
\ans{2}{The complement of $\val{qmissfrac}^{\val{qpool}}$: \val{qpoolpass}\%. Mixed: half the tasks reach the complement of
  $\val{qeasyfrac}^{\val{qpool}}$, half stay at zero: \val{qtrue}\% (pool card).}
\ans{3}{(a) $\EffectiveBranchingFactor$ = \val{qkids} $\times$ \val{qkeepfrac} = \val{qeff}, above one: the expected frontier grows
  (dying out needs over \val{qcritical}\% rejection). Expected nodes \val{qlevelone} + \val{qleveltwo} + \val{qlevelthree} =
  \val{qnodes}, against \val{qfull} unpruned. Ceilings must stop it (branching card). (b) Regenerate A and B; keep C. Without the
  A$\to$B edge, B survives stale (dependencies key idea).}
\ans{4}{The ceiling: no new work. Nothing passed the sealed suite: report unresolved or escalate. The score
  and visible tests only rank (budgets key idea).}
\end{answers}

\sheetsec{Worked example}
\begin{practicebox}\boxtitle{How far can a passing patch be trusted?}
A policy generates \val{pool} candidates and returns the first that passes, in fixed order. \val{validrate}\% are
valid; the check rejects \val{falsereject}\% of valid ones and passes \val{falseaccept}\% of broken ones.\par\vspace{5pt}
{\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[sheet]
  \pgfmathsetmacro\u{40}
  \node[lab, anchor=east, align=right] at (56,7) {expected passes\\in \val{pool} draws};
  \draw[hotbox] (60,0) rectangle ({60+\val{validcount}*\u},14);
  \draw[nbox] ({60+\val{validcount}*\u},0) rectangle ({60+\val{passcount}*\u},14);
  \node[note, anchor=north] at ({60+\val{validcount}*\u/2},-3) {\val{validcount} valid};
  \node[note, anchor=north] at ({60+(\val{validcount}+\val{passcount})*\u/2},-3) {\val{invalidcount} broken};
  \node[note, anchor=west] at ({64+\val{passcount}*\u},7) {\val{passcount}};
\end{tikzpicture}\par}\vspace{6pt}
\begin{steps}
\item Pass rate: $\val{validfrac} \times \val{frkeep} + \val{validmiss} \times \val{fafrac} = \val{validpass} + \val{invalidpass}$
  $=$ \res{$\VerifierPassProbability = \val{passprob}$}.
\item Precision: $\val{validpass} \div \val{passprob}$ $=$ \res{\val{precision}\%}; \val{defect}\% of passes are broken.
\item Any selection: $1-(1-\val{passprob})^{\val{pool}}$ $=$ \val{anyselect}\%. Defective return:
  $\val{anyselectfrac} \times \val{defectfrac}$ $=$ \res{\val{wrongreturn}\% of tasks}.
\end{steps}
\takeaway{The pool almost always finds a pass; nearly a third of returns are broken.}
\end{practicebox}

\sheetsec{Common mistakes}
\mistake{``Let it reason longer; more tokens give a better diagnosis.''}%
  {More tokens re-examine the prompt; only a check adds a fact. \val{answertokens} tokens at \val{tokenms} ms take \val{answersec} s, \val{proberatio}$\times$
   the \val{probemillis} ms test that observes whether the waiter wakes.}{}
\mistake{``The highest-scoring candidate has done the task.''}%
  {With equal true quality, the top of \val{argpool} noisy scores carries about \val{argshift} standard deviations of error,
   and a loophole in \val{exploitrate}\% of samples is in \val{exploitpresent}\% of pools. A score ranks; it does not accept.}{}

\begin{qs}
\item Under a \val{deadlinesec} s deadline, three repair policies spend per attempt: single call \$\val{qaspend},
  accepted \val{qapass}\%; \val{breadthcount} in parallel \$\val{qbspend}, \val{qbpass}\%; feedback (up to two \val{roundsec} s

  per accepted repair, and what happens to the rest?
\item \assumed{} one task passes at \val{qpass}\% per candidate. (a) pass@$\CandidatePoolSize$ for a pool of \val{qpool}?
  (b) Mixed workload: half the tasks pass at \val{qeasy}\%, half never. What does the pool deliver?
\item (a) \assumed{} each node proposes \val{qkids} children; a step check rejects \val{qreject}\%. Does the tree die out on
  its own? Expected nodes to depth \val{qdepth}, against no pruning?
  (b) A test refutes premise P. A was built from P, B from A, C independently. What is regenerated,
  and what if the A$\to$B edge went unrecorded?
\item A search reaches its token ceiling. No candidate passes the sealed suite; the best scores \val{qscore} on a
  learned scorer and passes the visible tests. Which rule decides, and what does the runtime return?
\end{qs}
