  \mlsyscredit{Hardware Acceleration, \S11.7.5 (the 2$\times$2 trace) and figure 5 (interactive Explore figure: Systolic Array Dataflow); Kung 1982. $C_{10}$ and $C_{11}$ follow the same stagger.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{3.5 min}{Core}%
    Walk through the 2 by 2 trace computing $C = AB$ ($C_{00} = a_{00}b_{00} + a_{01}b_{10}$), one hop per cycle. First, each processing element (PE) holds one weight while activations move right and partial sums move down. Second, in cycle 0, $a_{00}$ meets $b_{00}$ in $\text{PE}_{00}$, and its product shifts down to $\text{PE}_{10}$ (holding $b_{10}$) in cycle 1. Third, reveal $a_{01}$ entering row 1 one cycle late so the matching reduction index meets in $\text{PE}_{10}$, emitting $C_{00}$ while $a_{00}$ moves right and $a_{10}$ enters $\text{PE}_{00}$. Fourth and fifth, $C_{01}$ and $C_{10}$ exit in cycle 2 and $C_{11}$ in cycle 3: two input rows take four cycles.

    \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%

\begin{frame}{Open questions}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35cm}
    \item Hand-written kernels still lead on new hardware for months. Should the next accelerator be designed so a compiler can map it well, even at some cost in peak rate?
    \item Model designers already choose widths of 512 over 500. Who should fit shapes to tiles and waves: the model team or the compiler?
    \item A fixed dataflow is efficient yet inflexible, whereas a GPU relies on software to find reuse; which wins as models change faster than chips?
  \end{itemize}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{1 min}{Core}%
    Raise three open engineering questions for discussion or brief written response: trading peak hardware rate for compiler-friendly regularity (connecting to Ansor and Definition 11.4), dividing shape-alignment responsibility between model teams and compilers, and weighing fixed TPU-style dataflows against flexible GPU software mapping.
