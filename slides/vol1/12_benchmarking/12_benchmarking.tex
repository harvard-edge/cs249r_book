\begin{frame}{One server's p99 is 10 ms; the whole request's is 140 ms}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.95\textwidth,height=5.9cm,keepaspectratio]{tail_step}{3}
  \mlsyscredit{Measured: Dean and Barroso, ``The Tail at Scale,'' CACM 2013, Table 1 (a Google service). Lower panel: Benchmarking, \S12.10 (illustrative).}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{3.5 min}{Core}%
    Define median (p50, beaten by half of requests) and p99 (met or beaten by 99 percent). First, Dean and Barroso (2013, Table 1) measured one leaf server in a Google service at 1 ms median and 10 ms p99. Second, when the root fans each request across many leaves and waits for all of them, median jumps to 40 ms and p99 to 140 ms (14x the single-leaf p99) because touching 100 leaves with a 1 percent slow rate hits at least one slow leaf roughly 63 percent of the time. Third, in the chapter's illustrative case, evenly spaced model-only tests report a 15 ms mean while production p99 hits 150 to 200 ms, completely hiding misses against a 33 ms camera deadline. Distinguish a service-level objective (SLO, target on a metric like p99) from an SLA (contract with penalties); generative services split tails into TTFT and TPOT, and Lecture 13 models how queues build them.

    \notecue{Watch for}{Students often dismiss p99 as a rare edge case; across one million requests, p99 affects 10,000 users.}

    \notecue{Next}{Have students compute the 100-shard tail amplification formula by hand.}
  }
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A closed-loop client erases the stall it should measure}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.92\textwidth,height=5.15cm,keepaspectratio]{loop_step}{3}
  \mlsyscredit{Benchmarking, \S12.2.4 (interactive Explore figure: Coordinated Omission).}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
    Demonstrate coordinated omission across three steps. First, a closed-loop client (any naive ``send, wait, send'' loop) sends the next request only after receiving a reply; when the server freezes for 500 ms (for instance during garbage collection), the client pauses too and logs just one slow request. Second, an open-loop client sends on an independent schedule like real users (MLPerf Server uses Poisson arrivals), so requests queue behind the stall and record 14 slow responses. Third, highlight the error: the closed-loop harness hid 13 of 14 tail events (Lecture 13 models the resulting queues).

    \notecue{Next}{Catch a second measurement trap inside Python GPU benchmarking code: asynchronous kernel launches.}
  }
\end{frame}
