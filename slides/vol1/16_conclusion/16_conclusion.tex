\mlsysnote{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{0.5 min}{Core}%
  Section 1 traced constraint propagation case by case across five lighthouses. Engineering an unfamiliar system requires general rules, so Section 2 gathers the thirteen principles from the book's four parts around conservation of complexity, the heuristic that simplifying one layer often shifts burden to another. The principles fall into five epistemic kinds, meaning the types of statements they make. Bounds are unbeatable physical limits, decompositions are additive sums, fitted models hold only inside their fitted regime, policies are product-chosen targets, and heuristics are predictions that bounds or measurements must verify. On each part slide, name the principle shown by the evidence before checking its label.
