\begin{frame}{DP-SGD clips each example's gradient, then adds noise}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.94\textwidth,height=4.6cm,keepaspectratio]{dpsgd_mech_step}{4}
  \mlsyscredit{Responsible Engineering, \S15.5.2, after Abadi et al., ``Deep Learning with Differential Privacy,'' CCS 2016.}
  \note{[2.5 min] Differential privacy bounds what any one record can change about what a model outputs; epsilon is the size of that bound, and smaller is more private. Its training method, DP-SGD, changes one step. Step 1: compute each example's gradient separately, not the batch average. Step 2: clip each to a maximum norm C, so no single example can pull the weights far. Step 3: add the clipped gradients and add Gaussian noise with standard deviation sigma times C, which hides any one example's contribution; then update. Step 4: a privacy accountant adds up the privacy spent at every step; training stops when the chosen epsilon is used up. ENGAGE: why must clipping come before the noise? Expected: the noise is scaled to C; without a bound on each example's influence, no finite noise hides it. WARN: differential privacy bounds influence under its stated assumptions; it is not encryption and does not stop access. The formal definition is in the chapter's footnote and in Dwork and Roth. FLEX: [CORE].}
\end{frame}
