\mlsysnote{[1 min] The segment's law, the chapter's equations 4 and 5. Training throughput is bounded by the smaller of two rates: what the accelerator can compute, and what the input path can supply. The supply rate is storage bandwidth, less overhead, divided by the bytes each sample costs. Once supply is the smaller rate, a faster accelerator buys nothing; the two levers are more bandwidth or fewer bytes per sample. The next segment is about the second lever. LINK: this is the data term of the iron law from Lecture 1, in its overlapped form, where a pipeline runs at the rate of its slowest stage. FLEX: [CORE].}

\begin{frame}{Drift is a change in $p(x)$, $p(y)$, or $p(y \mid x)$}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.3cm}
    \item Moving a petabyte costs about a day and \$\val{egress_usd}, so large datasets pull the computation to them.
    \item Training runs at the slower of compute and data supply.
    \item Bytes per sample is a lever as strong as bandwidth; which wins depends on the binding stage.
    \item Drift statistics flag; controlled comparisons against a release diagnose.
  \end{itemize}
