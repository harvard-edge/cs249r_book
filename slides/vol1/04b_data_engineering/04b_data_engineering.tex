\mlsysnote{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{1 min}{Core}%
  Equations 4 and 5 express Lecture 1's overlapped iron law for input pipelines: training throughput cannot exceed the minimum of accelerator compute rate $R_{\text{compute}}$ and data supply rate $BW_{\text{storage}}(1 - \text{overhead}) / (\text{bytes per sample})$. Whenever data supply is smaller, buying a faster GPU yields zero speedup; engineers must either increase effective storage bandwidth or reduce bytes per sample (Segment 3).

  \notecue{Next}{Test what happens when a team pre-decodes JPEGs to spare the host CPU on a storage-bound pipeline.}
}

\begin{frame}{PSI adds every bin that moved, 0.135 here}
  \centering
  {\small $\text{PSI} = \sum_{\text{bins}} \big(\text{recent} - \text{training}\big)\,\ln\dfrac{\text{recent}}{\text{training}}$}\\[0.1cm]
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.84\textwidth,height=4.7cm,keepaspectratio]{psi_bins_step}{3}
  \mlsyscredit{Data Engineering, \S4.12.1 (formula and bands). Bin shares are illustrative.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%
    PSI bins training loudness into equal 20\% quintiles (blue). Step 1 overlays the quieter recent batch (red: 30, 25, 20, 15, and 10\% across bins). Step 2 weights each bin's share change by the log ratio so every shifted bin contributes positively: $0.10 \ln(1.5) = 0.041$ in the quietest bin and $0.10 \ln(2) = 0.069$ in the loudest. Step 3 sums to 0.135. Practitioner heuristics treat under 0.1 as stable, 0.1 to 0.25 as moderate, and above 0.25 as requiring action; related KL divergence is asymmetric and diverges on empty baseline bins, so monitors fix direction and smooth.

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.3cm}
    \item Moving a petabyte costs about a day and \$20,000, so large datasets pull the computation to them.
    \item Training runs at the slower of compute and data supply.
    \item Bytes per sample is a lever as strong as bandwidth; which wins depends on the limiting stage.
    \item Drift statistics flag; controlled comparisons against a release diagnose.
  \end{itemize}
