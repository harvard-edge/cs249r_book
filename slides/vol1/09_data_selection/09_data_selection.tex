\begin{frame}{MinHash finds near-duplicates without comparing every pair}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=\textwidth,height=5.6cm,keepaspectratio]{minhash_step}{4}
  \mlsyscredit{Data Selection, \S9.3.2; method: Broder 1997. Page text: the C4 sentence Lee et al., ACL 2022 (arXiv:2107.06499, footnote 1) found \val{lee_repeats} times. Hash values illustrative.}
  \note{[1.5 min] Step 1: a real page. Lee et al. found this \val{lee_words}-word sentence, which begins ``by combining fantastic ideas, interesting arrangements,'' repeated \val{lee_repeats} times verbatim in C4, a large web corpus. Its shingles are the overlapping runs of three words; two pages that share most shingles are near-duplicates. Step 2: hash every shingle and keep the minimum. The chance that two pages share that minimum equals the fraction of shingles they share (their Jaccard similarity). With \val{minhash_hashes} hash functions the page becomes a signature of \val{minhash_hashes} numbers. Step 3: cut the signature into bands and hash each band into a bucket. Step 4: compare only pages that share a bucket. WebText holds roughly \val{webtext_docs_m} million pages. All pairs would be $\val{all_pairs_m}\times10^{\val{all_pairs_e}}$ comparisons; MinHash needs one \val{webtext_gb} GB read, about \val{read_s} seconds, and \val{sig_gb} GB of signatures in host memory. WARN: hashing runs on host CPUs while the accelerator idles. FLEX: [CORE]; steps 2 and 3 can be summarized in one sentence.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25cm}
    \item An example is worth the learning it returns per FLOP.
    \item Selection pays only if scoring plus subset training beats full training, at equal quality.
    \item Deduplicate first; rank the rest with a cheap proxy; keep a coverage floor.
    \item Label where the model is unsure, and compare against random labeling at the same budget.
    \item A FLOP saved becomes time saved only if storage and the host keep up.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{[1 min] One line per segment. Ask students to star the one they would have gotten wrong at the start of the lecture, and to write the selection inequality from memory at the top of their notes. FLEX: [CORE].}
\end{frame}
