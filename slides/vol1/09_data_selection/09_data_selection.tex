\begin{frame}{Learning curves give the verdict a one-epoch ratio cannot}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.88\textwidth,height=5.0cm,keepaspectratio]{ppd_curve_step}{2}\\[0.05cm]
  \uncover<2->{\normalsize In the chapter's model, doubling the data halves what the next FLOP teaches.}

  \note{[2 min] Step 1: two learning curves, accuracy against the share of the dataset used. \section{Pruning}

\begin{frame}{MinHash finds near-duplicates without comparing every pair}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=\textwidth,height=5.6cm,keepaspectratio]{minhash_step}{4}
  \mlsyscredit{Data Selection, \S9.3.2; method: Broder 1997. Page text and hash values are illustrative.}
  \note{[2 min] Step 1: a web page, and its shingles, the overlapping runs of three words. Two pages that share most shingles are near-duplicates. Step 2: hash every shingle with one hash function and keep the minimum. The chance that two pages share that minimum equals the fraction of shingles they share (their Jaccard similarity). Repeat with 128 hash functions and the page becomes a signature of 128 numbers. Step 3: cut the signature into bands and hash each band into a bucket. Similar pages land in the same bucket for at least one band. Step 4: compare only pages that share a bucket. WebText holds roughly 8 million documents, each a web page. All pairs would be $3.2\times10^{13}$ comparisons (8 million squared, halved); MinHash needs one 40 GB read, about 6 seconds, and 4.1 GB of signatures in host memory. WARN: hashing runs on host CPUs, not on the accelerator. FLEX: [OPTIONAL] steps 2 and 3 can be summarized in one sentence.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Deduplication returns 3 to 14 hours of GPT-2's run}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.92\textwidth,height=4.0cm,keepaspectratio]{dedup_hours_step}{2}\\[0.25cm]
  \uncover<2->{\normalsize Cost: one 6-second read and 4.1 GB of signatures.}
  \mlsyscredit{Duplicate shares: Lee et al., ACL 2022. Applied to GPT-2's run: Data Selection, \S9.3.2.}
  \note{[2 min] Real measurements: Lee et al. found that near-duplicates made up 3.04 percent of the C4 web corpus and 13.63 percent of RealNews. Step 1: if WebText carried the C4 share, removing it saves about 3 hours and 18 kWh of the 4.4-day, 590 kWh run. Step 2: at the RealNews share, about 14 hours and 80 kWh. The cost is a 6-second read and host hashing. Lee et al. also found that deduplicated training reduced memorized text and kept or improved perplexity (how well the model predicts held-out text). ENGAGE: why deduplicate before any other selection? Expected: it is the cheapest and it never removes information. \end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A linear curriculum warmup serves about half its samples}
  \centering
  \safeimg[width=0.88\textwidth,height=5.0cm,keepaspectratio]{warmup.pdf}\\[0.05cm]
  {\normalsize A claimed saving must say: cheaper warmup epochs, or fewer epochs?}
  \mlsyscredit{Data Selection, \S9.4.1, linear pacing. W = 10 is illustrative.}
  \note{[1.5 min] Curriculum learning orders the stream instead of shuffling it: easy examples first, harder ones as the model settles. Two parts: a difficulty scorer that ranks samples, and a pacing rule. With linear pacing, epoch $j$ of a $W$-epoch warmup trains on the easiest $j/W$ of the $D$ samples. Summed over the warmup, the loader serves $D(W+1)/2$ samples in place of $W\cdot D$; at $W = 10$, 5.5 $D$ instead of 10 $D$, so the warmup costs about half its full-data FLOPs. WARN: that saving is fixed by the pacing rule before any benchmark runs. Any further saving must come from reaching the target in fewer epochs, which depends on the scorer, model, and data. A claim must say which of the two it measured. The chapter's illustrative case reaches target in 115 epochs against 150, which it treats as an assumption. FLEX: [OPTIONAL] mention that loaders pre-sort data into difficulty-tiered shards, since re-sorting every epoch breaks sequential reads.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25cm}
    \item An example is worth the learning it returns per FLOP.
    \item Selection pays only if scoring plus subset training beats full training, at equal quality.
    \item Deduplicate first; rank the rest with a cheap proxy; keep a coverage floor.
    \item Label where the model is unsure, and compare against random labeling at the same budget.
    \item A FLOP saved becomes time saved only if storage and the host keep up.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{[1 min] One line per segment. Ask students to star the one they would have gotten wrong at the start of the lecture, and to write the selection inequality from memory at the top of their notes.}
\end{frame}
