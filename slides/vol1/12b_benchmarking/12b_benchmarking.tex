  \mlsysactivity{Estimate}{2.5 min}

  {\large $n = \dfrac{z^2\, p\,(1-p)}{m^2}$}\quad
  {\footnotesize $p$: fraction that passes;\; $m$: margin;\; $z$: 1.96 at 95\%, 2.576 at 99\%}\\[0.15cm]

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\\[0.15cm]
  \uncover<4->{\safeimg[width=0.85\textwidth,height=1.95cm,keepaspectratio]{sizing.pdf}}
  \mlsyscredit{Benchmarking, Napkin Math 12.5 (1,825 images). MLPerf Inference rules, minimum query counts (mlcommons/inference\_policies). $n$ derived.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%
    Show that accuracy and latency percentiles share one binomial sizing law, $n = z^2 p(1-p)/m^2$, where $p$ is pass fraction, $m$ is rank margin, and $z$ is the confidence multiplier (1.96 at 95 percent, 2.576 at 99 percent). \begin{frame}{The more accurate network was three times worse calibrated}

Second, apply temperature scaling: divide all logits by one positive constant fitted on held-out validation data, cutting ImageNet ResNet-152 ECE from 5.48 to 1.86 percent. Because dividing every score by one positive scalar preserves argmax, temperature scaling fixes confidence without altering top-1 accuracy or recovering misclassified nighttime species.

    \notecue{Optional}{For a 50-minute class, skip Guo et al. and move straight to real unseen camera-trap shifts in WILDS.}
