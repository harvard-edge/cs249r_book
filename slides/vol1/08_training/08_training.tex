\section{Time}

\begin{frame}{A training token costs $6P$ FLOPs; an inference token, $2P$}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.92\textwidth,height=4.9cm,keepaspectratio]{flops_per_token_step}{2}\\[0.35cm]
  \uncover<2->{\normalsize Backward: one product for the activation gradient, one for the weight gradient.}
  \mlsyscredit{Model Training, Lighthouse 8.1.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
    Let $P$ denote the parameter count. First, in the forward pass each weight participates in one multiply-add (2 FLOPs) per token, giving $2P$ FLOPs, which is all inference pays. Second, the backward pass executes two matrix products per weight (derived in Lecture 5): one propagates error to the previous layer (activation gradient, $2P$) and one computes the parameter update signal (weight gradient, $2P$), adding $4P$ for $6P$ per training token. Over $D$ tokens, total work is $O \approx 6PD$ ($1.5 \times 10^{21}$ FLOPs for GPT-2 XL).

    \notecue{Watch for}{The $6P$ rule omits quadratic attention-score products; for GPT-2 XL at 1,024 tokens those add roughly 9 percent (320.8 versus 295 TFLOP per step in the chapter's per-layer count).}

    \notecue{Next}{Combine $6PD$ with hardware peak rate and utilization into the training-time law.}
  }
\end{frame}

\mlsysnote{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
  The training-time law specializes Lecture 1's iron law to an entire training run: $T_{\text{train}} = 6PD / (R_{\text{peak}} \cdot \eta_{\text{hw}})$. The numerator $6PD$ is total algorithmic work over data, while the denominator is achieved hardware rate: peak rate at the chosen format times utilization $\eta_{\text{hw}}$ (eroded by stalls, data waits, synchronization, and slow kernels). Three levers shorten training: less work, higher peak, or higher utilization. Emphasize the bottom line: memory never appears in the equation because an overflowing step runs at zero speed; capacity is a precondition checked first.

  \notecue{Next}{Apply the training-time law to one GPT-2 XL step before scaling up to the full run.}
}
\mlsysfocus{The Training-Time Law}{%
  $\displaystyle T_{\text{train}} = \frac{\overbrace{6PD}^{\text{work, } O}}{\underbrace{R_{\text{peak}}}_{\text{peak rate}} \times \underbrace{\eta_{\text{hw}}}_{\text{utilization}}}$\\[0.6cm]
  {\small\textcolor{white!75}{Memory is not in the law. It decides whether the step runs at all.}}%
}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25cm}
    \item Run time is $6PD$ over peak times utilization; utilization moves time and energy together.
    \item Mixed-precision Adam holds 16 bytes per parameter before any activation.
    \item A step holds weights, gradients, optimizer state, and activations; only activations grow with batch and sequence.
    \item Busy time bounds MFU; profile before fixing, and fit before profiling.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{1 min}{Core}%
    Each takeaway corresponds to one segment plus the profiling sequence: run time equals $6PD / (R_{\text{peak}} \cdot \eta_{\text{hw}})$ moving time and energy together; mixed-precision Adam holds 16 B/param statically; only saved activations scale with batch and sequence length; and engineers fit first, then profile. Ask students to star the single line they would have missed before today.
