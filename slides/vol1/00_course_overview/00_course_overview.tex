  \mlsyscredit{Introduction, \S1.7, after Horowitz 2014 (45 nm process, DDR3 DRAM; per byte read and per operation).}

\end{frame}

\mlsysnote{[3 min] [CORE] The iron law as the book writes it (Introduction, equation 2): time is samples per task, times FLOPs per sample, times seconds per FLOP, one factor per D·A·M axis. Samples are the units of work: images, examples, or tokens. The units cancel to seconds. Point to the line beneath the equation: the GPT-3 estimate from earlier was this law with its factors grouped. The first two factors multiply to the total work, 3.14 times 10 to the 23 FLOPs. Seconds per FLOP is one over the rate the cluster achieves, 1,024 GPUs times 312 TFLOP/s times 0.45, about 7 times 10 to the minus 18 seconds. The product is about 2.2 million seconds, about 25 days. Seconds per FLOP is the realized time per operation, not the datasheet peak, so it already includes memory stalls and idle time; that is why the decode example earlier still fits. Lecture 1 unpacks the machine factor into memory, compute, and overhead terms. For students who know computer architecture: it has the same form as the CPU iron law, instructions per program times cycles per instruction times seconds per cycle.}
\mlsysfocus{The Iron Law of ML Systems}{%
  $\displaystyle T = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Samples}}{\text{Task}}}_{\text{Data}} \times
   \underbrace{\frac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{Sample}}}_{\text{Algorithm}} \times
   \underbrace{\frac{\text{Seconds}}{\text{FLOP}}}_{\text{Machine}}$\\[0.6cm]
  {\small\textcolor{white!75}{GPT-3 on 1,024 GPUs, from the earlier estimate:}}\\[0.25cm]
  {\small\textcolor{white!75}{$\underbrace{3.14\times10^{23}\ \text{FLOPs}}_{\text{Samples} \times \text{FLOPs/Sample}} \times \underbrace{7\times10^{-18}\ \text{s/FLOP}}_{1/(1{,}024 \times 312\times10^{12} \times 0.45)} \approx 2.2\times10^{6}\ \text{s} \approx 25\ \text{days}$}}%
}
