\begin{frame}{Waiting time explodes as utilization nears 100 percent}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \begin{column}{0.72\textwidth}

    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.26\textwidth}
      \uncover<5->{\normalsize Mean\\[0.1cm] $W = \dfrac{S}{1-\rho}$\\[0.4cm] p99\\[0.1cm] $W_{99} \approx \dfrac{\val{p99_factor}\,S}{1-\rho}$\\[0.4cm]
      {\footnotesize $S$: service time\\ $\rho$: utilization}}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \note{[4 min] [CORE] Utilization, rho, is the fraction of time the GPU is busy: arrival rate divided by the rate it can serve. S is the service time, \val{b1_ms} ms. M/M/1 means random arrivals, variable service times, one server. Step 1: at \val{util_50} percent busy, the mean time in system is \val{knee_50} ms, twice the service time. The two laws: at \val{util_70} percent busy, the mean is \val{knee_70} ms but p99 is \val{knee_p99_ex} ms. ENGAGE: a mean of \val{knee_mean_ex_0} ms against a \val{p99_budget_ms} ms deadline: safe? \end{frame}

Step 1: read the weights (\val{cs_ssd} s from a local SSD, \val{cs_s3_lo} to \val{cs_s3_hi} s from cloud storage such as S3), create the CUDA context (the driver's per-process state on the GPU, \val{cs_cuda_lo} to \val{cs_cuda_hi} s), then warm up: run a few dummy inferences so lazy initialization happens before a user arrives. Totals: about \val{cs_warm} s with a precompiled engine on a warm container, about \val{cs_cold} s from scratch. Step 3: measured loading of large language models with PyTorch: OPT-30B onto \val{opt_gpus} GPUs in \val{opt_s} s, Llama 2 70B onto \val{llama_gpus} GPUs in \val{llama_s} s; downloading a \val{sllm_gb} GB checkpoint at \val{sllm_gbps} GB/s takes at least \val{sllm_dl} s before loading starts. WARN: the fallacy is that cold start matters only for the first request. It strikes during spikes, deployments, and recovery, exactly when capacity is short. Step 4: the file format matters too. A \val{ckpt_gb} GB checkpoint takes \val{pickle_s} s through Python's pickle, which rebuilds every object, and \val{safetensors_s} s through memory-mapped safetensors (Example 13.10). FLEX: [CORE].}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Open questions}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35cm}
    \item A provider could offer a slower, cheaper tier that waits to batch. Who should decide how much latency a request is worth?
    \item When a spike exceeds capacity, should a service reject what it cannot finish in time, serve a smaller model, or serve late? Who decides?
    \item Below \val{crossover} images/s, T4s beat one H100 per image. As traffic grows, when should a team switch, given a \val{cs_cold} s first deploy?
