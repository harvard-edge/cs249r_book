\begin{frame}{Estimate: how many images to test a 1 percent group?}
  \mlsysactivity{Estimate}{3 min}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \vspace{0pt}
      \begin{mlsyscard}{mlsysaccent}
        \textbf{Given}\\[0.05cm]
        Group: 1\% of users\\
        Error rate within $\pm$1 point, 95\%\\
        Worst case: error rate 0.5
      \end{mlsyscard}
      \vspace{0.15cm}\small
      \uncover<2->{Per-group margin $= 1.96\sqrt{0.25/n}$\\[0.2cm]}
      \uncover<3->{$n = 1.96^2 \times 0.25 / 0.01^2 = \mathbf{9{,}604}$}

\mlsysnote{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{0.5 min}{Core}%
  State Principle 13, the Bias Feedback Model, aloud: $\Delta(k) \approx \Delta(0)\,\alpha^k$, where $\Delta$ is the inter-group gap, $k$ counts retraining cycles, and $\alpha$ is the feedback factor. When $\alpha > 1$, a small gap compounds; when $\alpha < 1$, it decays. A fairness check passed at launch certifies only $k = 0$.

  \notecue{Next}{Show the censored-label mechanism in lending that pushes $\alpha$ above one.}

\begin{frame}{Denied applicants never produce a label}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.70\textwidth,height=5.6cm,keepaspectratio]{censored_loop_step}{3}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{1.5 min}{Core}%
    Trace why Lecture 14's retraining loop becomes a bias-amplifying loop across three clicks. First, the credit model splits applicants into approved and denied branches. Second, only approved applicants repay or default and generate ground-truth labels; denied applicants never produce an observed repayment outcome. Third, retraining updates the model solely on the approved branch, starving future versions of evidence on any group the prior model under-approved.

\begin{frame}{Five things to carry out of this lecture}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25cm}
    \item A model can predict its target well and still harm people when the target is a proxy.
    \item A group becomes testable when it is named: 10,000 examples for a 1\% group, or 1,000,000 at random.
    \item Fairness criteria conflict under unequal base rates; the chosen one has a price stated as a number.
    \item Under feedback, a check passed at launch drifts unless the loop observes what it censored.
    \item A ranking service that shows only what it chose never learns what it hid; exploration is the price of keeping the feedback factor below one.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{1 min}{Core}%
    Read the five takeaways aloud, mapping line 1 to proxies, line 2 to stratified test sizing, line 3 to conflicting criteria and their explicit price, and lines 4 and 5 to censored feedback and exploration. Ask students to star the single takeaway they found least intuitive.

    \notecue{Next}{Leave students with two open policy-and-engineering questions to reflect on.}
  }
\end{frame}
