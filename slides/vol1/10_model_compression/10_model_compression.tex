      \small A $4096 \times 4096$ FP32 layer, factored at rank $k = 128$. Megabytes before and after?\\[0.25cm]
      \uncover<2->{$4096^2 \times 4$ B = \textbf{67.1 MB}\\[0.1cm]
        $2 \times 4096 \times 128 \times 4$ B = \textbf{4.2 MB}, 16$\times$ fewer\\[0.25cm]}
      \uncover<3->{\footnotesize Cost: an intermediate vector, a second kernel launch, and ranks off the tile size waste the array.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{Model Compression, \S10.2.2 and Systems Perspective 10.1.}
  \note{[3 min] [CORE] Low-rank factorization replaces one weight matrix $W$ ($m$ by $n$) with two thin ones, $U$ ($m$ by $k$) and $V$ ($k$ by $n$), whose product approximates it. It works when a few directions carry most of the transformation, which trained weights often show. ENGAGE: one minute, compute both sizes. Step 2: dense, 4,096 squared times 4 bytes is 67.1 MB; factored, two 4,096 by 128 matrices are 4.2 MB, 16 times fewer values. Matrix-vector arithmetic falls the same way, from $mn$ to $k(m + n)$ multiply-accumulates. Step 3: the cost. The layer now runs as two kernels, with an intermediate vector of length 128 written and read between them unless they are fused, plus a second launch. If the rank does not match the hardware tile (a multiple of 16, for example), part of the matrix unit sits idle. WARN: the error depends on how fast the singular values decay; the chapter's 16 times assumes rank 128 captures almost all of the layer. Choosing rank is a validation question, like a pruning threshold. LINK: same lesson as channel tiles, shapes must suit the hardware.}
\end{frame}

      \uncover<3->{\small $L = (1 - \alpha)\,L_{\text{CE}} + \alpha\,T^2\,\text{KL}(\text{teacher}_T \,\|\, \text{student}_T)$\\[0.2cm]}
      \uncover<4->{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth,height=3.4cm,keepaspectratio]{distilbert}}\\[0.1cm]

    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \note{[4.5 min] [CORE] Distillation trains a small dense student to imitate a large teacher. Step 1: a hard label says cat, 100 percent. Step 2: the teacher says cat 85, dog 10, fox 5: it also says which wrong answers are close, and the student learns from that. Step 3: dividing the teacher's logits by a temperature $T$ before the softmax flattens them; at $T = 4$ the same teacher gives 48, 28, 24 (derived). The loss mixes ordinary cross-entropy on the label, weight $1 - \alpha$, with the KL divergence to the softened teacher, weight $\alpha$; the $T^2$ factor keeps the two gradients on the same scale. ENGAGE: what happens as $T$ grows very large? Expected: every class approaches a third, and the teacher's signal disappears. Step 4: DistilBERT against BERT: 40 percent fewer parameters, 60 percent faster, 97 percent of the language-understanding score. Step 5: half the layers removed, yet 1.6 times faster, not 2: embeddings and fixed per-request costs do not shrink. WARN: the student is dense, which is the advantage over unstructured pruning, but distillation costs a training run. Land the segment: remove structure the hardware can drop, or train a smaller dense student; scattered zeros pay only past a break-even that the format and the kernel set.}
\end{frame}
