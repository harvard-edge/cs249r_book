\mlsysnote{[1 min] The one law of the lecture. A quantizer stores an integer $x_q$ and two numbers per range: a scale $s$, the real step between neighboring integers, and a zero-point $z$, the integer that stands for real zero. The clipping range $[\alpha, \beta]$ is the real interval kept; $b$ is the bit width, so $2^b - 1$ steps. Reading back: $x \approx s(x_q - z)$. Say it in words: subtract the zero-point, multiply by the step. Everything that follows in this segment is about choosing $\alpha$ and $\beta$ well.}
\mlsysfocus{Affine Quantization}{%
  $\displaystyle x \approx s\,(x_q - z)$\\[0.5cm]
  {\normalsize $\displaystyle s = \frac{\beta - \alpha}{2^{b} - 1} \qquad z = \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{-\alpha}{s}\right)$}\\[0.4cm]
  {\small\textcolor{white!75}{$[\alpha, \beta]$: the clipping range \quad $b$: bits \quad $s$: scale \quad $z$: zero-point}}%
}

\begin{frame}{A clipping range trades the outliers against resolution}
  \centering
  \safeimg[width=0.92\textwidth,height=4.6cm,keepaspectratio]{book_activation_histogram.pdf}\\[0.1cm]
  \uncover<2->{\small Clipping near 1.1 gives the bulk finer steps; per-channel ranges help when channels differ.}
  \mlsyscredit{Model Compression, \S10.3.3 (after Wu et al. 2020).}
  \note{[2 min] Step 1: activations of one ResNet-50 layer: most values sit below about 1.1, with a long tail past 2. INT8 has only 256 levels. If the range runs to the largest outlier, most of the levels cover the nearly empty tail and the bulk gets coarse steps. Step 2: clipping near 1.1 spends the levels on the bulk; the few tail values saturate. Calibration methods choose the clip: the maximum, a percentile such as 99.9, or the range that minimizes the information lost (a KL-divergence criterion). Per-tensor ranges use one scale for the whole tensor; per-channel ranges give each output channel its own scale, which helps when channels have very different spreads. ENGAGE: which loses more if you clip too hard: a classifier's confident cases or its rare cases? FLEX: [OPTIONAL]; cut for a 60-minute version.}
\end{frame}
