\begin{frame}{The machine factor splits into memory, compute, and overhead}
  \centering
  \vspace{0.1cm}
  {\large $T = \underbrace{\dfrac{\text{Samples}}{\text{Task}} \times \dfrac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{Sample}}}_{\uncover<2->{\term{OrangeLine}{total operations $O$}}} \times \dfrac{\text{Seconds}}{\text{FLOP}}$}\\[0.55cm]
  \uncover<3->{{\large $T = \underbrace{\dfrac{\Dvol}{\BW}}_{\substack{\term{BlueLine}{memory}\\ \gloss{bytes moved / bytes per second}}} + \underbrace{\dfrac{O}{\Rpeak \cdot \etahw}}_{\substack{\term{OrangeLine}{compute}\\ \gloss{operations / (peak rate $\times$ utilization)}}} + \underbrace{\Llat}_{\substack{\term{midgray}{overhead}\\ \gloss{kernel launch, sync, network}}}$}}\\[0.3cm]
  \uncover<4->{\small $\Dvol$ counts bytes moved; one decoded token moves every weight.}
  \mlsyscredit{Introduction, \S1.7, equations 2 and 3.}
  \note{[2.5 min] [CORE] Step 1: the product from the opening recall. Step 2: the first two factors multiply to O, the total operations. For GPT-3, about 3.14 times 10 to the 23. Compute: operations divided by the peak rate times utilization, eta, the fraction of peak actually achieved. Overhead: launching GPU programs (kernels), synchronizing, networking. Step 4: data appears twice and means different things. In the product it counts samples; in the sum D-vol counts bytes. One generated token is one sample but gigabytes of weights moved.
