  \note{[2.5 min] [CORE] Build left to right, one era per click. Step 1: symbolic AI wrote intelligence as rules. \section{Silent Failure}

\begin{frame}{Accuracy falls with how far the data has moved}
  \centering
  {\large $\text{Accuracy}(t) \approx
    \underbrace{\text{Accuracy}_0}_{\uncover<2->{\term{GreenLine}{at deployment}}} -
    \underbrace{\lambda}_{\uncover<3->{\term{OrangeLine}{sensitivity}}} \cdot
    \underbrace{\mathcal{D}(P_t \,\|\, P_0)}_{\uncover<4->{\term{BlueLine}{distance from the training data (drift)}}}$}\\[0.25cm]
  \only<1-4|handout:0>{\phantom{\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth,height=4.0cm,keepaspectratio]{degradation_step3.pdf}}}%
  \only<5|handout:0>{\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth,height=4.0cm,keepaspectratio]{degradation_step1.pdf}}%
  \only<6|handout:0>{\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth,height=4.0cm,keepaspectratio]{degradation_step2.pdf}}%
  \only<7-|handout:1>{\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth,height=4.0cm,keepaspectratio]{degradation_step3.pdf}}
  \mlsyscredit{Introduction, \S1.6, equation 1. Illustrative lines; the 95 and 85 percent recall points are the chapter's pedestrian example.}
  \note{[3 min] [CORE] The degradation equation, a local fit, not a law. Step 1: accuracy after the model has been deployed for time t. Step 2: Accuracy-zero, the accuracy at deployment. Step 3: lambda, the model's sensitivity to shift, fitted from observed data. Step 4: D of P-t and P-zero, a statistical distance between today's inputs and the training inputs. A growing distance is called drift. Step 5: the chapter's example as a line: Accuracy-zero is where it starts, lambda is its slope. Recall is the fraction of the pedestrians in view that the detector finds. Winter sun and bulky clothing were rare in training, and recall falls from 95 to 85 percent while every frame is processed on time; the miss rate triples. \begin{frame}{The machine factor splits into memory, compute, and overhead}
  \centering
  \vspace{0.1cm}
  {\large $T = \underbrace{\dfrac{\text{Samples}}{\text{Task}} \times \dfrac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{Sample}}}_{\uncover<2->{\term{OrangeLine}{total operations $O$}}} \times \dfrac{\text{Seconds}}{\text{FLOP}}$}\\[0.55cm]
  \uncover<3->{{\large $T = \underbrace{\dfrac{\Dvol}{\BW}}_{\substack{\term{BlueLine}{memory}\\ \gloss{bytes moved / bytes per second}}} + \underbrace{\dfrac{O}{\Rpeak \cdot \etahw}}_{\substack{\term{OrangeLine}{compute}\\ \gloss{operations / (peak rate $\times$ utilization)}}} + \underbrace{\Llat}_{\substack{\term{midgray}{overhead}\\ \gloss{kernel launch, sync, network}}}$}}\\[0.3cm]
  \uncover<4->{\small $\Dvol$ counts bytes moved, not samples: one decoded token moves every weight.}
  \mlsyscredit{Introduction, \S1.7, equations 2 and 3.}
  \note{[3 min] [CORE] Step 1: the product from Lecture 0. Step 2: the first two factors multiply to O, the total operations. For GPT-3, about 3.14 times 10 to the 23. Step 3: a real machine also moves data and pays fixed costs. Run one after another, time is the sum of three terms. Memory: bytes moved divided by bandwidth, the bytes per second memory can deliver. Compute: operations divided by the peak rate times utilization, eta, the fraction of peak actually achieved. Overhead: launching GPU programs (kernels), synchronizing, networking. Step 4: data appears twice and means different things. In the product it counts samples; in the sum D-vol counts bytes. One generated token is one sample but gigabytes of weights moved. WARN: students merge the two D's; ask which one halves when weights go from 16-bit to 8-bit. Expected: D-vol. For architecture students: the same split as CPU compute cycles, memory stalls, and overhead.}
\end{frame}

\mlsysnote{[1 min] The key law of the lecture. Read the top line as a sentence: time is bytes over bandwidth, plus operations over the achieved rate, plus fixed overhead. The second line is the overlapped form: when the hardware fetches the next block while computing the current one, the slower of the two stages hides the faster, so the sum becomes a maximum. The sum is the safe estimate; the maximum is the best case. Lecture 11 develops the overlapped form and the roofline.}
\mlsysfocus{The Iron Law of ML Systems}{%
  $\displaystyle T = \underbrace{\frac{\Dvol}{\BW}}_{\text{memory}} + \underbrace{\frac{O}{\Rpeak \cdot \etahw}}_{\text{compute}} + \underbrace{\Llat}_{\text{overhead}}$\\[0.6cm]
  {\small\textcolor{white!75}{With ideal overlap:\quad $\displaystyle T \ge \max\!\left(\frac{\Dvol}{\BW},\ \frac{O}{\Rpeak \cdot \etahw}\right) + \Llat$}}%
}
