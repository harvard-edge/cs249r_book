  \note{[4 min] [CORE] Back to the hook. Step 1: retrieval, 90 seconds. Step 2: Little's law from Part 1: streams decoding at once equal tokens per second times TPOT. At most 30,904 times 0.2 = 6,181 streams under 200 ms; 12,979 times 0.04 = 519 under 40 ms (derived bounds). Eight H100s hold 8 times 85.9 = 687 GB; 16-bit Llama 2 70B weights take about 141 GB (4 times the chapter's 35.3 GB at 4 bits), leaving 546 GB. A 1,256-token conversation (the case study's length, assumed) at 327,680 bytes per token holds 412 MB, so memory fits 546 GB / 412 MB = 1,327 streams. Under 200 ms memory binds, and the deadline has slack: 1,327 / 30,904 is a 43 ms step. That is why the conversational run keeps 99 percent. Under 40 ms only 519 fit the deadline, so the deadline binds and throughput falls to 42 percent. WARN: with 8-bit weights and cache, memory holds about 3,000 (617 GB / 206 MB), still under 6,181. Step 3: read the table; each lever moves one limit. ENGAGE: which lever first on the 40 ms server? (Expected: one that shortens the step, since the deadline binds.) WARN: serving 30,904 tokens/s under 40 ms takes about 2.4 times the servers (30,904 / 12,979, derived). FLEX: [CORE].}
