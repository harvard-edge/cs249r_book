\begin{frame}{Pairwise couplings grow as $N^2/2$ while alerts grow as $N$}

  \mlsyssteps[width=0.92\textwidth,height=5.3cm,keepaspectratio]{load_step}{3}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%

  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
  The 1928 coal-mine photo shows the origin of canary releases: early detection at low exposure. Rollout risk multiplies three terms (equation 8): regression probability (cut by pre-release testing), harm per exposed unit (cut by validated fallbacks), and exposed units (cut by traffic caps and fast rollback). Step 1: blue-green cutover shifts 100\% of traffic immediately, exposing every user until rollback completes. Step 2: a 5\% canary cuts exposure 20$\times$ for the same detection time, though shrinking a canary further stops helping if smaller traffic stretches detection time proportionally. In Napkin Math 12.6, a silent 0.5-point CTR drop at 5,000 req/s loses 2.16M clicks/day, or \$1.08M/day at \$0.50/click.

\begin{frame}{Shadow traffic tests a candidate before any user sees it}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.95\textwidth,height=4.65cm,keepaspectratio]{shadow_step}{3}\\[0.08cm]
  {\footnotesize Shadow mode spends compute for zero user harm; a canary buys live user feedback under guardrails.}
  \mlsyscredit{ML Operations at Scale, \S12.5.1 and \S12.5.4. Explore: Shadow Deployment (mlsysbook.ai).}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
  Walk through the three-step evidence ladder before full exposure. Step 1: shadow deployment mirrors live production requests asynchronously to candidate v2 while v1 serves 100\% of users; v2's outputs are logged for drift, crashes, and tail latency at zero user harm, trading extra inference compute for safety. Step 2: a 5\% canary directs live traffic to v2 at 20$\times$ lower exposure than cutover, measuring real user outcomes like conversion under automated guardrails. Step 3: a pre-registered evidence check promotes v2 up the ladder (1, 5, 25, 50, 100\%) or rolls back immediately to validated v1 upon breach.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Shadow traffic cannot measure how users respond to changed predictions because users never see v2's output; live feedback requires the canary stage.}

  \notecue{Next}{Calculate how long each canary pause must last to collect its planned evidence sample.}
}

Optimization follows the dominant term: spending \$80K on INT8 quantization to cut a \$400K/month inference bill by 40\% pays back in 2 weeks versus 1.8 months for training. Platform spend scales similarly: $50 \times 20\text{ h} \times \$150 / \$120\text{K} = 1.25$ benefit-cost at 50 models, versus losing half its cost at 20 models.

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
