\mlsysnote{[3.5 min] [CORE] NARRATE: the Fleet Law (Introduction, equation 3) adds the three axes into one step time. The first term is the compute for the whole step on one device, divided across N devices: compute. T comm of N is the exposed time moving gradients and activations: communication. T sync of N is barrier waits, straggler waits, scheduling, and recovery: coordination. T overlap is the communication or coordination hidden behind compute, subtracted so it is not counted twice. Read the line below: the 2,048-GPU step is 0.9 plus 0.4 plus 0.2, with no overlap. WARN: only the first term shrinks as N grows; the communication and synchronization terms usually grow with N. That is why doubling devices does not halve the step. LINK: Lecture 1 owns the Fleet Law and scaling efficiency; every lecture in this course names the term its mechanism changes.}
\mlsysfocus{The Fleet Law}{%
  $\displaystyle T_{\text{step}}(N) = \underbrace{\frac{T_{\text{compute}}}{N}}_{\textcolor{white}{\text{compute}}} + \underbrace{T_{\text{comm}}(N)}_{\textcolor{white}{\text{communication}}} + \underbrace{T_{\text{sync}}(N)}_{\textcolor{white}{\text{coordination}}} - \underbrace{T_{\text{overlap}}}_{\textcolor{white}{\text{hidden}}}$\\[0.6cm]
  {\small\textcolor{white!75}{2,048 GPUs: $1.5\ \text{s} = 0.9 + 0.4 + 0.2 - 0$}}%
