  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
    In 2020, OpenAI's transformer language model GPT-3 required $3.14 \times 10^{23}$ floating-point operations (FLOPs, total work; Brown et al., 2020). One NVIDIA A100 accelerator, a generation newer than the graphics processing units (GPUs) used in that run, peaks at 312 TFLOP/s ($3.12 \times 10^{14}$ operations per second, the rate). Before reading on, choose how long one A100 would need at peak speed from choices A through D.

    \notecue{Check}{The answer is choice C. Dividing work by peak rate gives $\text{time} = \text{work} / \text{rate} \approx 3.14 \times 10^{23} / (3.12 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOP/s}) \approx 1.0 \times 10^{9}\text{ s} \approx 32$ years. Tempting answers are A (3 months) and B (3 years), from underestimating the gap between $10^{23}$ and $10^{14}$ or confusing FLOPs (work) with FLOP/s (rate).}

    \notecue{Watch for}{Keep total work (FLOPs) distinct from rate (FLOP/s); $\text{time} = \text{work} / \text{rate}$ links them.}

    \notecue{Teach}{Vote on A through D before revealing C, and write $\text{time} = \text{work} / \text{rate}$ on the board.}

  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{4 min}{Core}%
    Launching a multi-node job on a shared supercomputer relies on Slurm, a cluster job scheduler that allocates hardware and enforces time limits. Each of these eight exact lines from BLOOM's June 2022 Slurm script encodes a systems decision taught later. The \texttt{--nodes=48} and \texttt{--gres=gpu:8} flags request 48 eight-GPU nodes (Lecture 2 covers node architecture), and \texttt{--time 100:00:00} caps each job slot at 100 hours, forcing at least 27 slots and 26 relaunches over 109 days (derived; Lecture 8 covers scheduling). Last, \texttt{SAVE\_INTERVAL=100} sets the checkpoint interval (Lectures 4 and 7), and \texttt{NCCL\_ASYNC\_ERROR\_HANDLING=1} configures NCCL, NVIDIA's library for multi-GPU data exchange (collective communication, Lecture 5).

Frequent saves pause training and stress shared storage (Lecture 4), whereas rare saves increase lost rework after every crash (Lecture 7 turns this trade-off into a formula).
