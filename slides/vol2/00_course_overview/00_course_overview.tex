\begin{frame}{Predict: GPT-3 on one A100 at full speed?}
  \mlsysactivity{Predict}{2 min}
  \begin{columns}[c]

      {\footnotesize Work: $3.14\times10^{23}$ operations\\Peak: 312 TFLOP/s\\(trillion operations per second)}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \Large
      \choice{A}{3 months}\\[0.2cm]
      \choice{B}{3 years}\\[0.2cm]
      \alt<2->{\colorbox{mlsysaccent!15}{\choice{C}{30 years}}}{\choice{C}{30 years}}\\[0.2cm]
      \choice{D}{300 years}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{Photo: NVIDIA A100 card, Nvidia (CC BY-SA 4.0), Wikimedia Commons. Work: Brown et al. 2020, via Introduction \S1.1.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
    Training GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020) required $3.14 \times 10^{23}$ floating-point operations (FLOPs, total work). A single NVIDIA A100 accelerator, one generation newer than the GPUs used in the actual run, peaks at 312 TFLOP/s ($3.12 \times 10^{14}$ operations per second, the rate). On the reveal, dividing total work by peak rate yields about $1.0 \times 10^{9}$ seconds, or roughly 32 years, confirming choice C.

    \notecue{Check}{Ask students to vote on choices A through D before revealing C. Choice C holds because $\text{time} = \text{work} / \text{rate} \approx 3.14 \times 10^{23} / (3.12 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOP/s}) \approx 1.0 \times 10^{9}\text{ s} \approx 32$ years. Tempting distractors A (3 months) and B (3 years) come from underestimating the exponent gap between $10^{23}$ and $10^{14}$ or confusing FLOPs (work) with FLOP/s (rate).}

    \notecue{Watch for}{Write $\text{time} = \text{work} / \text{rate}$ visibly so students keep total FLOPs distinct from FLOP/s.}

    \notecue{Next}{The next slide compares this 32-year single-GPU ceiling with the actual 10,000-GPU training time.}
  }
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{BLOOM's team chose its save interval after a crash}
  \begin{mlsyscard}{mlsysaccent}
    \small\itshape
    ``Just last night the training crashed [\ldots] 250 iterations is about \textasciitilde8h. So if the training crashes at 7:50, we lose 8h of work. [\ldots] Probably every 4h is sane enough of a compromise.''
    \upshape\footnotesize\textcolor{midgray}{\hfill BLOOM-176B README}
  \end{mlsyscard}\par\vspace{0.18cm}
  \centering\safeimg[width=0.92\textwidth,height=2.4cm,keepaspectratio]{bloom_save_timeline.pdf}\\[0.1cm]
  {\small\textcolor{midgray}{Lecture 7 turns this judgment into a formula.}}
  \mlsyscredit{BigScience Workshop, github.com/bigscience-workshop/bigscience, train/tr11-176B-ml/README.md, section Checkpoints; quoted exactly, with omissions marked.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
    The quoted README passage captures how BLOOM's engineers set their checkpoint cadence by judgment after a crash. At roughly 2 minutes per step, 250 steps spanned about 8 hours, so a crash at 7:50 erased almost 8 hours of work across 384 GPUs; they settled on saving roughly every 4 hours as a practical compromise. The timeline below shows the core trade-off: frequent saves pause training and stress storage (Lecture 4), whereas rare saves increase lost rework on every crash (Lecture 7 turns this into a formula).

    \notecue{Watch for}{Avoid deriving the optimal checkpoint interval formula today; leave the quantitative derivation for Lecture 7.}

    \notecue{Next}{Students next discuss why BLOOM's team paid to keep 32 GPUs idle throughout the 109-day run.}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.18cm}
    \item One A100 would need about 32 years for GPT-3; about 10,000 GPUs took about 15 days, and landmark runs now use 16,384.
    \item A fleet joins accelerators, links, storage, and a building into one job; BLOOM used 384 A100s for 109 days and planned for failure.
    \item By the end of the course you will size and defend fleet designs with numbers.
    \item The course climbs an eight-row stack in four parts, and C$^3$ names what each decision spends.
    \item Two predictions are on your card; Lectures 1 and 7 return to them.
  \end{itemize}
