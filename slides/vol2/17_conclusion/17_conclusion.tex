\begin{frame}{Six principles sit on the four bands of the fleet stack}

  \note{[4 min] NARRATE: six principles, each condensing one part of the course. Step 1: the physical band. \mlsysnote{[2 min] [CORE] NARRATE: the Fleet Law from Lecture 1, unchanged (Conclusion, \S17.2, equation 1). Infrastructure sets the per-device rates behind the compute term and the link bandwidths behind the communication term. Communication and waiting fill the second and third terms, and neither is guaranteed to shrink as N rises. Failure lands in the same terms: checkpoint pauses, detection timeouts, and restarts add to the synchronization term averaged over a run; asynchronous checkpointing moves part of that into overlap; a rollback adds lost steps back to the work. Responsibility and sustainability add no new term but spend the existing ones: verification occupies compute, approval gates add coordination, and every second of step time costs joules at the fleet's average power. Their acceptance also needs evidence the law cannot supply: subgroup error rates, privacy accounting, energy over a stated boundary. ENGAGE: which term does a checkpoint pause land in? (Expected: synchronization, or overlap if asynchronous.) FLEX: [CORE].}

\begin{frame}{Checkpoint overhead grows as $\sqrt{N}$, from \val{ov_ref_pct}\% to \val{ov_exp_pct}\%}
  \centering
  \safeimg[width=0.86\textwidth,height=4.6cm,keepaspectratio]{overhead.pdf}\\[0.1cm]
  {\small Overhead at the Young-Daly interval $\approx \sqrt{2\delta / M}$:\enspace $\delta$ = \val{t_write_s} s write, $M$ = time between interruptions}
  \mlsyscredit{Conclusion, \S17.3 (\val{ckpt_tb} TB checkpoint, \val{t_write_s} s write; Young 1974, Daly 2006). Curve and points derived.}
  \note{[2.5 min] NARRATE: the Young-Daly interval balances the time spent writing checkpoints against the work redone after a failure. At that interval, checkpoint-plus-rework overhead is about the square root of twice the write time over the interval between interruptions. The \val{ckpt_tb} TB checkpoint takes \val{t_write_s} s to write. At \val{ref_gpus} GPUs, with \val{mtbi_ref_h} hours between interruptions, overhead is \val{ov_ref_pct} percent of wall-clock time; at \val{exp_gpus} GPUs, \val{ov_exp_pct} percent (derived). Because the interval falls as 1/N, overhead grows as the square root of N, so each doubling multiplies it by \val{ov_growth}. The interval itself shrinks from about \val{tau_ref_min} to about \val{tau_exp_min} minutes (derived). LINK: Lecture 7 owns the Young-Daly interval; Lecture 4 owns the checkpoint write path. ENGAGE: what would halve this overhead? (Expected: a write four times faster, since overhead goes as the square root of write time.) WARN: these figures count GPU failures only and assume the write does not contend with data loading. FLEX: [CORE].}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What speedup must \val{exp_gpus} GPUs reach to pay off?}
  \mlsysactivity{Estimate}{3.5 min}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \begin{mlsyscard}{mlsysaccent}\small
        Overhead: \val{ov_ref_pct}\% at \val{ref_gpus}; \val{ov_exp_pct}\% at \val{exp_gpus}\\[0.1cm]
        Run time $\propto \Tstep(N) / (1 - \text{overhead})$\\[0.1cm]
        Accelerator-hours $=$ GPUs $\times$ run time
      \end{mlsyscard}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
      \small
      1. Break-even step speedup?\enspace\uncover<2->{$\dfrac{1 - \val{ov_ref}}{1 - \val{ov_exp}} \approx$ \textbf{\val{breakeven}$\times$}}\\[0.3cm]
      2. Accelerator-hours at an ideal \val{exp_factor}$\times$?\\[0.1cm]
      \uncover<3->{\hspace*{1em}$\val{exp_factor} \times \dfrac{\val{breakeven}}{\val{exp_factor}}$: \textbf{+\val{ideal_hours_pct}\%} accelerator-hours}\\[0.3cm]
      \uncover<4->{Recovery does not block the expansion. Short of a superlinear speedup, doubling never saves accelerator-hours.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{Conclusion, \S17.3 (decision rule). Values derived in images/calcs.py.}
  \note{[4 min] ENGAGE: pairs, two minutes, both questions. Step 2: for fixed work, run time is proportional to step time over the retained fraction, one minus overhead. The larger cluster shortens the run only if its measured step speedup exceeds the ratio of retained fractions: \val{retained_ref} over \val{retained_exp}, about \val{breakeven}, which the chapter rounds to \val{breakeven2} (derived). Recovery alone does not block the expansion. Step 3: accelerator-hours are GPUs times run time. Doubling the GPUs multiplies them by \val{exp_factor} times \val{breakeven} divided by the achieved speedup. Even at the ideal \val{exp_factor} times, they rise \val{ideal_hours_pct} percent (derived). Step 4: so the expansion buys calendar time with accelerator-hours and energy, and every point of scaling efficiency lost to exposed communication raises that price.
