}
\end{frame}

\mlsysnote{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
  Revisit Lecture 1's Fleet Law unchanged (\S17.2, eq. 1). Infrastructure sets per-device compute rates ($C_1$) and link bandwidths ($C_2$); exposed communication and barrier waits populate $T_{\text{comm}}(N)$ and $T_{\text{sync}}(N)$ without shrinking as $N$ grows. Failures land in the same terms: synchronous checkpoint pauses, timeouts, and restarts add to amortized $T_{\text{sync}}$ (asynchronous checkpointing shifts part into $T_{\text{overlap}}$, while rollbacks add lost steps back to work). Responsibility and sustainability add no fifth term; instead, verification spends compute, approvals spend coordination, and every second draws joules at average fleet power, alongside external evidence such as subgroup gaps, DP budgets, and energy boundaries.

  \notecue{Next}{A Concept Question tests which principle explains a synchronous job dropping from 94\% to 38\% scaling efficiency.}
}
\mlsysfocus{The Fleet Law}{%
$\Tstep(N) = \underbrace{\dfrac{\Tcomp}{N}}_{C_1} + \underbrace{\Tcomm(N)}_{C_2} + \underbrace{\Tsync(N)}_{C_3} - \underbrace{\Tover}_{\text{hidden}}$\\[0.4cm]
{\normalsize Responsibility and sustainability add no term; they spend these.}%
}

\begin{frame}{Checkpoint overhead grows as $\sqrt{N}$, from 1.7\% to 2.4\%}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.86\textwidth,height=4.6cm,keepaspectratio]{overhead_step}{2}\\[0.1cm]
  {\small Overhead at the Young-Daly interval $\approx \sqrt{2\delta / M}$:\enspace $\delta$ = 24.5 s write, $M$ = time between interruptions}
  \mlsyscredit{Conclusion, \S17.3 (2.45 TB checkpoint, 24.5 s write; Young 1974, Daly 2006). Curve and points derived.}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  Apply Lecture 7's Young-Daly optimal checkpointing formula (using Lecture 4's 24.5 s write for a 2.45 TB checkpoint): wall-clock overhead is approximately $\sqrt{2\delta / M}$. Step 1: at 1,024 GPUs ($M = 48.8\text{ h}$), overhead is 1.7\% (derived). Step 2: doubling to 2,048 GPUs ($M = 24.4\text{ h}$) raises overhead only to 2.4\% while shortening the checkpoint interval from about 49 to about 35 minutes (derived). Because $M \propto 1/N$, overhead scales as $\sqrt{N}$ ($1.41\times$ per doubling), and cutting overhead in half requires a 4$\times$ faster write path (scaling as $\sqrt{\delta}$).

  \notecue{Watch for}{These numbers count GPU failures only and assume checkpoint writes do not contend with data loading.}

  \mlsysnotemeta{4 min}{Core}%

Step 2: 768 and 1,024 GPUs reach 136.7k and 176.9k tokens/s, a $1.86\times$ gain from 512 to 1,024 (derived) as exposed communication (Lecture 5) and smaller per-GPU batches lower MFU from 63.5\% to 59.0\% (Lecture 6). Transferred illustratively to our doubling, $1.86\times$ finishes 46\% sooner ($1 - 1.007/1.86$) while spending $+8.3\%$ more GPU-hours ($2 \times 1.007/1.86$, derived).

  \notecue{Watch for}{MegaScale ran on different GPUs and topology; use 1.86$\times$ only to gauge effect size, never as a substitute for measuring our own cluster.}

  \notecue{Next}{If instead the doubled cluster spends twice the total compute, Kaplan's scaling curve prices the quality gain.}
