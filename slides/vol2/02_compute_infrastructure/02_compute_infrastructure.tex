\begin{frame}{Four walls limit performance at four physical scales}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.95\textwidth,height=5.7cm,keepaspectratio]{walls_step}{4}
  \mlsyscredit{Compute Infrastructure, \S2.1 (figure 1), \S2.3, \S2.5.1, \S2.6, \S2.7.1. Pod failure interval: 10,000 GPUs, per-GPU MTTF 50,000 h, independent failures.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
    This four-step build lays out the entire lecture as a ladder of physical limits. First, at the accelerator scale, HBM delivers 3.35 TB/s to arithmetic cores capable of consuming far more: the memory wall. Second, at the node scale, intra-node NVLink delivers 450 GB/s per direction per GPU, then drops $9\times$ to 50 GB/s over InfiniBand between servers: the communication wall. Third, at the rack scale, four eight-GPU servers draw 33.5 kW versus 12 kW for a standard enterprise rack: the power wall. Fourth, at the pod scale, 10,000 GPUs with a 50,000-hour per-GPU MTTF lose a GPU roughly every 5 hours: the reliability wall. Relieving one wall by adding GPUs inevitably adds links, watts, and failing parts, exposing the next wall.

    \notecue{Watch for}{Which wall limits a specific run depends on the workload; hardware sets the ceilings, and workload intensity picks the active limit.}

\begin{frame}{Capacity decides whether it fits; bandwidth decides how fast}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.92\textwidth,height=5.0cm,keepaspectratio]{two_tests_step}{2}
  \mlsyscredit{Compute Infrastructure, \S2.3.1: Llama-3 70B, FP16 weights, batch-1 decode; floor = weight bytes / HBM bandwidth on one accelerator-equivalent path.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
    Hardware selection requires two independent tests. First, step 1 checks capacity: Llama-3 70B FP16 weights require 141.2 GB against one H100's 80 GB, demanding at least two GPUs before counting KV cache or workspace. Then, step 2 checks bandwidth for batch-1 decode on one accelerator-equivalent path: streaming 141.2 GB sets lower bounds of 69.2 ms on an A100 (2.04 TB/s), 42.1 ms on an H100 (3.35 TB/s), and 17.7 ms on a B200 (8 TB/s). Doubling peak FP16 FLOP/s without raising HBM bandwidth leaves this decode floor completely untouched.

    \notecue{Watch for}{A model that fits comfortably in HBM yet misses its latency target fails deployment just as surely as an out-of-memory crash.}

    \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%

Second, step 2 raises batch size to 64, reaching roughly 22\% of peak yet staying left of the ridge. Step 2 fills both columns across four archetypes: 175B large-batch training is compute-limited per chip and interconnect-limited across nodes (compare sustained throughput); batch-1 70B decode is HBM bandwidth-limited (GB/s per dollar); 10 TB recommender embedding tables (Archetype B) exceed GPU HBM and rely on host DRAM capacity (GB per dollar); and sparse Mixture-of-Experts models (such as Mixtral-8x7B with 12.9B of 46.7B parameters active) route tokens via AllToAll across fabric bisection (Lecture 3).

    \notecue{Check}{Have pairs fill in the middle column in two minutes before revealing step 2. A common error is marking all four as compute-limited because all four run on GPUs.}

    \notecue{Watch for}{A single workload can switch walls with scale: compute-bound on one chip, communication-bound across a pod.}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
