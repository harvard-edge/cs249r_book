\begin{frame}{Capacity crosses three boundaries before it becomes work}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.98\textwidth,height=5.0cm,keepaspectratio]{goodput_chain_step}{3}\\[0.05cm]
  {\footnotesize\textcolor{midgray}{Gang: the full set of devices a synchronous job needs before any step can run.}}
  \mlsyscredit{Fleet Efficiency, \S9.2 and figure 1; Wongpanich et al. 2025. Widths schematic.}
  \note{[3 min] [CORE] Each row is capacity-time; each boundary keeps part of it. Step 1, scheduling goodput $S$: the share of offered capacity-time held by complete allocations, every resource one synchronous step needs. Unallocated devices, a gang holding half its nodes, and fragments no queued job fits are lost here. Step 2, runtime goodput $R$: the share of held time spent on steps a checkpoint retains. Startup, input waits, checkpoint pauses, recovery, and rework are lost here. Step 3, program goodput $P$: ideal time of the retained steps over their actual time, against a named hardware ideal. Exposed communication, stragglers, pipeline bubbles, memory stalls, and kernel dispatch are lost here. Each factor divides by what the previous one passed, so the denominators cancel and the factors multiply. A lost interval is charged once, at the first boundary it fails. ENGAGE: a gang holding 8 of its 16 nodes; which factor? (Expected: $S$.) WARN: the factor that records a loss is not always the team that fixes it.}
\end{frame}

\mlsysnote{[1 min] [CORE] The lecture's one law. Say it once, slowly: offered capacity times three fractions, each on its own boundary. Google introduced this decomposition for its TPU fleet and called it ML productivity goodput (Wongpanich et al. 2025). Useful output equals peak capacity times MPG, and the cost of a useful hour is the raw price divided by MPG.}
\mlsysfocus{ML Productivity Goodput}{%
$\text{MPG} = \underbrace{S}_{\text{held}/\text{offered}} \times
     \underbrace{R}_{\text{retained}/\text{held}} \times
     \underbrace{P}_{\text{ideal}/\text{retained}}$\rule[-1.4cm]{0pt}{0pt}%
}

\begin{frame}{Fleet profiling reads a hierarchy, cheapest record first}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.98\textwidth,height=6.2cm,keepaspectratio]{ladder_step}{3}
  \mlsyscredit{Fleet Efficiency, \S9.3.2, table 3, and figure 4.}
  \note{[2.5 min] [CORE] Three records, read from the top. Step 1: scheduler records. The control plane already writes them for admission and billing, so they cost nothing and cover every job: submission, device assignments, the moment the gang became complete, release. They settle $S$ and give the held time that $R$ divides by. A wedged job holding a full gang looks identical to a healthy one here. Step 2: the job timeline, a few fields per step: first step, step durations, checkpoint commits, restarts, input-wait time. It settles $R$, and it shows that retained steps run slow, without saying why. Step 3: step traces, every kernel and collective on sampled ranks for seconds. They settle $P$ and the term to shorten. A clean trace from a healthy hour says nothing about restarts. WARN: opening the profiler first always finds some gap in a step and can miss a week lost to queues or restarts. For the running job, the scheduler showed partial holds, the timeline showed slow steps from day one, and only then did the team trace one data-parallel group.}
\end{frame}

  \note{[2 min] [CORE] The product rule says whether a change raises efficiency; it does not say when a job finishes. Equation 7: time to result is queue wait plus ideal work divided by device count times $R$ times $P$. $S$ is absent because capacity idle elsewhere does not slow a job that holds its gang. Llama 3's own table gives two operating points. GPU-hours per unit of work rose by 0.43 over 0.41, 1.049 times, 4.9 percent. Neither point dominates, because one is faster and the other cheaper. These are points on the efficiency frontier, the configurations where goodput, cost, and time cannot all improve together. ENGAGE: who picks 16,384, and who picks 8,192? (Expected: a release deadline picks speed; a busy shared fleet picks the efficient allocation.) WARN: a configuration counts only if it still reaches the quality target and fits in memory.}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.18cm}
    \item Useful output is offered capacity $\times S \times R \times P$; each lost hour is charged at the first boundary it fails.
    \item Fix the contract and window first; read scheduler records, then the timeline, then traces.
    \item Held time leaks through partial gangs and five runtime terms; their order depends on job size.
    \item MFU or MBU measures $P$; a fix helps only through the exposed term it shortens.
    \item Rank levers and trades by the product; time to result and cost place a job on the frontier.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{[1 min] [CORE] One line per segment. Ask students to star the line they would have gotten wrong at the start of class.}
\end{frame}
