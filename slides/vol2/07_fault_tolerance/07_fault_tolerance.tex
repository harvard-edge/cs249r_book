\begin{frame}{A 50,000-hour GPU fails every 5 hours in a 10,000-GPU job}
  \centering
  {\normalsize $R_{\text{sys}}(t) = e^{-N\lambda t}$ \qquad $\text{MTBF}_{\text{sys}} = \dfrac{1}{N\lambda} = \dfrac{\text{MTTF}}{N}$}\quad{\small MTBF: mean time between job failures}\\[0.1cm]
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.95\textwidth,height=5.2cm,keepaspectratio]{mtbf_ladder_step}{3}
  \mlsyscredit{Fault Tolerance, equations 3 and 4 and table 1. GPU-only basis: 50,000 h per GPU, independent, constant rate.}
  \note{[2.5 min] [CORE] The equations: R is the probability that no component has failed by time t; lambda is one GPU's failure rate, one per 50,000 hours. With N independent components the rates add, so the system's mean time between failures (MTBF) is MTTF over N. Step 1: 8 GPUs fail about every 6,250 hours, 260 days; 64 GPUs every 33 days. Step 2: 512 GPUs every 4 days, 1,000 every 2 days. Red bars mean a failure more often than once a week. Step 3: 10,000 GPUs every 5 hours, 25,000 every 2 hours. A three-month run on 10,000 GPUs expects hundreds of failures. LINK: Lecture 1's reliability gap, the probability that all N components are up at once, is the same product. At 99.99 percent availability per GPU, all 10,000 are up together only about 36.8 percent of the time. WARN: these are planning averages; they do not schedule incidents, and a short job can finish without a failure.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Persistence chooses retry, quarantine, or replacement}
  \centering
  {\normalsize Three axes: \textbf{persistence} (does it come back?), \textbf{manifestation} (crash, slow, or wrong?), \textbf{scope} (how many GPUs?).}\\[0.3cm]
  \footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}M{2.2cm}M{4.3cm}M{4.6cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    Persistence & Signature & Fleet response \\
    \midrule
    \textcolor{GreenLine}{\textbf{Transient}} & One wrong value or lost message; retry succeeds & Correct or retry; roll back if saved state was touched \\
    \uncover<2->{\textcolor{OrangeLine}{\textbf{Intermittent}}} & \uncover<2->{Returns under specific load, heat, or voltage} & \uncover<2->{Drain and quarantine; readmit only after stress tests under load} \\
    \uncover<3->{\textcolor{RedLine}{\textbf{Permanent}}} & \uncover<3->{Repeats at the same device, address, or link} & \uncover<3->{Replace; restore a checkpoint from before the fault} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \mlsyscredit{Fault Tolerance, \S7.3 and \S7.4.1, table 3; fault, error, failure: Avizienis et al. 2004.}
  \note{[2 min] [CORE] A fault is the defect, an error the corrupted state it produces, a failure the misbehavior the job sees. The chapter places every fault on three independent axes, each choosing part of the response. Step 1: a transient fault, such as a particle strike flipping one bit, leaves nothing to repair; the question is only whether its effect escaped into saved state. Step 2: an intermittent fault, such as a cracked solder joint, passes a reboot test and fails again under the next job's heat. Keep it suspect until stress tests under load clear it. Step 3: a permanent fault, such as a stuck-at bit in a multiplier, repeats on every retry, so rerunning on the same device reproduces the error instead of exposing it. Manifestation decides whether anyone notices. A crash announces itself; a slow node and a wrong value do not. Scope decides the blast radius from the last segment. WARN: the axes are not exclusive bins; a transient fault can crash a process or silently corrupt a gradient.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A checkpoint exists only when its manifest is published last}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.18cm}
    \item A job's failure interval is MTTF over $N$ at best; name the basis, and expect measured clusters to fail sooner.
    \item Persistence, manifestation, and scope choose the response; liveness checks miss slow and wrong.
    \item Save every $\sqrt{2\delta M}$, and count only a manifest-published, consistent cut as saved.
    \item Each failure costs detection, restart, load, and warmup; fencing and elastic invariants keep recovery correct.
    \item Serving buys availability with replicas, sizes failover by state, and degrades by a validated ladder.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{[1 min] [CORE] One line per segment, with recovery split from checkpoints. Ask students to star the line they would have gotten wrong at the start of the lecture.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Open questions}
