Assurance: no per-student claim, release-blocking owner, or invalidation rule. Fairness: optimizing historical school distributions over individual merit without explicit accountable choice. Evidence: failing to disaggregate gaps by cohort size with denominators before results day. Capacity: zero budgeted review capacity for 68\% of 246,110 candidates. Remedy: no individual evidence-based appeal, forcing blanket reversal four days later. The algorithm did what it was programmed to do; the surrounding sociotechnical system failed deployment review.

  \notecue{Check}{Ask whether Ofqual's algorithm failed because it was inaccurate. No: it hit its historical-distribution objective, proving today's thesis that accuracy alone never establishes fitness for deployment.}

  \notecue{Next}{The takeaways slide distills each of today's five segments into one governance obligation.}
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.18cm}
    \item An assurance case justifies one use and expires on material change.
    \item Unequal base rates make criteria conflict; choosing one is governance.
    \item A gap is evidence only with its denominator, interval, and subgroups.
    \item Each safeguard is capacity on a named path; plan deletion before training.
    \item Evidence protects people only when an owner can correct a decision.
  \end{itemize}

Emphasize the first and fifth bullets if short on time.
