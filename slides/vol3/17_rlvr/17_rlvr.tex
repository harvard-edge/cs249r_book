\begin{frame}{A task the policy never solves gives zero signal}
  \centering
  \safeimg[width=0.92\textwidth,height=4.5cm,keepaspectratio]{groups.pdf}\\[0.15cm]
  {\small A group of $G$ at pass rate $p$ holds a pass with probability $1 - (1-p)^G$; at $p = 0$, none.}
  \mlsyscredit{Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards, \S17.2.3 and equation 2. Coefficients computed.}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
    Each row shows a group of 8 attempts on one task with group-mean baseline coefficients underneath (green pushes up, red pushes down). Top: when a policy never solves the task ($p = 0$), every reward and every coefficient is 0, moving zero weights regardless of rollout spend (and a fixed positive baseline only pushes all failures down equally without preferring anything).   \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
    Entropy collapse is the premature loss of action diversity on unsolved tasks. This measured curve from DAPO (Yu et al. 2025, Figure 2b, Qwen2.5-32B on AIME) shows symmetric $\pm 0.2$ clipping (teal) driving token entropy from about 0.7 to near zero within roughly 1,000 steps, making group samples nearly identical so rewards tie and gradients vanish. Why: capping the probability ratio at 1.2 allows a dominant 0.9 token to reach 1.08 (uncapped), while a rare 0.01 token can rise by only 0.002 to 0.012. Clip-Higher (purple) raises only the upper bound and sustains entropy near 0.4.

\begin{frame}{Open questions}
  \normalsize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.3cm}
    \item A monitor that penalizes detected hacks may teach hacks the monitor cannot see. How should a team use a monitor during training?
    \item Most agent tasks lack an executable check. When can a judge model be the reward, and how often must it be recalibrated as the policy shifts?
    \item If RL mostly sharpens what the base model can already sample, what must change for it to teach new capabilities?
  \end{itemize}
  \note{%
    \mlsysnotemeta{1 min}{Optional}%
    Three open prompts for discussion or short reflection: using hack monitors during training without teaching obfuscation that evades the monitor (METR recommends patching scorers rather than penalizing detected hacks; Baker et al. 2025); deploying learned judges only where executable checks do not exist while recalibrating as the policy shifts; and investigating what allows RL to create new capabilities beyond sharpening base-model reach (Yue et al. 2025).
