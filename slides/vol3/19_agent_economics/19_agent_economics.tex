\begin{frame}{Each accepted task carries $(1-\alpha)/\alpha$ rejected ones}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      \centering\safeimg[width=\textwidth,height=5.3cm,keepaspectratio]{multiplier.pdf}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \small
      $\Ceff = \dfrac{\sum_i C_{\text{task},i}}{N_{\text{acc}}} = \bar C_{\text{acc}} + \dfrac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\,\bar C_{\text{rej}}$\\[0.3cm]
      {\footnotesize $\alpha$: share of initiated tasks accepted under one contract by a cutoff. A retry on one ticket adds no accepted task.}\\[0.3cm]
      \begin{mlsyscard}{mlsysaccent}\small A token cut shrinks one term. Acceptance divides \textbf{every} term.\end{mlsyscard}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{Agent Economics, \S19.2.2 (cohort cost per accepted task) and its rejected-per-accepted figure, redrawn at slide size.}
  \note{[2.5 min] [CORE] The cohort form of cost per accepted task. The numerator is every initiated task's account, failed, timed-out, and canceled ones included; the denominator counts distinct tasks accepted under one declared contract by a stated cutoff. Split the numerator by outcome and it becomes the mean accepted cost plus the rejection multiplier times the mean rejected cost. The multiplier is an accounting identity and assumes no model of the tasks. Point to the curve: at 25 of 100 accepted each success carries 3 failures; at 75 of 100 it carries 0.33. Below about 30 percent the curve climbs steeply. ENGAGE: run A resolved 168 of 500. How many unresolved runs did each resolved one carry? (Expected: 332 over 168, about 2.) WARN: three successful retries on one ticket still deliver one accepted task. LINK: Lecture 1 owns the per-attempt planning form, expected attempt cost over acceptance probability; this cohort form is what an account measures. FLEX: [CORE].}
\end{frame}
