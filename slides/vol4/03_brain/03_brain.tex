  {\small OpenVLA keeps no key-value (KV) cache between frames. Keeping 5 s of history at 10 Hz would cost \textbf{\textcolor{figred}{21.0 GB}}, more than the weights.}
  \mlsyscredit{Ch.\ 3, equation 1 and \S3.5.2: $2 \times 32 \times 4{,}096 \times 2$ B per token, 800 tokens per step; hypothetical history. Module: 64 GB ($64 \times 2^{30}$ B $=$ 68.7 GB).}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{1.5 min}{Optional}%
  To attend over past tokens without recomputing them, every transformer layer caches a key and value vector per token: for 32 layers of width 4,096 in 16 bits, each token occupies $2 \times 32 \times 4{,}096 \times 2 = 524{,}288$ bytes, about 0.52 MB, so one 800-token step adds 419 MB. Single-frame models like OpenVLA and our intent model rebuild context each frame and carry no cross-frame KV cache. Retaining 5 seconds of history at 10 Hz would consume 21.0 GB, exceeding the 14 GB of model weights and taking 35.0 GB (over half the Orin's 68.7 GB memory) while adding traffic to the same memory bus carrying camera frames without making answers fresher.

\begin{frame}{Prefill reuses each weight 288 times; decode uses it once}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=14.2cm]{roofline_step}{3}\\[0.05cm]
  \uncover<3->{\small One evaluation splits into prefill (288 tokens, 256 $+$ 32) and decode (one per pass).}
  \mlsyscredit{Ch.\ 3, \S3.4.1 and figure 10, with the Orin's numbers on the axes: ridge $=$ 43 TFLOP/s $/$ 204 GB/s. Intensities count weight traffic only (optimistic).}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  The roofline plot (Williams, Waterman, and Patterson 2009) puts arithmetic intensity (FLOPs per off-chip weight byte) on the horizontal axis and attainable TFLOP/s on the vertical axis for the Orin. First, the slanted bandwidth roof (204 GB/s) meets the flat 43 TFLOP/s compute roof at a ridge point of 210.8 FLOP/byte. Second, during decode each output token executes a matrix-vector multiply using each 2-byte weight for one multiply-add (1 FLOP/byte), capping throughput at 0.204 TFLOP/s, just 0.47\% of peak. Third, during prefill all 288 input tokens (256 image patches plus 32 instruction tokens) share each fetched weight simultaneously, landing past the ridge in the compute-bound regime.

  \notecue{Watch for}{4-bit weight quantization speeds up bandwidth-bound decode rather than compute-bound prefill. Dividing FLOPs by the 43 TFLOP/s peak overestimates batch-1 decode speed by two orders of magnitude; the systems appendix reviews rooflines.}

  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{1.5 min}{Optional}%
  This plot presents the chapter's illustrative calibration regimes (Table 4): in distribution, reported confidence (0.91) matches task success (0.89) within 0.02; near out of distribution (glare or lens droplets), confidence stays at 0.89 while success plunges to 0.52; under degraded sensing, 0.84 versus 0.31; and far out of distribution, 0.78 versus 0.08. Softmax scores sum to one by algebra, and ReLU networks provably produce arbitrarily high confidence far from training data (Guo et al. 2017; Hein et al. 2019). Chunk policies like ACT emit no normalized probability at all, requiring calibrated dispersion, ensemble disagreement, or conformal thresholds (Lecture 10) tied to each \texttt{model\_id}. A naive filter admitting scores above 0.78 passes every regime, accepting a 48\% near-OOD failure rate.

  \notecue{Watch for}{These four regime pairs are stipulated teaching values clearly marked illustrative on the slide.}
