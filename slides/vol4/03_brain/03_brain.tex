  exp\_weights = torch.from\_numpy(exp\_weights).cuda().unsqueeze(dim=1)\\
  raw\_action = (actions\_for\_curr\_step * exp\_weights).sum(dim=0, keepdim=True)
  \end{logblock}
  \end{mlsyscard}
  \vspace{0.1cm}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \centering\safeimg[width=7.6cm]{blend.pdf}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}\small
      Index 0 is the oldest chunk, so it weighs most.\\[0.15cm]
      \uncover<2->{Real-time chunking instead freezes the setpoints that run during inference and fills in the rest to agree with them.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{github.com/tonyzhaozh/act, commit 742c753, imitate\_episodes.py (indentation removed). Black, Galliker, and Levine, NeurIPS 2025. Ch.\ 3, \S3.5.1.}
  \note{[2 min] NARRATE: the real code from ACT's repository. Every chunk that covers step t is stored; the weights decay with index, so the oldest prediction gets the largest weight (with k = 0.01 and \val{act_chunk} overlapping chunks, the oldest weighs \val{ens_ratio} times the newest). The blend smooths the seam between chunks. Two limits. First, an average of two valid predictions need not be valid. An older chunk that passes left of an obstacle and a newer one that passes right average to a path through it (the picture; Lecture 6 owns mode averaging). Second, the blend must drop contributions whose evidence has expired and carry the oldest capture time it keeps. Step 2: real-time chunking freezes the setpoints that will execute during inference and generates the rest of the new chunk to agree with them. ENGAGE: on our machine, how many chunks overlap at one step? (Expected: \val{horizon} over \val{per_renewal}, so \val{overlap}.) WARN: a blend's evidence is as old as its oldest contribution. LINK: Lecture 4 interpolates admitted setpoints per bus cycle; Lecture 11 owns smooth joins between segments. FLEX: [CORE].}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Are these two claims true or false?}
  \mlsysactivity{Concept question}{2.5 min}
  \small
  \textbf{1.}\enspace A numerically valid, smooth chunk can be executed whenever it arrives.\\[0.1cm]
  \uncover<2->{\hspace*{0.5cm}\textbf{\textcolor{figred}{False.}} Run to its end, the mug's chunk lands \val{err0_mm} $+$ \val{drift_span} $=$ \val{err_end} mm off against a \val{tol_mm} mm tolerance.}\\[0.35cm]
