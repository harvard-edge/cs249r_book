\begin{frame}{Does a residual test catch a misdated update?}
  \mlsysactivity{Vote}{3 min}
  \small
  \textbf{Innovation}: reading minus prediction. \textbf{NIS}: innovation$^2$ over predicted variance (mean 1 when honest).\\[0.06cm]
  Misdated update raises expected NIS to 6.25; test rejects NIS $>$ 3.84.\\[0.04cm]
  {\normalsize\choice{A}{Always caught}\qquad\choice{B}{About 2 in 3}\qquad\choice{C}{Never}}\\[0.04cm]
  \uncover<2->{\centering\safeimg[width=0.86\textwidth,height=2.65cm,keepaspectratio]{gates.pdf}\\}
  \uncover<3->{\footnotesize Over 50 innovations the mean NIS sits near 6.25, far above the honest 0.65 to 1.43.}
  \mlsyscredit{Spatial Memory, \S9.2.1 (NIS, NEES, chi-square tests; Bar-Shalom et al. 2001). Probabilities from the noncentral chi-square distribution (derived).}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%
  Define innovation (measurement minus prediction) and normalized innovation squared (NIS, mean 1 when consistent, jumping to expected 6.25 under our misdated update). Have students vote individually, discuss 30 seconds with a neighbor, and vote again before revealing Steps 2 and 3.

  \notecue{Check}{Choice B (about 2 in 3) is right: under noncentral chi-square noise, one misdated update clears the 95\% rejection threshold (3.84) only 63\% of the time (and 99\% threshold 6.63 only 39\%), letting over one third of corrupted frames through. In Step 3, averaging 50 independent innovations cleanly separates honest filters (95\% band 0.65 to 1.43) from persistent misdating near 6.25. Offline against ground truth, NEES audits state error against covariance; online, NIS audits innovations (Bar-Shalom et al. 2001).}

  \notecue{Watch for}{A filter can gradually absorb steady errors into its state, silencing NIS; sliding innovation tests audit covariance honesty without replacing capture-epoch validation.}

  \notecue{Next}{Check how quickly an uncalibrated IMU drifts when dead-reckoning alone between exteroceptive fixes.}
}
\end{frame}

Just as JPL's sandbox testbed rehearsed Spirit's escape because cameras never observed soil strength, Lecture 18 names this limit observational indistinguishability.
