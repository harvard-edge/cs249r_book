      \small
      Half the demos pass left, half pass right.\\[0.3cm]
      \uncover<3->{The squared-error optimum is the mean:\\[0.15cm]
      {\normalsize $\pi^*(o) = \mathbb{E}[a \mid o]$}\\[0.15cm]
      $[v, +\omega]$ and $[v, -\omega]$ average to $[v, 0]$, straight ahead.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{Policy Training, \S6.2.3, equation 3 and Definition 6.1 (schematic).}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  Build mode averaging (Definition 6.1) across three steps. First, a mobile base faces a warehouse column. Second, demonstrations are 100\% collision-free: half steer left $[v, +\omega]$ and half steer right $[v, -\omega]$. Third, a deterministic regressor minimizing mean squared error converges to the conditional expectation $\pi^*(o) = \mathbb{E}[a \mid o] = [v, 0]$, driving directly into the column, the single path zero demonstrators chose. More data, bigger networks, and lower held-out squared error push predictions even closer to that unsafe mean, requiring a multimodal output distribution (mixture, diffusion, or flow head) rather than greater capacity.

  \notecue{Watch for}{In image regression, averaging produces a harmless blur; across Lecture 1's causal boundary, averaging valid robot motions produces a physical collision. Lecture 3 previewed this same hazard for temporal chunk blending.}

  \notecue{Next}{Inspect real Diffusion Policy rollouts on Push-T to see how iterative denoising commits to one valid mode.}
}
\end{frame}

  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%

  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{1.5 min}{Optional}%
  Equation 2 summarizes the Bellman recursion uniting RL and optimal control: action value $Q^\pi(s,a)$ sums immediate reward plus discounted expected future value, state value $V^\pi(s)$ averages $Q^\pi$ under policy $\pi$, and advantage $A^\pi(s,a) = Q^\pi(s,a) - V^\pi(s)$ tests whether action $a$ beats the policy's typical choice. On the latch, a fast plunge has positive advantage only if the chosen reward weights value speed over force. Control engineers recognize $-V$ as cost-to-go solved analytically via the Riccati equation for LQR, whereas RL fits $Q$ and $V$ from samples when dynamics are unknown.
