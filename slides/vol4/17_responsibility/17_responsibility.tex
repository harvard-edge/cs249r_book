\begin{frame}{Oversight needs time, information, and authority}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \centering\safeimg[width=\textwidth,height=3.6cm,keepaspectratio]{uber-test-vehicle.jpg}\\[0.05cm]
      {\footnotesize An Uber self-driving test vehicle. In Tempe in 2018, its backup operator answered in court.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \small A role is oversight a person can perform only if its holder has\\[0.12cm]
      \textbf{\textcolor{mlsysaccent}{time}} to act before the hazard arrives,\\
      \textbf{\textcolor{mlsysaccent}{information}} about what the machine is doing, and\\
      \textbf{\textcolor{mlsysaccent}{authority}} to change it.\\[0.2cm]
      A role that fails any of the three moves blame onto its holder.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \mlsyscredit{Photo: foam from Elgaland Vargaland, CC BY-SA 2.0, Wikimedia Commons (not the Tempe vehicle). Maricopa County Attorney 2023; Elish 2019; Responsible Physical AI, \S17.3, Definition 17.1.}
  \note{
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  Define a human oversight role (Definition 17.1; matching Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven, 2018 on meaningful human control): any duty relied on by the safety case is performable only if its holder has time before the hazard arrives, accurate information about machine state, and authority to alter motion. Failing any one shifts blame onto the operator without protecting anyone. Recall Lecture 1's 2018 Tempe crash (illustrated by an Uber test vehicle of the same class): the backup operator answered in court and pleaded guilty to endangerment in 2023, exemplifying Elish's (2019) moral crumple zone, absorbing blame to shield system design.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Placing a person in the loop never makes an unsafe design responsible unless all three conditions hold.}

  \notecue{Next}{Students now diagnose whether our remote assistant passes the time test at the warehouse rack end.}
}
\end{frame}

Worker assessment requires a separate declared system and owner.
