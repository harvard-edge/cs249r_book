\begin{frame}{A takeover moves the right to propose; the check stays}
  \centering
  \safeimg[width=0.98\textwidth,height=5.3cm,keepaspectratio]{propose_admit.pdf}\\[0.02cm]
  {\footnotesize Token: the right to propose on a channel. MCU: the safety microcontroller.}
  \mlsyscredit{Supervisory Intervention, \S14.2, table 1 and Definition 14.1.}
  \note{
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  Contrast safety enforcement (left) against supervisory intervention (right) across Lecture 1's proposal boundary and Lecture 4 and 12's safety MCU enforcer. During enforcement, the learned policy keeps proposing while the enforcer alters any unsafe proposal. During intervention, the policy's proposal token is revoked on named channels and granted to an authenticated operator, yet every manual command passes through the exact same 1~kHz enforcer. State the chapter's rule plainly: enforcement changes a proposal and leaves the proposer in place; intervention changes the proposer and leaves the check in place. If a startled operator commands a rollover turn, the enforcer clamps or rejects it just like a policy command.

  \notecue{Watch for}{A takeover grants only the right to propose, never direct actuator access; the third law governs people and models alike, and a local stop must act without waiting for the operator.}

        Jerk limit $j_{\max} = 7.11$~m/s$^3$\\
