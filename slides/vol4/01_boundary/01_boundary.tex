  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  First, an aborted database transaction rolls back cleanly to its initial state $S_0$, which is why retry works in software. Second, a moving machine has no prior physical state to restore: applied torque moves it from $S_0$ to $S_1$, and braking carries it to a new state $S_2$ with heated windings, friction loss, and potentially a deformed fixture or broken gear tooth (right). Recovery is always a new forward trajectory bounded by actuator torque, friction, and speed limits, so the permission path must verify before admitting an action that a feasible recovery still exists from the resulting state. Which physical margin governs depends on the task: stopping clearance for the base, contact force for an arm, or winding heat for a held load.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Driving a joint back to its initial coordinates does not reverse heat dissipation, mechanical wear, or plastic deformation.}

\begin{frame}{Stopping takes travel during the delay plus braking}
  \centering
  {\normalsize $\StoppingDistance = \underbrace{\SpeedAtDetection\PrebrakeDelay}_{\text{travel during delay}} + \underbrace{\dfrac{\SpeedAtDetection^2}{2\CredibleBrakingDeceleration}}_{\text{travel while braking}} \le \ClearDistance$}\qquad
  {\scriptsize\begin{tabular}[b]{@{}l@{}}Card: $\SpeedAtDetection$ = 1.5 m/s, $\CredibleBrakingDeceleration$ = 2 m/s$^2$,\\ $\ClearDistance$ = 1.10 m (illustrative); $\PrebrakeDelay$ = 133.6 ms (derived)\end{tabular}}\\[0.1cm]
  {\small Each millisecond of delay costs \textbf{1.5 mm}.}\\[0.05cm]
  \only<1|handout:0>{\mlsyskitfig[width=\textwidth,height=4.25cm,keepaspectratio]{budget_bar_step1}}%
  \only<2-|handout:1>{\mlsyskitfig[width=\textwidth,height=4.25cm,keepaspectratio]{budget_bar_step2}}
  \mlsyscredit{\S1.4.2, equation 1, Napkin Math 1.1; kit bar.}
  \note{%

\begin{frame}{Four laws: one physical, three design principles}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}P{5.2cm}P{8.6cm}@{}}
    \textbf{Law (part)} & \textbf{Documented failure} \\\midrule
    \textbf{Irreversibility} (I), physical & Tempe: 18.7 m with braking withheld \\
    \leavevmode\uncover<2->{\textbf{Endogenous data} (II)} & \leavevmode\uncover<2->{ALVINN: no recoveries in its logs} \\
    \leavevmode\uncover<3->{\textbf{Proposal is not permission} (III)} & \leavevmode\uncover<3->{Therac-25: software replaced interlocks} \\
    \leavevmode\uncover<4->{\textbf{Permission must be earned} (IV)} & \leavevmode\uncover<4->{Tempe operator; Cruise moved without fresh evidence} \\\bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\\[0.25cm]
  \uncover<4->{\small Only the first is a law of physics. The book elevates the other three by choice.}
  \mlsyscredit{The Causal Boundary, \S1.10 and table 3; Pomerleau 1991 (\S1.4.1, note 2); Leveson and Turner 1993; UL 4600 (2023).}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  Reveal the four laws one per part, linking each to its established engineering lineage and documented failure. First, Irreversibility (Part I, stopping distance): physical work cannot be rolled back, as Tempe's 18.7 m braking hold illustrates. Second, Endogenous data (Part II, covariate shift and compounding error, Ross et al. 2011): a machine's actions select its next observations, so ALVINN's centered logs contained no recoveries (Pomerleau 1991; Lecture 5). Third, Proposal is not permission (Part III, Simplex runtime assurance, Sha 2001): Therac-25 overdosed patients when software replaced hardware interlocks (Leveson and Turner 1993). Fourth, Permission must be earned (Part IV, assurance cases and ODD, UL 4600): machines may run only where evidence supports them, unlike Tempe's unmonitored takeover and Cruise's move without fresh evidence.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Only the first law follows from mechanics and thermodynamics. Never call laws two through four laws of nature; teams can violate them, at a predictable engineering cost.}

  \notecue{Next}{Preview the complete chain of machine-checkable records that operationalizes these four laws across the term.}
}
\end{frame}
