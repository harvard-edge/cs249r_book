  \mlsyscredit{Photos: ALOHA 2 Team, arXiv:2405.02292, figure 1 (top); Sayanesy, CC0, Wikimedia Commons. Claim: The Causal Boundary, \S1.2.}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{3 min}{Core}%
  Beyond fenced arms and warehouse routing, learned models now generate robot motion directly. Between 2023 and 2025, research groups showed that models trained broadly on web images and text, called pretrained models, can be fine-tuned, or adapted, into vision-language-action models that emit motion commands. Collecting demonstrations relies on platforms like Google DeepMind's 2024 ALOHA 2 two-arm teleoperation workcells, low-cost robot stations where operators remotely guide arms, targeting bodies like the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. When a text model is uncertain, it writes a plausible sentence; when an arm emits a merely plausible motion, it can strike a fixture or a person.

  \notecue{Watch for}{High scores on offline image or text tests do not guarantee fast enough execution on a robot's onboard processor. Lecture 3 tests that limit through hardware latency, the time to compute each action. Lecture 1 traces how learned outputs moved from perception, meaning interpreting sensor readings, down to motors over sixty years.}

}

  \mlsyscredit{Photos: PEO ACWA (robotic operating area, Pueblo plant), CC BY 2.0; W.carter (IKEA Torp warehouse aisle), CC0; Wikimedia Commons.}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  A steel fence solves robot safety by exclusion, whereas sharing a floor with people removes that barrier. Inside a fenced work area, industrial arms repeat motions programmed in advance while physical barriers keep people outside their reach. Down an open aisle, by contrast, a warehouse mobile manipulator shares the floor with workers while a learned model selects motions that no engineer scripted beforehand. A machine belongs to physical AI when a learned model's output moves physical mass without a person approving every command. Internal stopping checks and operational evidence must then replace the fence.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Being a robot does not make a machine physical AI. The fenced cell holds industrial robots with no learned authority over their motion; Lecture 1 establishes the exact three-part scope test that decides which machines belong to physical AI.}

  \notecue{Next}{Break a shared-space mobile manipulator into its three functional subsystems, the body, the brain, and the nervous system.}
}

  Replacing a fence splits the machine into three subsystems, illustrated by the ER-FLEX mobile manipulator's two commercial units.
