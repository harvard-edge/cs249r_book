\begin{frame}{At 0.50 m/s the delay chain still sets most of the stop}
  \centering
  \mlsyssteps[width=0.92\textwidth,height=4.0cm,keepaspectratio]{cell_stop_step}{2}\\[0.15cm]
  \uncover<2->{\small Delay travel falls 3$\times$ with speed; braking falls 9$\times$. Kinetic energy falls from 13.5 J to 1.5 J.}
  \mlsyscredit{Slide variant of \S18.2.2, figure 2c. Derived; moving mass 12 kg (illustrative).}
  \note{
  \mlsysnotemeta{1 min}{Optional}%
  To keep margin below that speed ceiling, test a restricted 0.50~m/s mode against the nominal 1.50~m/s run, where 96~mm of delay travel plus 75~mm of braking reached 171~mm against the 60~mm stop line. Reducing speed to a third cuts linear delay travel $3\times$ to 32~mm and quadratic braking $9\times$ to 8.3~mm, bringing the stop to 40.3~mm with 19.7~mm remaining short of the line. \begin{frame}{A shield lowers a hazard rate only by its tested coverage}
  \centering
  \vspace{0.3cm}
  {\Large $\lambda_{\text{sys}} \le \underbrace{\lambda_h}_{\text{hazardous proposals}}\,\big(1 - \underbrace{c_h}_{\text{coverage}}\big) + \underbrace{\lambda_{\text{cc}}}_{\text{common cause}}$}\\[0.5cm]
  \small\raggedright
  \uncover<2->{Rates per operating hour; $c_h$ = probability the shield intercepts a hazardous proposal of class $h$.\\[0.15cm]}
  \uncover<3->{\textcolor{conseq}{Fleet rates already include the shield, so multiplying them by $(1 - c_h)$ counts it twice.}\\[0.1cm]
  \textcolor{conseq}{A film the shield cannot sense earns no credit.}}
  \mlsyscredit{Engineering Physical AI, \S18.3.2, equation 2 (shield dilution).}
  \note{
  \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Core}%
  Before deciding whether to invest in a stronger shield or a new sensor, price how much risk an independent shield removes. Equation 2 states the shield dilution bound, $\lambda_{\text{sys}} \le \lambda_h(1 - c_h) + \lambda_{\text{cc}}$, with all rates per operating hour. Its three terms are the hourly hazardous proposal rate $\lambda_h$, the tested interception coverage $c_h$ for hazard class $h$, and the hardware or common-cause rate $\lambda_{\text{cc}}$. Perfect coverage still leaves $\lambda_{\text{cc}}$, and zero coverage passes every hazardous proposal through. Two rules guard against misuse. First, observed fleet rates already include every active shield, so multiplying them by $1 - c_h$ double-counts protection. Second, hazards invisible to the shield, such as clear oil or fatigued latches, earn zero credit.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Shared occlusions can blind both the policy and the shield simultaneously, so $c_h$ must be measured specifically on states where the policy errs.}

  \notecue{Next}{Apply this bound in Napkin Math 18.3 to test whether a 0.9999 shield makes proposer error rates irrelevant.}
}
\end{frame}
