\begin{frame}{Two allowances bring the stop to 912.9 mm of 1,100}

  \only<1|handout:0>{\mlsyskitfig[width=0.95\textwidth,height=3.9cm,keepaspectratio]{budget_bar_step2}}%

  \uncover<3->{\footnotesize Body-owned terms: \textbf{742.5 mm}. At \textbf{2 m/s} the same delay needs \textbf{\textcolor{figred}{1,417.2 mm}}, 317.2 mm past the person.}
  \mlsyscredit{The Physical Body, eq.~2, Napkin Math 2.2; kit bar.}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  First, recall Lecture 1's 762.9 mm bar. Second, equation 2 adds two fixed spatial allowances: 50 mm for localization uncertainty and 100 mm for protective clearance that must remain empty at rest, bringing total stopping distance to 912.9 mm (187.1 mm short of 1,100 mm, derived). Third, isolate the body-owned terms: 562.5 mm braking, 150 mm allowances, and 30 mm during 20 ms brake onset sum to 742.5 mm, leaving 357.5 mm for all upstream delays (including Lecture 4's chunk lease). At 2 m/s, braking jumps from 562.5 to 1,000 mm while delay travel rises from 200.4 to 267.2 mm, pushing the stop to 1,417.2 mm, 317.2 mm past the worker.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Although mass cancels from $v^2/(2a_{\text{brake}})$ for a fixed deceleration, heavier payload directly lowers the credible deceleration $a_{\text{brake}}$ the drives and floor can sustain.}

  \notecue{Next}{Quantify how many milliseconds of extra delay that jump from 1.5 to 2 m/s corresponds to.}

First, an obstacle maneuver triggers peak inference and hard acceleration together: 68.6 A for drives plus 1.4 A for a 60 W processor, totaling 70 A. Second, across 60 m$\Omega$ of cell, harness, and switch impedance, 70 A drops 4.2 V, pulling a full pack to 49.8 V and a depleted pack to 37.8 V, just 4.8 V above lockout (a 150 A step exhausts all 9 V, derived). Third, this calculation screens only settled voltage; inductive switching transients dip deeper.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Cell internal resistance rises with cold temperatures and aging, deepening sag over a robot's life. Testing compute and motors separately misses coincident steps, and sharing this rail with the safety microcontroller risks resetting protection while the base is moving.}

  \notecue{Next}{Check the opposite rail hazard: where the base's 414 J of braking energy goes when a full battery refuses charge.}
}
