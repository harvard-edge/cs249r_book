  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{1.5 min}{Core}%
  Counting observation age starts inside the sensor itself, because one camera frame is not one instant. In the rolling-shutter photograph of a Dash 8 aircraft propeller, pixel rows read out sequentially while the blades turn. Across the pipeline, four boundary events bound age from sensing to motion. Capture occurs when photons strike pixels or magnetic flux sweeps a Hall magnetic-field sensor, dispatch when perception publishes dated evidence, admission when the permission path accepts a proposal, and onset when stator current begins accelerating the joint. Physical error accumulates across the entire capture-to-onset span. Subtracting capture time from current time is valid only when the camera, host, and drives share one hardware-synchronized clock under the IEEE 802.1AS timing standard.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Observation age does not stop when neural inference finishes. Transfer, admission, bus cycles, and current rise all keep aging the measurement before torque appears. Lecture 8 builds observation age stage by stage.}

  \notecue{Next}{Break down a full camera-to-joint pipeline to see how much of total age actually comes from neural inference.}
}
\end{frame}

  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2 min}{Optional}%
  Enforcing a freshness deadline fails if software discards the first stages before counting age, as shown in this real excerpt from ROS 2's \texttt{cam2image.cpp} (the publish-timestamp anti-pattern). \begin{frame}{Two allowances bring the stop to 912.9 mm of 1,100}
  \centering
  {\small $\dstop = v\,\taudelay + \dfrac{v^2}{2\abrake} + \dloc + \dmargin \;\le\; \Dclear$}\\[0.05cm]
  \only<1|handout:0>{\mlsyskitfig[width=0.95\textwidth,height=3.9cm,keepaspectratio]{budget_bar_step2}}%
  \only<2-|handout:1>{\mlsyskitfig[width=0.95\textwidth,height=3.9cm,keepaspectratio]{budget_bar_step3}}\\[-0.2cm]
  \uncover<3->{\footnotesize Body-owned terms: \textbf{742.5 mm}. At \textbf{2 m/s} the same delay needs \textbf{\textcolor{figred}{1,417.2 mm}}, 317.2 mm past the person.}
  \mlsyscredit{The Physical Body, eq.~2, Napkin Math 2.2; kit bar.}
  \note{%
  \mlsysnotemeta{2.5 min}{Core}%
  Lecture 1's 762.9 mm stop assumed exact position knowledge and zero final clearance. Equation 2 appends two fixed allowances, 50 mm for localization uncertainty and 100 mm for protective clearance that must stay empty at rest, raising the stop to 912.9 mm and leaving 187.1 mm before the 1,100 mm limit (derived). Separating the body-owned terms, 562.5 mm of braking, 150 mm of allowances, and 30 mm during 20 ms of brake onset sum to 742.5 mm, leaving 357.5 mm for upstream delays including the chunk lease that Lecture 4 sizes. At 2 m/s, braking jumps from 562.5 mm to 1,000 mm and delay travel from 200.4 mm to 267.2 mm, reaching 1,417.2 mm, or 317.2 mm past the person.

  \notecue{Watch for}{Although mass cancels from $v^2/(2a_{\text{brake}})$ for a fixed deceleration, heavier payload lowers the credible deceleration $a_{\text{brake}}$ the drives and floor can sustain.}

  \notecue{Next}{Convert that jump from 1.5 to 2 m/s into an equivalent number of milliseconds of extra delay.}
