Q: What does Amdahl's Law state for systems optimization?
A: Amdahl's Law states that total speedup is limited by the fraction of execution time occupied by the un-optimized serial portion.

Q: What is knowledge distillation?
A: Knowledge distillation trains a compact student model to match the softened probability distribution produced by a larger teacher model.

Q: What is Adam optimizer?
A: Adam computes individual adaptive learning rates for each parameter using running estimates of first and second gradient moments.

Q: What does Stochastic Gradient Descent do?
A: SGD updates parameters by taking a step in the negative direction of the loss gradient scaled by the learning rate.

Q: Why do we perform warmup runs during benchmarking?
A: Warmup runs prime CPU and GPU instruction caches and trigger frequency scaling before measuring steady-state performance.

Q: What was Vaswani's 2017 Transformer milestone?
A: Vaswani et al. showed that attention mechanisms alone can replace recurrent and convolutional layers for sequence modeling.

Q: Why is memory offset important for tensor slicing?
A: Memory offset identifies the starting memory address of a sliced sub-tensor within the original storage buffer.

Q: How does temperature affect text sampling?
A: Lower temperature concentrates probability mass on high-confidence tokens, while higher temperature flattens distribution to encourage diversity.

Q: What is fan-in in weight initialization?
A: Fan-in is the number of input connections entering a layer neuron.

Q: What is the KV cache?
A: The KV cache stores precomputed key and value vectors in memory across generation steps, reducing per-token decode complexity to O(S).

Q: What does a Sequential container do?
A: Sequential cascades multiple layers together, passing the output of each layer directly as input to the next.

Q: What is the derivative of cross-entropy with softmax?
A: The gradient simplifies cleanly to predicted probabilities minus one-hot target indicators.

Q: How does momentum accelerate gradient descent?
A: Momentum adds an exponentially decaying moving average of past gradients to dampen oscillations and accelerate through plateaus.

Q: What constitutes a single training step?
A: A training step computes forward predictions, calculates loss, zeroes gradients, runs backward propagation, and steps the optimizer.

Q: What is a 2D convolution in neural networks?
A: A 2D convolution slides a small learnable filter across spatial dimensions, computing dot products to detect local features.

Q: How does transpose work without copying memory?
A: Transpose creates a new view by swapping shape dimensions and stride values while pointing to the same underlying data.

Q: What does Mean Squared Error measure?
A: Mean Squared Error measures the average squared difference between predictions and target labels.
