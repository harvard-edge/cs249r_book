Q: What does the Roofline model tell us?

Q: What does CrossEntropyLoss measure?
A: CrossEntropyLoss measures the negative log-likelihood of the true class under predicted probability distributions.

Q: What is multi-head attention?
A: Multi-head attention projects queries, keys, and values into multiple subspaces, allowing the model to attend to information at different positions simultaneously.

Q: What is TinyGPT?
A: TinyGPT is a decoder-only autoregressive transformer that predicts the probability distribution of the next token given preceding context.

Q: What is an epoch in machine learning?
A: An epoch represents one complete pass through the entire training dataset.

Q: Why do we use Kaiming initialization?
A: Kaiming initialization scales weights by the square root of two divided by fan-in to keep activation variance stable across ReLU layers.

Q: What is teacher forcing?
A: Teacher forcing feeds ground-truth previous tokens into all sequence positions simultaneously during training, enabling parallel gradient computation.

Q: What is GELU activation?
A: GELU weights inputs by their probability under a Gaussian distribution, providing smooth non-linear gating.

Q: How does SVD low-rank compression work?
A: Singular Value Decomposition factors a weight matrix into two smaller rank-r matrices, reducing parameters when rank r is sufficiently small.

Q: What is the difference between Adam and AdamW?
A: AdamW decouples L2 weight decay from gradient moment updates, applying weight shrinkage directly to parameter values.

Q: What was Rumelhart's 1986 breakthrough?
A: Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams demonstrated that multi-layer perceptrons trained with backpropagation overcome the XOR limitation.

Q: What is a Pre-LN transformer block?
A: A Pre-LN block applies Layer Normalization before self-attention and feed-forward sublayers, creating an unobstructed residual gradient highway.

Q: What is a receptive field?
A: The receptive field is the spatial region of input pixels that contributes to the activation of a specific output neuron.

Q: What is the difference between prefill and decode phases?
A: The prefill phase processes the entire initial prompt in parallel, while the decode phase generates one token at a time sequentially.

Q: What is a backward pass?
A: The backward pass traverses the computational graph in reverse topological order, applying the chain rule to accumulate parameter gradients.

Q: What is kernel fusion?
A: Kernel fusion combines multiple consecutive operations into a single GPU or CPU kernel, eliminating intermediate memory roundtrips to DRAM.

Q: What is arithmetic intensity?
A: Arithmetic intensity is the ratio of floating-point operations performed to bytes transferred from memory, measured in FLOPs per byte.

Q: What does an EmbeddingLayer do?
A: An EmbeddingLayer maps discrete token IDs into continuous, dense latent vectors via an efficient memory lookup table.

Q: What is INT8 quantization?
A: INT8 quantization converts 32-bit floating-point weights and activations into 8-bit integers, reducing memory footprint by four times.

Q: What is overfitting?
A: Overfitting occurs when a model memorizes idiosyncrasies of training samples but fails to generalize to unseen test data.

Q: What are strides in a Tensor?
A: Strides define the number of memory elements to skip in flat DRAM storage to move one position along each dimension.

Q: What was LeCun's 1998 CNN milestone?

Q: What does top-k sampling do?
A: Top-k sampling restricts token candidates to the k highest-probability options, pruning low-probability noise from generation.

Q: What is tokenization?
A: Tokenization converts raw text strings into a sequence of integer token IDs corresponding to a discrete vocabulary.

Q: What is the contract of a Dataset in TinyTorch?
A: A Dataset implements len to return total sample count and getitem to return a single indexed sample.

Q: What is the autoregressive decode bottleneck?
A: Without caching, generating token N requires recomputing key and value vectors for all N minus one previous tokens, causing O(S squared) latency.

Q: What does a DataLoader do?
A: A DataLoader batches individual samples into tensors, shuffles indices across epochs, and handles mini-batch iteration.

Q: What does drop_last do in a DataLoader?
A: Drop last discards the final undersized batch of an epoch if total samples cannot be divided evenly by batch size.

Q: What is memory-bound execution?
A: A memory-bound kernel spends most of its execution time waiting for data transfers between DRAM and on-chip caches rather than computing.

Q: What is the difference between structured and unstructured pruning?
A: Unstructured pruning zeroes out individual weights arbitrarily, while structured pruning removes entire channels or heads to achieve practical hardware speedup.

Q: What causes quantization clipping error?
A: Clipping error occurs when extreme outlier values exceed the representable integer dynamic range and are clamped.

Q: What is a character-level tokenizer?
A: A character-level tokenizer maps each individual character, digit, and whitespace symbol to a unique vocabulary index.

Q: What does the Sigmoid activation function do?
A: Sigmoid squashes input values into a continuous probability range between zero and one.

Q: Why is cache locality critical for matrix multiplication?
A: Organizing matrix operations into 2D tiles that fit inside L1 and L2 caches maximizes data reuse and minimizes memory stalls.

Q: What is a contiguous tensor layout?
A: A contiguous layout stores adjacent elements in consecutive physical memory addresses without stride gaps.

Q: Why do we use the log-sum-exp trick in softmax loss?

Q: What is autograd in TinyTorch?

Q: Why do gradients accumulate with addition?

Q: What does the ReLU activation function do?
A: ReLU outputs the input value directly if positive, and outputs zero if negative.

Q: What is a dead neuron in ReLU networks?
A: A dead neuron receives negative inputs continuously, causing zero gradient and permanently halting weight updates.

Q: What is the Pareto frontier in model deployment?
A: The Pareto frontier represents the optimal trade-off boundary where latency cannot be reduced without degrading model accuracy.
