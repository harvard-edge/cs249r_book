\begin{abstract}
Benchmarking is the primary scientific instrument driving machine-learning systems progress, providing the empirical foundation to evaluate hardware-software trade-offs and diagnose systemic bottlenecks. Industry-standard suites like MLPerf establish this discipline by holding task, dataset, metric, and quality targets constant. However, their massive compute requirements prevent execution on single-node hardware. MLPerf EDU bridges this gap by bringing production-grade benchmark rigor to single-laptop execution through zero-discretion target inheritance and strict admission control. Every workload inherits its quality target from an upstream authority, treating any missed threshold or procedural violation as an invalid run. Executing this portfolio enforces 100\% target evaluation rigor across 14 workloads covering 8 MLCommons domains on consumer laptops. Across 112 DAM Taxonomy ablation runs (Data, Algorithm, Machine), MLPerf EDU transforms systems evaluation into an inspectable practice without compromising the evaluation contract, equipping students and researchers with a rigorous, executable foundation for single-node ML systems benchmarking. All workloads pass their target requirements by default and execute on CPU as the standard reference baseline. The path to production status runs through \mbox{cross-platform replication}, public evidence hosting, dataset-policy decisions, and external review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Historical precedent demonstrates how standard benchmarks transform fields. SPEC CPU~\citep{henning2006} ended the era of uncalibrated instruction-count claims in computer architecture by enforcing standardized execution suites. Similarly, TPC~\citep{poess2000} established audited governance in database systems, demonstrating that rigorous disclosure rules matter as much as workload code. These suites transformed systems evaluation into an inspectable science.

MLPerf~\citep{mattson2020,reddi2020mlperf} extended this discipline to machine learning. Unlike traditional workloads where output correctness is binary and cheap to verify, ML systems present a unique challenge: throughput and latency are fundamentally coupled to statistical task quality (accuracy, BLEU, FID). By fixing non-negotiable quality targets alongside latency measurements, MLPerf established the gold standard for ML systems evaluation.

However, a critical gap exists in ML systems education and research: learners and researchers require an accessible benchmark suite that instills production-grade empirical discipline, but production suites require multi-node GPU clusters, while local educational setups drop quality targets and auditability. To bridge this gap, any accessible ML systems benchmark suite must be governed by five foundational educational and systems principles:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.5pt]
\item \textbf{P1 (Quality-Performance Coupling).} Performance cannot be evaluated in isolation from task quality. Speedups achieved by outputting degraded results or relaxing accuracy targets are invalid, requiring benchmarks to enforce strict quality floors.
\item \textbf{P2 (Reproducibility and Auditability).} Evaluation claims require complete asset lineage and auditability so that results can be independently verified without relying on unvalidated self-reporting.
\item \textbf{P3 (Microarchitectural Fidelity).} Benchmarks must expose genuine systems bottlenecks—such as memory bandwidth limits and execution stalls—on accessible single-node hardware rather than relying on synthetic toy operators.
\item \textbf{P4 (Statistical Variance and Determinism).} Evaluators must account for measurement stability, recognizing when executions are deterministic and when training variability requires multi-seed statistical aggregation.
\item \textbf{P5 (Multi-Dimensional Trade-Offs).} Systems evaluation extends beyond chasing a single performance number, training evaluators to navigate trade-offs across data scale, algorithmic choices, and machine hardware boundaries.
\end{itemize}

Guided directly by these five governing principles, we developed \textbf{MLPerf EDU}, an accessible, production-derived ML systems benchmark suite engineered for single-laptop execution. To achieve comprehensive coverage without compromising benchmark authority, MLPerf EDU packages 14 canonical workloads across 8 core MLCommons domains: Causal Language Modeling (nanoGPT), Text Classification (DistilBERT), Information Retrieval (SBERT), Code Generation (Qwen2.5-Coder), Function Calling (BFCL Qwen3 AST), Graph Node Classification (GCN on \texttt{ogbn-arxiv}), Time-Series Forecasting (PatchTST), Image Classification (ResNet8), Keyword Spotting (DS-CNN), Visual Wake Words (MobileNetV2), Anomaly Detection (Autoencoder), Image Generation (EDM Diffusion), Recommendation (NCF on \texttt{MovieLens-20M}), and Reinforcement Learning (MCTS MiniGo). Every workload binds its input pipeline, runner contract, and upstream target threshold into an inspectable dossier with strict admission control.

Using this artifact, researchers and students execute controlled empirical studies that illuminate non-obvious relationships across the Data (D), Algorithm (A), and Machine (M) taxonomy. For example, while ML practitioners expect GPU hardware acceleration to scale uniformly across models, sparse Graph Neural Networks (GCN) and agentic tool-calling AST parsing show zero GPU speedup ($0.98\times\text{--}1.01\times$) over a standard CPU due to irregular memory gather operations and warp divergence. Conversely, INT8 dynamic quantization halves parameter byte traffic, effectively doubling operational intensity and shifting memory-bound workloads rightward into the compute-bound regime.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Benchmarking is a mature field with well-governed suites, and a new suite must justify its existence against established alternatives. Existing evaluation surfaces generally force a trade-off. Production suites enforce strict quality-controlled evaluation, but require cluster compute or custom physical hardware. Local educational setups fit local budgets, but drop quality targets, ruining comparability. MLPerf EDU bridges this gap by positioning itself against production suites, client/edge benchmarks, microbenchmarks, and educational lab environments.

\textbf{Production and client/edge suites.} The official MLPerf Training and Inference suites established quality-controlled, scenario-aware performance evaluation at production scale~\citep{mattson2020,reddi2020mlperf}. MLPerf Mobile~\citep{ignatov2021mlperf} and MLPerf Tiny~\citep{banbury2021mlperf} apply this methodology to smartphones and microcontrollers, while MLPerf HPC~\citep{farrell2021mlperf} addresses supercomputing clusters. Complementing physical system benchmarks, DataPerf~\citep{mazumder2023dataperf} measures data-centric AI algorithms and dataset quality, while AlgoPerf~\citep{dahl2023algoperf} evaluates algorithmic optimizer efficiency. 

A natural question is why existing MLCommons client or edge suites—such as MLPerf Client~\citep{mlcommons2024client}, MLPerf Mobile~\citep{ignatov2021mlperf}, or MLPerf Tiny~\citep{banbury2021mlperf}—cannot simply be repurposed for systems education and research. While MLPerf Client evaluates desktop PC user-experience scenarios and MLPerf Mobile/Tiny target smartphones and microcontrollers, these suites are specialized for specific hardware deployment classes. MLPerf Client focuses on desktop application inference pipelines (e.g., video conferencing, image super-resolution) without covering multi-domain model training, agentic tool-calling, graph neural networks, time-series forecasting, or structured Data-Algorithm-Machine (DAM) trade-off ablations. MLPerf Mobile and Tiny require physical smartphone device farms or custom microcontroller evaluation boards, while their model constraints (sub-megabyte to low-MB) omit modern transformer scaling, autoregressive KV-cache decode, and cross-encoder reranking. Conversely, MLPerf Training and Inference require multi-GPU cloud clusters and multi-gigabyte dataset downloads. MLPerf EDU inherits MLPerf's rigorous discipline while targeting locally executable, single-node evaluation. \Cref{tab:comparison} summarizes this positioning feature matrix.

\textbf{Educational lab setups.} Local educational setups and framework-building courses, such as MiniTorch~\citep{rush2021}, TinyGrad, or educational implementations, fit local budgets and make tensors, operators, and automatic differentiation inspectable. However, they typically drop quality targets, sacrificing comparability. A classroom result that executes quickly but silently fails its accuracy target is not a valid baseline. MLPerf EDU provides this missing discipline. By using PyTorch~\citep{paszke2019pytorch} for its standard execution path, the suite decouples its specification from any custom educational framework.

\textbf{Microbenchmarks.} DAWNBench introduced time-to-accuracy, connecting system performance to a fixed task-quality threshold~\citep{coleman2019}. TorchBench provides broad PyTorch framework regression coverage~\citep{ansel2024torchbench}. MLPerf EDU shares the quality-first rule of DAWNBench and the executable PyTorch surface of TorchBench, but targets fixed classroom exercises with inherited thresholds rather than leaderboards or framework regressions.

\subsection{Benchmark Requirements}

To bridge the gap between production cluster benchmarks and unvalidated educational scripts, a single-node ML systems benchmark suite must fulfill five core requirements:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{R1 (Zero-Discretion Target Inheritance):} Quality targets must be inherited directly from upstream standards (MLCommons or literature) without local downward calibration or discretionary relaxation.
\item \textbf{R2 (Single-Laptop Envelope Fit):} Workloads must execute to completion within standard consumer hardware constraints (x86 CPU, CUDA, MPS GPU, $\le 16\text{ GB}$ UMA RAM, $\le 190\text{ MB}$ dataset downloads) without cluster dependencies.
\item \textbf{R3 (Microarchitectural Fidelity):} Workloads must preserve real systems bottlenecks (Compute-Bound vs DRAM Memory-Bandwidth Bound) rather than relying on uncalibrated toy operators.
\item \textbf{R4 (Strict Admission Control):} Performance claims are admitted only if task quality ($G_{\text{sem}}$), scenario protocols ($G_{\text{protocol}}$), asset provenance ($G_{\text{provenance}}$), and host stability ($G_{\text{state}}$) are 100\% satisfied; shortfalls trigger hard rejection.
\item \textbf{R5 (Multi-Lens Pedagogical Trade-Offs):} The suite must support structured evaluation across Data (D), Algorithm (A), and Machine (M) taxonomy dimensions linked to curriculum learning goals.
\end{enumerate}

No existing benchmark surface satisfies all five requirements simultaneously (\Cref{tab:comparison}): production suites (MLPerf Train/Inf) enforce R1, R3, and R4 but violate R2; client/mobile/tiny suites violate R2 (device dependencies) and R5; educational lab scripts satisfy R2 but omit R1, R3, R4, and R5; microbenchmarks (TorchBench) omit R1 and R4. MLPerf EDU is engineered to fulfill all five requirements for single-node ML systems evaluation.

\subsection{Language, Code, and Agentic Workloads}
\label{sec:suite_language}

Language and retrieval workloads represent the dominant computational footprint in modern ML systems, spanning autoregressive text generation, bidirectional encoder representations, cross-encoder document reranking, and agentic tool invocation. Rather than viewing language models merely as statistical text generators, MLPerf EDU structures these workloads to expose the fundamental shift from compute-bound prompt prefill to memory-bandwidth-bound autoregressive decoding, alongside the control-flow challenges introduced by structured agent execution.

\textbf{Causal language modeling.} Character-level text generation using nanoGPT~\citep{karpathy2023nanogpt} on \texttt{tinyshakespeare}~\citep{eldan2023tinystories} serves as the foundational probe for transformer inference dynamics. During prompt prefill, batching matrix multiplications saturates parallel execution units. However, once the model transitions to single-token autoregressive generation, memory traffic dominates. Every token generated requires loading the complete model weight matrix and reading past key-value activations from DRAM, reducing operational intensity to less than $1.0\text{ FLOP/byte}$. Students observe firsthand how key-value caching prevents redundant sequence recomputation while converting execution into a memory-bandwidth-bound workload evaluated against an inherited cross-entropy loss threshold $\le 1.4700$.

\textbf{Text classification.} Sentiment analysis using DistilBERT~\citep{sanh2019distilbert} on GLUE SST-2~\citep{socher2013sst2} captures fixed-window bidirectional encoder inference. Unlike autoregressive models, bidirectional attention processes the entire sequence in a single contiguous pass without dynamic state retention. The workload illustrates how model distillation reduces transformer parameter footprints by $40\%$ while preserving $97\%$ of BERT's language understanding performance. Evaluated against an inherited accuracy threshold $\ge 91.06\%$, it provides a clean baseline for dense tensor dispatch on single-node hardware.

\textbf{Information retrieval.} Document reranking using MiniLM-L6-v2~\citep{reimers2019sbert} on NanoBEIR~\citep{thakur2021beir} models multi-query candidate scoring loops in production search pipelines. In information retrieval systems, bi-encoders rapidly retrieve candidate documents, after which a cross-encoder performs joint self-attention across query-document pairs to compute fine-grained relevance scores. This workload exercises nested batching loops and pairwise matrix multiplications, evaluating retrieval precision against an inherited mean nDCG@10 threshold $\ge 60.72\%$.

\textbf{Code generation.} Automated Python function synthesis using Qwen2.5-Coder~\citep{yang2024qwen25} on HumanEval~\citep{chen2021humaneval} introduces execution safety and sandboxed code evaluation. Benchmarking code generation extends beyond string matching; valid code must be executed inside isolated Docker or Subprocess containers to evaluate unit-test correctness under pass@1 scoring. This workload teaches containerized execution management, resource isolation, and sandboxed test harness design against an inherited functional correctness threshold $\ge 57.30\%$.

\textbf{Function calling and agentic tool use.} Structured tool invocation using Qwen3~\citep{yan2024bfcl} on the Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard AST evaluation split models the mechanics of LLM agents. The workload requires the model to generate syntactically valid Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs) that match formal JSON function signatures. Unlike free-form text generation, agentic function calling involves dynamic syntax validation and branch evaluation. On parallel hardware backends, non-deterministic token branching triggers warp divergence, where execution threads within a SIMD warp take different control-flow paths. Evaluated against an inherited AST accuracy threshold $\ge 82.92\%$, this workload illustrates how agentic control flow impacts hardware efficiency.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks and Temporal Dynamics}
\label{sec:suite_graph}

Graph neural networks and time-series forecasting represent non-Euclidean spatial topologies and multivariate temporal correlations, challenging traditional dense GEMM hardware accelerators with irregular memory access and long-context patch correlations.

\textbf{Graph node classification.} Node property prediction using a 3-layer Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)~\citep{kipf2017gcn} on \texttt{ogbn-arxiv}~\citep{hu2020ogb} demonstrates the severe systems bottlenecks created by irregular graph structure. Standard neural networks operate on contiguous 2D or 4D tensor buffers that map efficiently to SIMD vector registers and GPU warps. In contrast, graph neural networks rely on sparse gather and scatter operations to aggregate feature messages across graph edges. On the 169,343-node citation network of \texttt{ogbn-arxiv}, these irregular index lookups fragment cache lines, thrash L2 caches, and bottleneck on DRAM latency. As a result, GPU acceleration yields near $1.0\times$ speedup over CPU execution, exposing students to non-Euclidean memory thrashing evaluated against an inherited test accuracy threshold $\ge 71.74\%$.

\textbf{Time-series forecasting.} Multivariate temporal forecasting using PatchTST~\citep{nie2023patchtst} on the ETTm1 electricity transformer dataset addresses long-context sequence modeling in physical systems. Traditional time-series models process individual timestamps, leading to high computational complexity and noise sensitivity. PatchTST introduces channel-independent patch extraction, grouping adjacent timestamps into token patches before applying self-attention across time. This design reduces attention matrix complexity from $O(L^2)$ to $O((L/P)^2)$ while creating contiguous memory buffers that restore high operational intensity. Evaluated against an inherited test Mean Squared Error (MSE) threshold $\le 0.2900$, this workload demonstrates how domain-specific tensor representations restore hardware efficiency on sequence data.

\subsection{Vision, Audio, and Generative Edge Systems}
\label{sec:suite_vision}

Computer vision, audio processing, and embedded edge models capture the spectrum from ultra-constrained microcontroller execution to heavy iterative generative samplers, illustrating convolution dispatch, depthwise separable operators, audio spectrogram processing, and sub-megabyte memory boundaries.

\textbf{Image classification.} Object recognition using ResNet8~\citep{he2016resnet} on CIFAR-10 serves as the primary benchmark for spatial 2D convolutions and residual skip connections. Residual networks solve the vanishing gradient problem by adding identity shortcut connections across convolutional layers. Compute-bound 3x3 GEMM convolutions dominate execution, allowing parallel hardware units to saturate arithmetic pipelines. Evaluated against an inherited top-1 accuracy threshold $\ge 85.00\%$, ResNet8 provides an inspectable baseline for spatial convolution dispatch and memory layout optimizations on consumer processors.

\textbf{Keyword spotting.} Spoken word recognition using Depthwise Separable CNN (DS-CNN) on SpeechCommands~\citep{warden2018speech} models real-time audio keyword detection. Before neural execution, raw audio waveforms are transformed into 2D Mel-frequency cepstral coefficient (MFCC) spectrograms. DS-CNN processes these spectrograms by factorizing standard 2D convolutions into spatial depthwise convolutions followed by 1x1 point-wise convolutions, reducing parameter counts and FLOP requirements by over $80\%$. Evaluated against an inherited top-1 accuracy threshold $\ge 90.00\%$, this workload teaches front-end audio signal processing and depthwise operator efficiency.

\textbf{Visual wake words.} Person detection using MobileNetV2 on Visual Wake Words (VWW) captures duty-cycled vision execution on edge devices. MobileNetV2 uses inverted residual blocks and linear bottlenecks to constrain memory footprint within sub-megabyte SRAM boundaries. In edge deployments, models must execute within strict power budgets, running periodically (duty-cycling) rather than continuously. Evaluated against an inherited top-1 accuracy threshold $\ge 80.00\%$, this workload exposes students to sub-megabyte memory bounds and low-latency edge inference constraints.

\textbf{Anomaly detection.} Industrial machine condition monitoring using a Feature Autoencoder on ToyADMOS models unsupervised acoustic quality control. Industrial sensors monitor factory machinery by detecting deviations from normal operational sounds. The autoencoder compresses input acoustic features through a narrow bottleneck layer before reconstructing the original signal. Abnormal machine behavior increases reconstruction error, serving as the anomaly score. Evaluated against an inherited ROC AUC threshold $\ge 0.8500$, this workload teaches feature reconstruction error metrics and bottleneck representation learning.

\textbf{Image generation.} Unconditional image synthesis using EDM diffusion~\citep{karras2022edm} on CIFAR-10 represents the heavy computational demands of generative AI. Unlike single-pass inference models, diffusion models generate images by iteratively sampling from a continuous stochastic differential equation (SDE) or ordinary differential equation (ODE) over 50 numerical solver steps. Each step executes a full UNet forward pass containing multi-head cross-attention and spatial convolutions, resulting in tens of GFLOPs per generated image. Evaluated against an inherited Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) threshold $\le 1.7900$ calculated over 50,000 generated samples, this workload exposes students to iterative sampler stepping and high-throughput GEMM compute saturation.

\subsection{Recommendation and Search-Coupled Systems}
\label{sec:suite_recsys}

Recommendation systems and reinforcement learning capture high-cardinality sparse embedding table lookups and tree-search-guided dual policy-value network inference, representing critical production workloads in web services and decision-making AI.

\textbf{Recommendation systems.} Collaborative filtering rating prediction using Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF)~\citep{he2017ncf} on MovieLens-20M~\citep{harper2015movielens} captures the dual memory and compute bottlenecks of personalization pipelines. In production recommendation systems, user and item identifiers are mapped through massive embedding tables containing millions of high-dimensional vectors. Looking up sparse user and item embeddings creates uncoalesced memory reads across DRAM. Once retrieved, the embedding vectors are concatenated and passed through dense multi-layer perceptron (MLP) layers to predict user preference scores. Evaluated against an inherited \texttt{hit\_rate@10} threshold $\ge 0.6350$, this workload demonstrates negative sampling strategies and high-cardinality sparse embedding table access.

\textbf{Reinforcement learning and search.} Strategic action selection using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) guided dual policy-value networks (MiniGo)~\citep{silver2016alphago} models search-coupled decision making. MiniGo combines deep neural network evaluation with tree search. During game tree traversal, the system executes hundreds of MCTS simulations: selecting tree nodes, expanding leaf states, evaluating policy and value predictions using a dual-head convolutional network, and backpropagating rewards up the search tree. This hybrid architecture creates complex pointer-based memory access and dynamic search tree loops that challenge static graph compilers. Evaluated against an inherited move prediction accuracy threshold $\ge 0.4000$, this workload illustrates search-coupled neural execution, dynamic replay buffers, and dual policy-value heads.

\subsection{Measurement Methodology}
We evaluate all canonical workloads using standard CPU execution as the authoritative baseline, ensuring universal reproducibility across any consumer host platform regardless of GPU availability. In addition, UMA host characterization maps performance when hardware acceleration backends (MPS GPU) are enabled.

Workloads operate under one of two evaluation roles (\Cref{tab:reference-evidence}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Score-bearing workloads (\texttt{Score}):} Evaluated against strict accuracy or quality target thresholds inherited from MLCommons standards (e.g., top-1 accuracy $\ge 85.0\%$, loss $\le 1.470$, mean nDCG@10 $\ge 60.72\%$). Task quality is non-negotiable under admission rules.
\item \textbf{Performance-bearing workloads (\texttt{Perf.}):} Evaluate functional throughput, token generation rate (e.g., prefill and decode tok/s), or latency once functional correctness and schema compliance are verified.
\end{itemize}

To isolate systemic variance, the measurement methodology enforces strict timing controls alongside power and thermal stability guards. A failed or interrupted attempt is retained and cannot be repaired by replacing selected executions. Power source, low power mode, and sleep or wake changes are checked before and after each execution. A changed host state invalidates the attempt even when its task metric passes. Accuracy does not excuse unstable timing or uncontrolled measurement conditions. \Cref{tab:reference-evidence} details the Committed Reference Evidence, establishing baseline CPU performance metrics across the suite.

\textbf{Compute-bound workloads ($I \ge 16.7\text{ FLOPs/B}$).} Workloads right of the knee saturate execution units before exhausting memory bandwidth. LLM Prefill ($I=45.2$), ResNet8 image classification ($I=38.6$), and EDM diffusion image generation ($I=52.1$) exhibit high operational intensity. Their dense matrix multiplications (GEMMs) map efficiently to parallel arithmetic logic units, limited by peak compute capacity ($2.5\text{ TFLOP/s}$) rather than memory bus transfer rates.

\textbf{DRAM memory-bandwidth bound workloads ($I < 16.7\text{ FLOPs/B}$).} Workloads left of the knee stall waiting for memory data along the $150\text{ GB/s}$ slanted DRAM ceiling ($P = 150 \times I\text{ GFLOP/s}$). LLM Decode ($I=0.5\text{ FLOPs/B}$) is constrained by single-token autoregressive KV-cache reads across DRAM. GCN node classification ($I=1.2\text{ FLOPs/B}$) relies on sparse gather/scatter index lookups on \texttt{ogbn-arxiv}, fragmenting memory access and thrashing L2 caches. NCF recommendation ($I=2.1\text{ FLOPs/B}$) depends on high-cardinality sparse embedding lookups on \texttt{MovieLens-20M}, bottlenecking on shared memory bus bandwidth.

\subsection{Determinism and Variance}
A single accepted run decides a quality verdict; timing claims require repeated measurement. This asymmetry requires evaluating quality metric determinism~\citep{bouthillier2019,pham2020}.

Inference workloads demonstrated complete determinism. Running inference workloads under multiple seeds on the default MPS backend and repeating on CPU yielded bit-identical results. The quality metric did not move, exhibiting a 0.0\% coefficient of variation.

Training workloads exhibit significant seed variance, demonstrating why single-run training evaluations are invalid. Sweeping multiple seeds across training workloads showed standard deviations ($\sigma$) of [3.2\%, 6.8\%]. The per-epoch training curves in \Cref{fig:curves} confirm that shortfalls survive longer runs rather than closing with additional epochs. The spread exceeded the margin to the effective threshold, flipping pass or fail verdicts depending on the seed. Graph node classification fell below its tolerance floor under a different seed, while time-series forecasting reached inside its target under a third seed. Training evaluations require multi-seed statistical aggregation rather than single-run acceptance. \Cref{tab:multiseed} details the 5-seed statistical aggregation across all 14 workloads.

\subsection{Principle Fulfillment Summary}
\label{sec:principle_fulfillment}

The empirical findings in \Cref{sec:characterization} and \Cref{sec:dam_eval} directly fulfill the five core educational and systems principles introduced in \Cref{sec:intro}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.5pt]
\item \textbf{P1 (Quality-Performance Coupling).} Verified across all ablation runs (\Cref{tab:quant_summary}). Runs that achieved low latency via aggressive quantization (e.g., INT8 on ResNet8 dropping accuracy below $85.0\%$) triggered hard \texttt{FAIL} admission verdicts, proving that throughput claims are invalid without enforced quality floors.
\item \textbf{P2 (Reproducibility and Auditability).} Verified via cryptographic SHA-256 asset lineage and manifest validation (\texttt{.provd.json}). Every run logs exact hardware state, seed configuration, and checksums, guaranteeing unauthenticated auditability.
\item \textbf{P3 (Microarchitectural Fidelity).} Verified by Roofline analysis (\Cref{fig:roofline}) and CPU vs. MPS hardware telemetry (\Cref{sec:machine_lens}). Single-laptop execution preserves genuine compute-bound and memory-bound bottlenecks, including counter-intuitive zero-speedup phenomena ($0.98\times\text{--}1.01\times$) on irregular workloads (GCN, AST parsing).
\item \textbf{P4 (Statistical Variance \& Determinism Awareness).} Verified by multi-seed statistical telemetry (\Cref{tab:multiseed}). The evaluation quantifies bit-identical inference determinism ($0.0\%$ CV) against stochastic training variance ($\sigma = 3.2\%\text{--}6.8\%$), demonstrating why single-run training benchmarks lead to flawed conclusions.
\item \textbf{P5 (Multi-Dimensional Pareto Trade-Offs).} Verified through the DAM Taxonomy ablations (\Cref{fig:data_lens,fig:algo_lens,fig:cpu_vs_mps}). The results expose non-linear Pareto trade-offs across sample budgets ($D$), precision/optimizer overhead ($A$), and compute device scaling ($M$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

Single-node laptop envelope constraints cannot capture cluster-scale distributed training dynamics~\citep{mattson2020} or datacenter TPU interconnect scaling behavior~\citep{jouppi2017,jouppi2021}. Character-level nanoGPT preserves attention, training, KV-cache construction, and autoregressive decode while omitting production tokenization and data scale. MLPerf Tiny models expose embedded-model structures on a laptop but do not reproduce microcontroller memory limits. Distributed and datacenter-scale claims are outside v0.1.

This paper evaluates the benchmark artifact, not its effect on learning. Executable labs, staged profiles, and reviewable reports establish curricular affordances, but they do not establish learning gains, instructor adoption, or accessibility. A classroom study should measure whether students improve at identifying invalid comparisons, preserving semantic requirements, and defending systems claims.

Future work transitions the project to MLCommons open working group governance. The suite will also explore synthetic workload generation~\citep{chung2023} to reduce dependency on large public datasets while preserving system stress characteristics.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

MLPerf EDU brings benchmarking literacy to a scale that students and individual researchers can practice. By enforcing zero-discretion target inheritance and strict quality admission, the suite brings production-grade rigor to single-node ML systems evaluation. The suite preserves the empirical foundation needed to diagnose bottlenecks and compare systems on constrained hardware.

The complete benchmark suite, open-source repository, interactive lab exercises, and reference documentation are integrated into the ML Systems textbook ecosystem at \url{https://mlsysbook.ai}. Execution bundles are available for local reviewer handoff, enabling cross-platform replication.
