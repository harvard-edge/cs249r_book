\begin{abstract}
Machine-learning systems courses commonly ask students to optimize a model but
rarely require them to defend whether two measurements are comparable.
Production benchmark suites enforce that discipline through fixed tasks, quality
gates, controlled scenarios, system disclosure, and reviewable artifacts. Their
resource and operational requirements, however, place many representative paths
outside a course or individual artifact review. We present \textbf{MLPerf EDU},
an executable benchmark specification for laptop-class teaching and single-node
research. Rather than inventing proxy tasks, the v0.1 registry curates
\RegistryRows{} established workloads across \RegistrySuites{} suites and
retains each upstream model, dataset split, evaluator, and quality reference.
Three profiles separate installation checks, comparable classroom runs, and
controlled research variants without encoding optimizations in workload
identities. The framework admits performance evidence only after semantic,
protocol, provenance, and host-state gates pass. On one Apple M5 Max laptop, all
\ScoreBearingCases{} score-bearing cases meet their inherited quality gates.
Their reference medians span \ScoreReferenceMinSeconds{} seconds to
\ScoreReferenceMaxMinutes{} minutes and total \ScoreReferenceTotalMinutes{}
minutes for one pass through the score-bearing suite. Six cases complete five
fresh-process runs with timing coefficients of variation between
\FiveRunMinCV{} and \FiveRunMaxCV{}; six additional cases remain explicitly
provisional. The result is a locally executable benchmark artifact that
transfers professional measurement discipline to the classroom without claiming
production-scale equivalence. These project measurements are not
MLCommons-verified results. Remaining release work includes
\mbox{cross-platform replication}, public evidence hosting, dataset-policy
decisions, and external review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine-learning systems now span training, serving, retrieval, graph analytics,
time-series forecasting, vision, and embedded inference. Comparing those
systems requires more than timing a script. A benchmark must hold task semantics
and quality constant while recording the model, data, hardware, scenario, and
measurement procedure. MLPerf established this discipline for production
systems~\citep{mattson2020,reddi2020mlperf,banbury2021mlperf}. The same discipline
is valuable in education, yet production-scale workloads and submission
infrastructure often exceed the time, hardware, and operational budget of a
course or an individual artifact review.

The usual educational substitute is a collection of small models. Small models
solve the resource problem, but they do not automatically teach benchmarking.
A student can report a faster run after changing the data, silently weakening
quality, measuring warmup, or using a synthetic shortcut. None of those results
supports a fair systems comparison. The missing learning objective is
\emph{benchmarking literacy}, the ability to construct, inspect, reproduce, and
defend an evidence-backed performance claim.

MLPerf EDU addresses that gap with an executable specification rather than a
static scoreboard. Its scope is locally executable, quality-gated evaluation for
teaching and studying single-node ML systems. The suite supports controlled work
on processors, memory systems, runtimes, compilers, and model execution while
excluding distributed and datacenter-scale claims. Backward design begins with
the activity a student or researcher must perform, such as reproducing a result,
isolating a bottleneck, changing one systems variable, and defending
comparability. Workloads are admitted only when an established task can support
that activity on local hardware without replacing its model, data, evaluator, or
quality gate with a project-defined proxy.

\begin{definition}[Evaluation Tuple]
A benchmark configuration
$\mathcal{S}=(W,D,M,H,S,C,P)$ binds the workload, data, model, hardware,
scenario, constraints, and measurement protocol. It produces
$\mathcal{M}=(Q,L,T,E)$, containing task quality, latency, throughput, and an
energy observation when calibrated. An evidence package $\mathcal{E}$ records
the configuration, measurements, source state, assets, and model lineage. Two
results are comparable only when $\mathcal{E}$ makes every controlled equality
and intentional difference explicit.
\end{definition}

The framework turns the tuple into a fail-closed admission rule. A result $r$ is
admissible only when
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{admit}(r)={}&
G_{\mathrm{sem}}(r)\land G_{\mathrm{protocol}}(r)\\
&{}\land G_{\mathrm{provenance}}(r)\land G_{\mathrm{state}}(r),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the semantic gate is task quality for score-bearing cases and functional
correctness plus model lineage for performance-bearing cases. The remaining
terms check the declared protocol, content-bound evidence, and stable host
conditions. Admissibility and evidence strength are deliberately separate. A
case may pass every gate while remaining provisional until its repeatability
protocol is complete.

The paper makes five contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{An executable benchmark contract.} The evaluation tuple and
admission rule become registry, runner, grading, host-state, and reporting
invariants rather than paper-only guidance.
\item \textbf{A backward-designed workload portfolio.} Nine established
workloads cover language, retrieval, graph learning, time series, vision, and
embedded inference. Five additional proposals are documented and rejected or
deferred instead of being admitted through weaker targets or invented proxies.
\item \textbf{Three execution intents.} The \texttt{min}, \texttt{max}, and
\texttt{pro} profiles separate setup checks, comparable classroom runs, and
research variants while preserving stable workload identities.
\item \textbf{Auditable and lineage-bound evidence.} Runs emit JSON, HTML, CSV,
and provenance manifests that bind source, assets, hardware, metrics, and
training checkpoints.
\item \textbf{Measured laptop references.} Twelve source-locked cases traverse
the public command path. Nine pass inherited quality gates, six establish
five-run timing repeatability, and six remain explicitly provisional.
\end{enumerate}

The goal is not to replace official MLPerf suites or imply MLCommons approval.
It is to make professional evaluation practice inspectable at a scale students
and researchers can run locally. \Cref{fig:architecture} summarizes the system.

\section{Design Principles}
\label{sec:design}
% ============================================================================

Three principles guide MLPerf EDU's design, each addressing a failure mode that
turns a runnable script into an invalid benchmark.

\subsection{Pedagogical Transparency Over Performance}

Production benchmarks optimize for throughput with custom CUDA kernels,
mixed-precision arithmetic, and operator fusion. Those optimizations can make
the computation invisible to students. MLPerf EDU inverts this priority for its
reference workloads. The standard execution path uses inspectable Python and
PyTorch with explicit operations and named dimensions. Thin adapters aim to
preserve the authoritative model and task contract rather than replacing it
with a project-designed approximation. Conformance is audited rather than
assumed. The current keyword-spotting adapter preserves the DS-CNN topology and
quality gate but is prediction-nonidentical to the pinned LiteRT graph, so
promotion is blocked. Students can still inspect its depthwise and pointwise
convolutions as separate \texttt{nn.Conv2d} layers. The reference path is a
readable point of comparison, not a performance ceiling. Optimized kernels and
alternative runtimes belong in recorded \texttt{pro} configurations and remain
subject to the same semantic gate.

\subsection{Real Data With Provenance}

A benchmark without real data teaches stress testing, not systems evaluation.
Students must observe how data distributions affect convergence, accuracy, and
ranking. \Cref{tab:datasets} records every dataset used by a canonical mode,
while the executable registry carries structured dossiers with pinned sources,
sizes, splits, licenses, release status, and digest policy. Fetching and
redistribution are separate decisions. A dataset can support a reproducible
local run while its bytes remain excluded from a public evidence package.

\subsection{Complete Train--Checkpoint--Inference Loop}

Many tools provide only training or only inference. MLPerf EDU preserves the
complete pipeline when model lineage is part of the workload contract. Causal
language-model training produces a quality-approved checkpoint. Full, prefill,
and decode inference load that checkpoint and verify its report, manifest, and
digest before measurement. The current registry records dataset, model,
checkpoint, profile, quality, and result-role metadata in one place, ensuring
deterministic, reproducible experiments. Training, full generation, prefill, and
decode remain modes or phases of one workload. Treating them as separate
workload identifiers would hide their shared task and checkpoint lineage.

\section{Workload Suite}
\label{sec:suite}

The executable registry contains \RegistryRows{} workloads across
\RegistrySuites{} suites. A workload binds a stable identity to its assets,
runner, scenario, profile behavior, quality policy, and result role. Modes,
phases, execution options, and research variations do not create new workload
identities. This registry is the source of truth. The paper imports its counts
and evidence table from a generated snapshot, which prevents prose from drifting
away from executable metadata.

The three profiles describe execution intent. \texttt{min} is a quick
installation and plumbing check. It can use reduced or synthetic inputs and
never supports a public score. \texttt{max} is the standard comparable path with
the declared model, real data, gate, and protocol. \texttt{pro} is the research
envelope for ablations, backend studies, repetitions, and user-controlled
variants. A \texttt{pro} result is comparable only when its recorded
configuration defines the comparison.

These profiles encode execution intent rather than difficulty labels.
\texttt{min} supports installation, pre-lab checks, and continuous integration.
\texttt{max} is the classroom reference contract whose assets, evaluator, and
gate must not change. \texttt{pro} exposes repetitions, backends, compilers, and
ablations for projects or architecture studies. A result from \texttt{pro} is
comparable only within its fully disclosed configuration. This separation keeps
batching, compilation, precision, scheduling, and caching out of workload names.

The registry also separates public interpretation from execution success. The
twelve canonical cases include \ScoreBearingCases{} score-bearing cases and
\PerformanceBearingCases{} performance-bearing inference phases. All nine
workloads remain experimental until external review and governance decide
otherwise. A successful execution can therefore anchor a lab or research
comparison without being presented as an official benchmark score. This
fail-closed distinction is important for small workloads because functional
smoke data and reduced teaching exercises are not quality baselines.

\textbf{Selection principles.} Admission requires five properties. The task must
have a recognizable upstream authority, fit the declared laptop envelope,

\subsection{Language, Retrieval, Graph, and Time-Series Suites}

The language suite contains causal language modeling, text classification, and
information retrieval. The causal workload preserves nanoGPT's Shakespeare
character-level training recipe and exposes training together with full,
prefill, and decode inference under one identity~\citep{karpathy2022nanogpt}.
Training produces the quality-approved checkpoint consumed by all three
inference phases. DistilBERT on the GLUE SST-2 validation split adds encoder
classification~\citep{sanh2019distilbert}. A compact MS MARCO cross-encoder on
the three-dataset NanoBEIR subset adds reranking and retrieval metrics
~\citep{reimers2019sentencebert}.

The graph workload preserves the official three-layer GCN recipe and evaluator
for ogbn-arxiv~\citep{kipf2017semisupervised,hu2020ogb}. It exposes sparse
gather and scatter behavior that dense language and vision workloads do not.
The time-series workload preserves PatchTST training on the official ETTm1
split~\citep{nie2023patchtst}. Its long input window, patch extraction, and
multivariate attention provide a temporal workload without inventing a new
dataset or target.

\subsection{Vision and Tiny Suites}

The vision and Tiny suites inherit four MLPerf Tiny task
contracts~\citep{banbury2021mlperf}. Image
classification preserves the official ResNet8 model and 200-example CIFAR-10
accuracy set. Keyword spotting preserves the DS-CNN model and 1,000-example
speech-command accuracy set~\citep{warden2018speech}. Visual wake words
preserves the MobileNetV1 0.25 model and the official 1,000-example person
detection set. All three report top-1 accuracy against inherited targets.

Machine-sound anomaly detection preserves the ToyCar autoencoder, log-mel
feature recipe, 248-recording accuracy index, and ROC AUC evaluator. This metric
choice matters. Reconstruction error alone does not establish useful anomaly
ranking. Each implementation maps the official model into an equivalent
PyTorch graph and adds measurement and provenance without changing the task.

\subsection{Audited but Deferred Workloads}

Curation includes rejecting plausible workloads when their authoritative
contract does not fit the v0.1 product boundary. Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B on
HumanEval+ missed its published laptop-quality target by one
task~\citep{hui2024qwen25coder}. NVIDIA EDM on CIFAR-10 completed the official
three-trial, 50,000-image procedure but reported 1.80 FID rather than the
published 1.79~\citep{karras2022edm}. Qwen3-1.7B completed the 1,150-case BFCL
function-calling audit but reached 78.52\% rather than the published 82.92\% AST
summary~\citep{yan2025bfcl,bfcl2026leaderboard}. None was admitted by lowering
its target.

Recommendation and reinforcement learning were also deferred without
substituting a project-defined task. The inherited DLRM contract uses Criteo
Terabyte and does not have an equivalent small public checkpoint and quality
gate~\citep{naumov2019dlrm}. The original and later MiniGo contracts exceed the
laptop envelope~\citep{mattson2019training}.
Optimization methods such as batching, speculative decoding, and tool
scheduling remain configurations or research experiments rather than separate
workload identities.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

The evaluation tests three claims implied by the design. First, an unchanged
upstream task can meet its quality gate through the laptop implementation.
Second, the score-bearing suite fits a practical local execution budget. Third,
the framework distinguishes an executable result from one with enough repeated
evidence to support a timing claim.

The evidence campaign evaluates \ReferenceEvidenceCases{} canonical cases on an
Apple M5 Max host from source commit \texttt{\EvidenceSourceCommit}. CPU and
Apple MPS are used according to the declared case contract.
\FiveRunEvidenceCases{} cases use five fresh processes and pass the declared
semantic and timing-repeatability gates. \ProvisionalEvidenceCases{} cases use
one or two retained measurements. Every retained run passed execution, grading,
provenance, source, and semantic checks, but the provisional cases do not
support a repeatability claim.

The campaign uses a create-once policy. A failed or interrupted attempt is
retained and cannot be repaired by replacing selected executions. Power source,
Low Power Mode, and sleep or wake changes are checked before and after each
promotion execution. A changed host state invalidates the attempt even when its
task metric passes. This separation is important because accuracy does not
excuse unstable timing or uncontrolled measurement conditions.

narrowly above the unchanged 5\% promotion limit.

Evidence class describes execution integrity and repeatability, not adapter
equivalence. The keyword-spotting record is valid five-run historical evidence,
but its committed three-resolver audit fails exact prediction parity. The
quality-preserving but nonidentical adapter remains educationally useful while
blocking promotion. The 90\% quality gate is not a disagreement tolerance.

These results support the three design claims within one machine. The inherited
tasks remain reachable, the score-bearing reference path fits roughly one hour,
and the evidence classes prevent six incomplete protocols from being promoted
with the six verified cases. They do not establish performance portability.
Task metrics are intended to transfer within declared numerical tolerances;
runtime is not. The table therefore identifies each device, while retained
reports record the complete hardware and software fingerprint. A second machine
should be expected to reproduce the gate and protocol, not the Apple M5 Max
throughput.

\subsection{Inference Latency}

Inference evidence is eligible only after correctness and model identity are
established. Causal full, prefill, and decode inference load one retained
quality-passing training checkpoint and record its SHA-256 digest. The evidence
importer verifies the checkpoint package, source report, source manifest, and
package digest before accepting any phase. This closes a common loophole in
which inference silently uses random, stale, or separately trained weights.

Full inference measures complete autoregressive requests. Prefill measures
fresh KV-cache construction for a fixed 256-token prompt. Decode measures
cached-token generation after a fixed prompt. Reports retain median, p90, and
p99 latency together with phase-appropriate token throughput and sample counts.
The three retained CPU measurements report 695.5 output tokens/s for full
inference, 24.6 thousand prefill tokens/s, and 767.3 output tokens/s for decode.

Each phase passes its functional and lineage gate, but each currently has one
retained execution. The rates are therefore provisional system observations,
not repeatability evidence or portable performance targets. Their value lies in
their checkpoint, protocol, sample, and hardware context.

\section{Educational Integration}
\label{sec:education}

The benchmark contract is itself the learning object. Students do not merely
optimize a model. They learn to decide whether two results are comparable and to
defend that decision with artifacts. The intended progression has four stages.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Reproduce.} Run a fixed profile and verify its report and provenance
manifest.
\item \textbf{Diagnose.} Attribute time to data loading, model execution, or
request scheduling.
\item \textbf{Optimize.} Change one systems variable while preserving the declared
quality or parity gate.
\item \textbf{Defend.} Explain the comparison and identify the evidence supporting
each claim.
\end{enumerate}

The profiles place this progression within a course. Instructors can use
\texttt{min} as a pre-lab environment check, \texttt{max} as a fixed reference
for a bounded assignment, and \texttt{pro} as the disclosure envelope for an
open-ended project. The workload identity and semantic gate remain fixed across
that progression. Students therefore learn that optimization changes the system
under test, not the definition of success.

Three executable labs turn that progression into concrete work. The first
examines training-loop optimization under a fixed task. The second introduces
an inference system-under-test interface and compares a baseline path with a
KV-cache path while checking output parity. The third compares dense and sparse
architectures, including active parameter fraction and throughput. These labs
are standalone classroom experiments rather than canonical workload results. A
separate tutorial executes a first registered benchmark, locates its reports,
and verifies its provenance manifest.

\subsection{Example 1: Training-Loop Optimization}

Students begin with a deliberately inefficient but correct configuration. They
profile the run, form a hypothesis, and change one variable such as batching,
data loading, precision, or caching. The submission is not the fastest observed
number alone. It includes the original and modified configurations, task-quality
outcomes, timing distributions, and a statement of comparability. The supplied
lab uses ResNet-18 and Fashion-MNIST as a bounded exercise, not as the registered
MLPerf Tiny image-classification contract.

\subsection{Example 2: KV-Cache Inference}

Students implement a small inference system-under-test interface and compare
naive autoregressive decoding with KV-cache decoding. The lab passes only when
both paths emit identical tokens for every measured query. This connects a
systems optimization to an explicit functional gate before any latency or
throughput comparison is accepted.

\subsection{Example 3: Cross-Architecture Comparison}

A dense model and a sparse mixture-of-experts model can have similar headline
parameter counts while activating different fractions of their parameters per
token. Students report both total and active parameters, throughput, and task
quality. The exercise makes clear why parameter count by itself is not a systems
cost model.

Each lab has a deterministic smoke mode that executes real forward, backward,
inference, or parity checks without requiring a network connection. The smoke
paths establish software regression coverage, not classroom efficacy. This draft
does not claim measured learning outcomes. Course pilots, accessibility review,
and instructor studies remain future work.

\section{Architecture and Implementation}
\label{sec:architecture}

The implementation follows the original white-box design. A declarative registry
selects assets, runners, scenarios, profiles, gates, evidence roles, and
promotion rules. A unified command-line interface drives fetch, audit, run,
grade, verify, report, package, and validation operations. The same registry
generates the flat \texttt{workloads.yaml} compatibility mirror, website tables,
and paper snapshot. This single-source design makes disagreement between the
paper and executable contract a build failure rather than a documentation bug.

\subsection{Unified Dataset Factory}

Dataset recipes distinguish bundled, fetched, cached, synthetic, and restricted
assets. Reports record the resolved mode and relevant digests. A \texttt{min}
runner may use a reduced or synthetic input, but the public audit rejects that
mode for score-bearing evidence. Dataset dossiers also record citation, license,
release status, and expected cache behavior.

\subsection{Model Registry}

Model records bind a user-facing identifier to its implementation or pinned
upstream revision. Checkpoint-backed inference adds a lineage record containing
the source training workload, source task quality, and checkpoint digest. This
prevents a serving result from silently changing weights between repetitions.

\subsection{Load Generator and Inference Harness}

The Python-native load generator implements inspectable scheduling for
single-stream, offline, server, and multi-stream experiments~\citep{reddi2020mlperf}.
It records warmup and measurement counts, latency samples and percentiles,
throughput, and functional outcomes. It is designed for teaching and local
experimentation. It is not a drop-in replacement for official MLPerf LoadGen.

\subsection{Power Measurement}

Promotion campaigns record power source, Low Power Mode, and sleep or wake
state at each execution boundary on supported hosts. A campaign refuses to
start without external power and invalidates an affected attempt when the host
state changes. These guards improve timing control but do not provide calibrated
energy measurement. Absolute joule claims still require supported platform
counters or an external meter with a disclosed calibration.

\subsection{Submission Artifacts}

Each run produces a JSON report and human-readable HTML and CSV views. A
\texttt{.provd.json} manifest binds the report and declared sidecars by size and
SHA-256 digest. Hardware and software fingerprints, asset dossiers, quality
metadata, and checkpoint lineage travel with the result. Packages rewrite
machine-specific absolute paths so that verification works after extraction in a
clean directory.

Hashing establishes integrity, not authenticity. A self-consistent manifest can
still be fabricated by its creator. Independent reruns, signed attestations, or
controlled execution are needed for stronger trust. The framework deliberately
states this boundary instead of claiming that checksums prevent fraud.
The manifest therefore provides \mbox{unauthenticated integrity}, not proof of
origin or execution.

\subsection{CLI as Evaluation Tuple Instantiation}

The CLI makes the evaluation tuple concrete. Suite, workload, variant, and
profile select the model, data, scenario, constraints, and protocol. The commands
below cover the normal review path.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={CLI path from setup to verified evidence.},label={lst:cli}]
$ mlperf doctor
$ mlperf list workloads
$ mlperf fetch \
    --workload causal-language-modeling \
    --mode training --profile max
$ mlperf run \
    --workload causal-language-modeling \
    --mode training --profile max
$ mlperf grade submissions/
$ mlperf verify submissions/run.provd.json
$ mlperf package submissions/run.provd.json
$ mlperf audit --policy public
\end{lstlisting}

The same metadata drives individual runs and release presets. This reduces the
chance that a paper-only configuration diverges from the executable path.

\section{Validation Methodology}
\label{sec:feedback}

Validation is layered because a single green status can hide different kinds of
failure. Unit and integration tests exercise runners, schemas, registry
invariants, grading, provenance, packaging, and generators. Negative tests
reject path traversal, changed source bytes, seed disagreement, incomplete
functional gates, partial batch output, unavailable devices, unresolved dataset
bytes, and timing evidence above its declared variation limit. The
\texttt{smoke} preset runs four representative \texttt{min} workloads.
\texttt{coverage} runs all nine \texttt{min} workloads. \texttt{max} selects the
canonical quality paths, while \texttt{pro} selects the four research workloads
and repeats their \texttt{max} contracts under a separate profile. Each phase
verifies provenance and applies its declared quality or functional gate.

The artifact test suite collected 273 tests, with 272 passes and one expected
skip. The smoke and coverage presets passed 4/4 and 9/9 workloads. A
one-measurement \texttt{pro} validation passed all four research workloads,
their four quality gates, provenance verification, and grading with zero
warnings. The twelve source-locked \texttt{max} cases all have retained
evidence. A clean Python 3.12 wheel installed outside the checkout, loaded nine
workload definitions and twelve evidence records, executed the starter smoke
path, and verified the resulting provenance.

The Quarto site renders 24 pages and passes 48 desktop and narrow-viewport
checks. The paper generator binds this manuscript to the same evidence index and
source lock. The strict public audit remains fail-closed because all nine
workloads are experimental. The validation supports internal consistency and
reviewability, not external reproducibility. Hosted Linux CI, dataset-policy
decisions, and independent reproduction remain separate gates.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================================

\textbf{MLPerf.} The official MLPerf Training and Inference suites established
quality-constrained, scenario-aware performance evaluation at production scale
~\citep{mattson2020,reddi2020mlperf}. Their rules make quality, scenarios,
measurement regions, and disclosure part of result validity rather than
reporting conventions~\citep{mlcommons2026inferencerules}. MLPerf Tiny extends
the methodology to constrained embedded systems~\citep{banbury2021mlperf}.
MLPerf EDU inherits that discipline and selected workload contracts while
targeting locally executable teaching and single-node research. It does not
claim that laptop execution reproduces cluster or microcontroller scale.
\Cref{tab:comparison} summarizes the positioning.

\textbf{DAWNBench.} DAWNBench introduced time-to-accuracy and
cost-to-accuracy~\citep{coleman2019}. Its central methodological contribution was
to connect system performance to a fixed task-quality threshold. MLPerf EDU
retains that quality-first rule but targets a local classroom workflow rather
than cloud cost competition.

\textbf{TorchBench.} TorchBench provides broad PyTorch performance-regression
coverage for framework development~\citep{ansel2024torchbench}. MLPerf EDU shares
its executable PyTorch surface but adds task datasets, inherited quality gates,
classroom profiles, grading, checkpoint lineage, and a deliberately small
governed portfolio. Breadth is therefore not the objective. Each admitted
workload must support a complete semantic and evidentiary contract.

\textbf{Educational ML frameworks.} Framework-building courses make tensors,
operators, and automatic differentiation inspectable. MLPerf EDU is
complementary. Its learning object is the benchmark contract rather than the
framework implementation. PyTorch supplies an immediate standard execution path
without coupling the specification to a custom educational framework. Other
frameworks can later implement the same contracts, but they are not required to
define them.

\textbf{Non-ML benchmarks.} SPEC CPU emphasizes controlled conditions,
disclosure, and reproducible comparison~\citep{henning2006}. TPC shows that
benchmark governance and multi-dimensional reporting are as important as the
workload code~\citep{poess2000}. MLPerf EDU applies those lessons to
classroom-scale ML systems.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

The framework admits only contracts that fit local hardware without changing
their defining task. That choice preserves authority and access, but it does not
capture every production bottleneck. Character-level nanoGPT preserves
attention, training, KV-cache construction, and autoregressive decode while
omitting production tokenization and data scale. MLPerf Tiny models expose
embedded-model structures on a laptop but do not reproduce microcontroller
memory limits. Distributed and datacenter-scale claims are outside v0.1.

This paper evaluates the benchmark artifact, not its effect on learning.
Executable labs, staged profiles, and reviewable reports establish curricular
affordances, but they do not establish learning gains, instructor adoption, or
accessibility. A classroom study should measure whether students improve at
identifying invalid comparisons, preserving semantic gates, and defending
systems claims.

Six cases currently have evidence strong enough for a five-run repeatability
claim. The other six are provisional even though their code executes and their
quality or functional gates pass. This is a deliberate limitation. Promotion
still requires the complete multi-run protocol, stable host conditions, asset
policy, and portable evidence rather than confidence based on one successful
run.

The current references come from one Apple M5 Max system. CPU and Apple MPS
paths establish local feasibility, but most canonical evidence uses one backend
per case. Runtime is platform-specific. Cross-platform work should include Linux
CPU and CUDA systems, clean-wheel installation, repeated timing, and explicit
treatment of numerical drift.

Several datasets remain fetch-only or require a release-policy decision. The
project also needs an authoritative license for the complete published artifact,
public hosting for larger evidence packages, and external decisions about the
name and any relationship to MLCommons. Technical completion does not settle
those governance questions.

Power-state guards improve the internal validity of timing campaigns but do not
provide calibrated energy. Defensible joule claims require supported platform
counters or an external meter. The five deferred workload proposals also remain
outside the suite unless an unchanged authoritative contract clears both the
quality and laptop-feasibility gates. The resulting v0.1 portfolio does not yet
cover recommendation, reinforcement learning, code generation, image generation,
or function calling.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Internal validity.} Five fresh-process runs reduce sensitivity to one
timing sample for the six verified cases. The provisional cases cannot support
that claim. Shared implementation error can remain. Provenance digests detect
byte changes and path drift; they do not prove that the computation occurred.

\textbf{External validity.} One laptop establishes local feasibility, not
performance portability. The evidence records hardware and software fingerprints
so later systems can test both quality transfer and timing variation.

\textbf{Construct validity.} The framework assumes that practicing controlled
comparisons on reduced workloads improves production benchmarking judgment. That
claim is plausible but unmeasured. The selection process may also favor tasks
with compact pretrained checkpoints or established small evaluation sets.
Formal course studies, independent workload review, and external artifact
evaluation are needed to assess both effects.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

MLPerf EDU brings professional benchmark discipline to a scale that students and
individual researchers can inspect. Its central contribution is not a list of
small models. It is an executable contract that binds model, data, hardware,
scenario, quality, protocol, and evidence before a performance claim is accepted.

The present implementation contains \RegistryRows{} workloads across
\RegistrySuites{} suites and \ReferenceEvidenceCases{} canonical evidence cases.
All score-bearing cases pass their quality gates, and all performance-bearing
phases pass their functional and lineage gates. \FiveRunEvidenceCases{} cases
also pass the five-run repeatability rule with timing variation between
\FiveRunMinCV{} and \FiveRunMaxCV{}. The remaining
\ProvisionalEvidenceCases{} are explicitly provisional and make no repeatability
claim. Those results make the benchmark ready for external technical review,
not for a claim of official status. Public evidence hosting, cross-platform
reproduction, dataset policy, licensing, and MLCommons feedback remain visible
next steps.

The intended educational value lies in the workflow students practice. They
reproduce a baseline, diagnose a bottleneck, change one variable, preserve
quality, and defend comparability with artifacts. That workflow can outlast any
particular model or hardware generation. Demonstrating its learning effect is
the next empirical question.

\subsection*{Reproducibility}

The repository contains the executable registry, reference runners, evidence
summaries, website, labs, tutorial, and paper generator. The
public entry point is \texttt{mlperf}. Runs produce JSON, HTML, CSV, and
\texttt{.provd.json} artifacts. The paper imports generated registry counts and
reference values so its quantitative claims remain synchronized with the
committed class-labeled evidence. The larger raw packages are retained for
\mbox{local reviewer handoff} and do not yet have public URLs.
